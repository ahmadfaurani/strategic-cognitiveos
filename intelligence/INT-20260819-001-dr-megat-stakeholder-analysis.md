# INT-20260819-001 — Strategic Stakeholder Analytical Report: Dr. Megat Zuhairy (NACSA CE)

## Assessment Overview

**Subject:** Ir. Dr. Megat Zuhairy bin Megat Tajuddin, Chief Executive, National Cyber Security Agency (NACSA), Malaysia
**Assessment Date:** 19 August 2026
**Analytical Confidence:** HIGH
**Sources:** Official NACSA materials, CyberDSA 2026 statement, statutory NACSA information, public professional biographies (GCF, NACSA announcement), recent documented public engagements (July 2026 NACSA-Huawei, AISCF announcements)

## Key Findings

### 1. Tier-1 Classification
- Formal Authority: 5/5
- National Cybersecurity Influence: 5/5
- Regulatory Relevance: 5/5
- NCII Relevance: 5/5
- Sovereign-Technology Narrative Relevance: 5/5
- AI-Security Relevance: 5/5
- Ability to Influence Institutional Acceptance: 5/5
- NOT a sales stakeholder — engagement objective is strategic alignment, institutional awareness, confidence creation

### 2. Narrative Alignment: VERY HIGH
NACSA's own vocabulary already includes "sovereign cyber security resilience" (MCSS 2025–2030). The opportunity is NOT to persuade NACSA that sovereign cybersecurity matters — it is to demonstrate that an operational Malaysian capability exists which advances the direction NACSA already advocates.

### 3. Six Observable Strategic Themes
1. From cyber protection to cyber resilience (MCSS 2025–2030)
2. Cyber sovereignty (explicit in CyberDSA 2026 statement)
3. AI security (AISCF, autonomous agent controls)
4. Cryptography and digital sovereignty (MyKriptografi, PQC)
5. Public-private collaboration (mission alignment, not vendor selling)
6. National capability orientation (sovereign resilience, local capacity)

### 4. Recommended Engagement: 4-Stage Progressive Model
- Stage 1: Establish credibility (national requirement → capability → evidence)
- Stage 2: Demonstrate alignment (map against MCSS, Act 2024, NCII, AISCF)
- Stage 3: Demonstrate capability (5 key questions)
- Stage 4: Seek strategic alignment, not endorsement (Awareness → Alignment → Familiarisation → Observation → Validation)

### 5. Diligence Notes
- Doctoral qualification discrepancy: NACSA 2023 says PhD Technology and Innovation Management (UiTM); GCF 2026 says DBA (UiTM). Use honorific only.
- NACSA-Huawei cooperation (July 2026) includes digital sovereignty — sovereignty moving from narrative into programmes

### 6. Key Risks
1. Premature use of NACSA association (must not present as endorsement)
2. Excessive productisation (lead with national capability architecture)
3. Sovereignty without evidence (must demonstrate data residency, IP, operational control)
4. AI claims without security governance (must show AI governance, human authority, auditability)

## CyberDSA 2026 Implication

Dr. Megat sits near the apex of the stakeholder architecture but late in the activation sequence (Gate 6). A stakeholder with this level of national and regulatory authority should encounter a proposition that has already achieved internal alignment, product readiness, technical validation, partner alignment, coherent narrative, evidence of Malaysian capability and clearly defined institutional relevance.

The objective: when the proposition reaches NACSA, it appears not as an idea seeking sponsorship but as a credible national capability worthy of strategic attention.

## Analytical Confidence Statement

**HIGH** — Assessment supported by official NACSA materials, Dr. Megat's official CyberDSA 2026 statement, statutory NACSA information, public professional biographies and recent documented public engagements. Where public sources conflict (doctoral qualification), the discrepancy has been explicitly preserved rather than resolved by assumption.
