# CyberDSA 2026 — Engineered Success Framework Applied Use Case

**Doctrine References:** §9 (Engineered Success Framework), §10 (Objective Decomposition)
**Workstream:** CyberDSA 2026 Product Launch & Market Activation
**Mission:** 47 success criteria across 6 dimensions, 5 checkpoints, 7 weeks to event

---

## PART 1: THE PROBLEM §9 SOLVES

### Without Engineered Success (Anti-Pattern §44)

A typical CyberDSA preparation might look like:

> *"We need to get the products ready, sort out the CSM partnership, make sure marketing materials are done, prepare the booth, and follow up on leads after. DAF is overseeing everything, Fuad is handling tech, Hadri is doing architecture, Shuhada is on commercial, and Azza is doing marketing."*

This violates 8 anti-patterns:
- ❌ Treats request as independent (no linkage between dimensions)
- ❌ Summarises without extracting implications (no failure conditions defined)
- ❌ Confuses meetings with outcomes ("get products ready" is not an outcome)
- ❌ Confuses documents with progress (no definition of what "ready" means)
- ❌ Allows ambiguous ownership ("DAF is overseeing everything" = no real ownership)
- ❌ Accepts "ongoing" as meaningful status (no checkpoints or evidence)
- ❌ Hides important risks inside long reports (no failure conditions)
- ❌ Optimises formatting while ignoring execution (looks organised, isn't actionable)

### With Engineered Success (Doctrine §9)

The same mission becomes a **12-element engineered success architecture** with decomposed objectives, defined failure modes, critical path mapping, and verifiable evidence at each checkpoint.

The framework answers a fundamentally different question: not *"What do we need to do?"* but ***"What conditions must become true for the mission to succeed, and how do we engineer those conditions?"***

## PART 2: THE 12-ELEMENT FRAMEWORK APPLIED

### Element 1: OBJECTIVE

**What outcome must be achieved?**

> **Strategic Objective:** Successfully launch Aras Integrasi's cybersecurity product portfolio at CyberDSA 2026 (Oct 5–7, MITEC Kuala Lumpur) — demonstrating national-grade sovereign technology capability, activating commercial pipeline with CSM partnership, and converting event engagement into qualified POC commitments within 30 days post-event.

**Objective Decomposition (§10):**

```
Strategic Objective
  └─ CyberDSA 2026 Product Launch & Market Activation
      ├─ Operational Outcome 1: Product Readiness (3 products demo-ready)
      ├─ Operational Outcome 2: Commercial Pipeline (pre/during/post engagement)
      ├─ Operational Outcome 3: CSM Partnership (visible joint presence)
      ├─ Operational Outcome 4: Marketing & Positioning (clear product story)
      ├─ Operational Outcome 5: Execution (3-day event run effectively)
      └─ Operational Outcome 6: Post-Event Conversion (real commercial momentum)
          ├─ Work Package: Demo scope definition → Milestone: CP1 → Action: Hadri+Fuad meeting → Owner: Hadri → Evidence: Signed scope doc → Done: scope locked
          ├─ Work Package: Segmentation framework → Milestone: CP1 → Action: Ember delivers framework → Owner: Ember → Evidence: Framework doc → Done: 193 orgs tiered
          ├─ Work Package: Positioning sign-off → Milestone: CP1 → Action: DAF signs off → Owner: DAF → Evidence: Approved statement → Done: positioning locked
          └─ [continues for all 47 criteria...]
```

**Doctrine §10 rule:** *"Do not allow work packages to exist without linkage to an objective."*

This means: if someone is working on something that doesn't trace back to one of the 6 operational outcomes → either it belongs on a different initiative, or the objective structure is incomplete.

### The Core Insight

**Reactive management discovers problems when they happen. Engineered success discovers problems before they happen — and engineers the conditions to prevent them.**

The framework's value is not in the document. It's in the **structural thinking** it forces:

1. **You cannot define "done" without specifying evidence** → prevents false completion claims
2. **You cannot identify failure conditions without mapping dependencies** → prevents cascade failures
3. **You cannot find the critical path without decomposing objectives** → prevents schedule-blind execution
4. **You cannot track leading indicators without defining success conditions** → prevents late detection
5. **You cannot assign ownership without defining work packages** → prevents ambiguous accountability
