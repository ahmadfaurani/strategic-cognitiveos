## Executive Summary

LVMH commissioned private intelligence firms to investigate journalists from **La Lettre** (sister publication of Intelligence Online) and their luxury sector correspondent **Sophie Lecluse**. The operation was discovered when assignment orders circulated among Paris private investigators in May 2025. Multiple firms refused the mandate as "too imprudent."

**LVMH formally denied** commissioning the investigation, despite having signed a **judicial agreement in 2021** (€10m settlement) that prohibited hiring private investigators against journalists following the **Fakir newspaper scandal**.

## Key Findings

### The Investigation Mandate

**Circulated:** May 2025 to London and Paris private investigators  
**Client Description:** "Exposed in the luxury goods sector"  
**Stated Concern:** Articles in La Lettre threatening client interests  

**Specific Targets:**
1. **La Lettre editorial team** — Identify "person primarily responsible for recent interest" in LVMH/luxury sector
2. **Sophie Lecluse** (Luxury Sector Journalist) — "For what purpose did Sophie Lecluse take an interest in this affair?" (source identification risk)
3. **Competitor intelligence** — Queries about upcoming articles on LVMH or **Kering** (Pinault family)
4. **Industry influence theories** — Suggested La Lettre might be "under influence" of **Hermès** or **Goyard**

**Assignment Order Characteristics:**
- Deliberately vague wording
- Strange phrasing suggesting poor translation from English
- Opened door to personal investigation of journalist

## Historical Context: 2021 Fakir Scandal (Squarcini-LVMH Case)

### Timeline

| Period | Event |
|--------|-------|
| 2013-2016 | Squarcini network surveils Fakir newspaper, infiltrates editorial team |
| 2019-2021 | Judicial investigation, CJIP (judicial public interest agreement) negotiated |
| 2021 | LVMH pays **€10m** to French Treasury (3x theoretical maximum fine) |
| 2021 | LVMH undertakes: No longer hire private investigators against journalists |
| 28/11/2024 | Trial opens at Paris Criminal Court (10 defendants including Squarcini) |
| 14/03/2025 | Sentences handed down: prison terms + professional activity bans |

### Key Figures in Fakir Case

| Name | Role | Outcome |
|------|------|---------|
| **Bernard Squarcini** | Former DCRI (French domestic intelligence) head | Criminal conviction, professional ban |
| **Pierre Godé** | Arnault's right-hand man at LVMH, Squarcini's contact | Died before charges filed |
| **François Ruffin** | Fakir founder, journalist target | Victim of surveillance |
| **Bernard Arnault** | LVMH Chairman | Escaped prosecution via CJIP |

### Judicial Framework Established (March 2025 Ruling)

- Criminal court judges established strict legal framework for corporate intelligence
- Prison sentences + sweeping professional activity bans
- Reinforced that corporate espionage against press is criminal offense

**2025 Violation:** This La Lettre surveillance operation directly contravenes LVMH's 2021 CJIP undertaking, creating fresh legal exposure.
