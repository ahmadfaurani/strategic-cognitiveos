## PART 1: METHODOLOGY — WHY THREE METHODS?

### The Triangulation Principle

No single analytical method can capture all dimensions of a complex operational workstream. Each doctrine section asks a fundamentally different question:

| Method | Core Question | What It Reveals | What It Cannot See |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Cognitive Loop (INT-006)** | "What is happening and what does it mean?" | Dynamic patterns, correlations between signals, bottleneck emergence, learning extraction | Static structural constraints (failure modes, critical path, resource gaps) |
| **Actionable Intelligence (INT-007)** | "What should we do about it?" | Signal-to-action pipeline, prioritisation conflicts, ownership clarity, verification standards | Long-range dependencies, structural failure conditions, resource adequacy |
| **Engineered Success (INT-008)** | "What conditions must become true?" | Dependency chains, critical path, failure modes, leading indicators, predictive model | Dynamic pattern shifts, real-time signal changes, learning extraction |

### Why Convergence Matters

When three independent methods — each with different blind spots — arrive at the same conclusion, that conclusion has **structural validity**. It is not an artifact of one method's bias. It is a property of the system being analysed.

This report identifies where all three converge (high confidence), where two converge (medium confidence), and where only one speaks (low confidence — flag for monitoring).
