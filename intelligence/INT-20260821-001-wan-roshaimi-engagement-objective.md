# Wan Roshaimi — Multi-Layer Stakeholder Engagement Objective

**Source:** DAF directive, Telegram 2026-08-21 06:33 UTC
**Assessment type:** Strategic engagement architecture
**Stakeholder:** STK-20260812-001 — Ts. Wan Roshaimi Bin Wan Abdullah, CTO, CyberSecurity Malaysia

---

## One-Sentence Objective

Position Wan Roshaimi as the technical anchor of a durable CSM × Aras partnership — starting with co-branding validation for CyberDSA 2026, extending into integration architecture for the CSCDC transition, and maturing into a long-term technical partnership for sovereign cybersecurity capability.

---

## 5-Layer Engagement Architecture

### Layer 1 — Immediate: CyberDSA 2026 Gate 4 (Bounded, Sep 10)

**Objective:** Technical validation of the CSM × Aras co-branded stack.

Formal, bounded ask inside the activation framework. Thinnest layer — if we only engage him for Gate 4, we waste the relationship.

**Output:** Technical alignment note — architecture posture validated, integration boundaries agreed, CyberDSA technical representation confirmed.

**Dependencies:** Gate 0 (Roshdi) + Gates 1-2 (Azrul, Zulfeka) must close first.

### Layer 2 — CyberDSA Endgame: Co-Branding Credibility (Oct 5)

**Objective:** His technical endorsement materially strengthens market positioning of all three products.

Wan Roshaimi is the person whose validation makes "CSM × Aras co-branded" defensible. Roshdi authorizes institutionally — Wan Roshaimi makes it technically credible. Without his validation, co-branding is an executive decision without technical backing. With it, the products carry both institutional authority AND technical authority.

**Why it matters:** When government, CNII operators, or GLCs see "CSM × Aras," they assess whether CSM's technical leadership actually stood behind the technology. Wan Roshaimi's endorsement is the answer.

### Layer 3 — Post-CyberDSA: Technical Partnership Architecture (Oct–Dec)

**Objective:** Position Aras as CSM's natural technical integration partner across the full capability stack.

Wan Roshaimi's CTO scope (responsive, proactive, pre-emptive) maps directly to Aras product architecture:

| CSM CTO Scope | Aras Capability | Engagement Territory |
|---|---|---|
| Responsive (incident, recovery) | GovSec TIP (threat intelligence, AI-assisted analysis) | Operational cyber defence integration |
| Proactive (assurance, readiness) | VoronCitadel (governance, risk, compliance) | Security posture, regulatory alignment |
| Pre-Emptive (emerging threats) | chain:SENTRY (blockchain, emerging tech) + PQC readiness | Anticipatory defence, future-readiness |

If he sees Aras as the partner that integrates across all three dimensions, the relationship shifts from "co-branding for an event" to "technical partnership architecture for national capability."

**Active tracks requiring CTO-level technical validation:**
1. GovSec × SiberSUITE telemetry integration (INIT-20260804-003) — Hadri/Fuad working with Hafiz/Zaharudin
2. Cybersecurity Score Card (INIT-20260810-001) — CNII sector scoring, exploration phase
3. CBOM Agent (INIT-20260810-002) — AI-enabled component analysis, feasibility assessment
4. AI Systems Co-Design Lab (INIT-20260804-004) — he was cc'd on acceptance email

All four are CSM joint tracks needing CTO-level validation to move from exploration to execution.

### Layer 4 — CSCDC Transition: Infrastructure Positioning (2026–2027)

**Objective:** Position Aras capabilities within the CSM → CSCDC transition infrastructure roadmap.

Wan Roshaimi is CTO during the most significant institutional transition in Malaysian cybersecurity history — CSM merging into CSCDC under JPM-MKN-NACSA. CSCDC infrastructure procurement (RM 485K — social listening, content studio, encrypted portal) is near-term. But the bigger picture: **whatever technical architecture CSM builds or validates now will likely carry into CSCDC.**

If Wan Roshaimi validates the Aras integration architecture (GovSec as analytics layer consuming SiberSUITE telemetry, CBOM for component analysis, Score Card for posture assessment), that architecture becomes a reference point for CSCDC's future technology stack. Products become infrastructure, not just products.

**This is the layer where sovereign technology positioning becomes real.** "Designed for national-grade deployment" becomes "deployed within the national cybersecurity authority's technology architecture."

### Layer 5 — Long-Term: National Technical Leadership Relationship (2026+)

**Objective:** Establish Wan Roshaimi as the technical counterpart in a durable CSM × Aras operating relationship.

CyberDSA is the catalyst, not the end state. Wan Roshaimi makes that real on the technical side. His career pattern (technical security → consulting → CIO → financial governance → international → national leadership) means he thinks in terms of **sustainable national capability**, not product purchases.

His public thought-leadership themes (PQC migration, emerging threats, anticipatory defence) align with Aras's emerging technology roadmap (chain:SENTRY, PQC Sandbox, CBOM Agent). Over time, the relationship should produce:

1. **Joint technical architecture decisions** — co-designing, not just Aras proposing and CSM approving
2. **Technical validation framework** — repeatable process for evaluating Aras capabilities within CSM/CSCDC infrastructure
3. **Emerging technology partnership** — PQC, AI-enabled defence, blockchain security as joint research/evaluation tracks
4. **Skills transfer and operationalisation** — his decision lens includes "Can Malaysia operate it sustainably?" — answer: "Yes, because Aras is building Malaysian engineering capability alongside CSM's institutional expertise"

## Engagement Sequencing

1. **First meeting (Gate 4):** Technical narrative + co-branding validation. Lead with national outcome, prove with architecture, de-risk with governance, close with defined next step. (Per stakeholder brief 5-stage strategy.)
2. **Second meeting (post-CyberDSA):** Integration architecture discussion — GovSec × SiberSUITE, Score Card, CBOM. How these fit CSM's technology roadmap and CSCDC transition.
3. **Ongoing:** Technical partnership cadence — quarterly architecture review, emerging technology evaluation, joint PQC/emerging threat work.

---

## Risk Assessment

### Under-Engagement Risk
If treated as only a Gate 4 checkbox:
1. Co-branding lacks technical depth — Roshdi authorizes institutionally, but technical validation is shallow. Market will notice.
2. CSCDC transition passes by — architecture decisions made during CSM→CSCDC will determine national cybersecurity technology stack for years. If Aras isn't in those conversations, someone else will be.
3. Emerging technology alignment never happens — PQC, AI-enabled defence, blockchain security are his territory. Connection between his thought-leadership and Aras's product roadmap never forms.

### Over-Engagement Risk
Per DEC-20260819-004:
1. **Misallocation as delivery resource** — he's NOT a POC engineer, product demonstrator, or project manager. He validates, advises on architecture and governance — he doesn't execute.
2. **Premature scope expansion** — bringing full CSCDC transition conversation before co-branding validation is done dilutes the Gate 4 ask and potentially triggers institutional resistance.

## Engagement Guardrails (from stakeholder brief)

**DO:**
- Anchor on mission, resilience and measurable outcomes
- Bring technical evidence and a realistic operating model
- Show how the capability integrates with existing CSM ecosystem assets
- Define governance, ownership and post-POC path early

**AVOID:**
- Leading with product features or generic AI claims
- Assuming technical endorsement equals procurement authority
- Presenting a POC without success criteria or operationalisation plan
- Overstating institutional alignment before formal validation
