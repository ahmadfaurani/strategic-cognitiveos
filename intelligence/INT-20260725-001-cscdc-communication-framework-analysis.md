## 2. Strategic Context

CSCDC was established through the consolidation of PTPKM (Pusat Pengurusan dan Teknologi Kriptologi Malaysia) and CyberSecurity Malaysia (CSM) operational expertise. This positions CSCDC as Malaysia's premier cryptographic and cyber defence authority under the Prime Minister's Department.

The framework addresses three emerging threat vectors explicitly:
- AI-based cyber weapons
- Deepfakes and digital information manipulation
- Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) readiness via PQC Sandbox

**Assessment [HIGH]:** The document correctly identifies that a cyber defence organisation's effectiveness depends on communication, not just technical capability. The framing of communication as a "strategic instrument" rather than traditional PR is a mature positioning.

## 3. The Four Strategic Pillars (Paksi Strategik)

| Pillar | Name | Objective | Approach |
|--------|------|-----------|----------|
| 1 | Sovereign Authority | CSCDC as sole authoritative source for cyber policy, encryption standards, and national cryptography | Control official narrative domestically and internationally; prevent contradictory statements between JPM agencies |
| 2 | Proactive Transparency | Fill information vacuums during cyber incidents before malicious actors exploit them | Rapid structured reporting on threat status, citizen impact, and technical mitigation in real-time |
| 3 | Ecosystem Synergy | Bridge the gap between government regulators and private sector CNII operators | B2B communication channels for compliance standards, local cyber IP promotion, threat intelligence sharing |
| 4 | Community Resilience | Build human-layer cyber defence at the community level | Translate technical findings into plain language, mobilise Cyber Community Champions, collaborate with educational institutions and NGOs |

**Assessment [HIGH]:** The four-pillar architecture is well-structured and covers the full spectrum from authority (top-down) to community (bottom-up). The inclusion of Community Resilience as a co-equal pillar — not an afterthought — is strategically sound.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** Pillar 3 (Ecosystem Synergy) could benefit from more specific mechanisms for CNII engagement. The current description is aspirational; operational B2B communication protocols need further development.

## 4. Organisational Structure

### 4.1 Leadership

- **Chief Communications Officer (CCO)** — reports directly to CEO/Top Management of CSCDC
- Functional coordination with MKN and KSN office
- Two divisions: Corporate Operations and Strategic & Industry Impact

### 4.2 Seven Operational Units

**Division A: Corporate Operations (Internal & Reputation)**

| Unit | Name | Primary Function |
|------|------|-----------------|
| 1 | Media Relations & PR | External reputation management, media statements, press conferences, media monitoring, media registry |
| 2 | Internal Communications & Cultural Integration | Post-merger cultural unification (PTPKM + CSM), intranet, monthly bulletin, townhalls, internal infosec awareness |
| 3 | Digital Media & Brand Management | Portal management, social media (LinkedIn, X, Facebook), graphic/multimedia production, brand identity enforcement |

**Division B: Strategic & Industry Impact (Macro & Crisis)**

| Unit | Name | Primary Function |
|------|------|-----------------|
| 4 | Crisis & Cyber Incident Communications | Fast-response command, Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook, holding statements, War Room leadership, counter-disinformation |
| 5 | Government & International Relations (G2G) | Cabinet briefing papers, PM/senior minister speeches, MoU/MoA publicity, international diplomacy reports (ASEAN, UN, ITU), parliamentary briefings |
| 6 | Industry & Talent Communications (B2B) | MCSS 2025–2030 promotion, cryptographic compliance standards, PQC Sandbox industry engagement, cyber talent pipeline |
| 7 | Advocacy, Literacy & Community Resilience | Technical translation for public, anti-deepfake/anti-scam campaigns, Cyber Community Champions programme, curriculum integration with KPM/KPT, demographic literacy surveys |

**Assessment [HIGH]:** The division of Corporate Operations vs Strategic Impact is a clean separation. Unit 4 (Crisis) and Unit 5 (G2G) are particularly well-scoped. The post-merger cultural integration function (Unit 2) shows awareness that the PTPKM-CSM consolidation creates real integration challenges.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** The structure assumes 18 personnel (1 CCO + 2 Deputy Directors + 7 Unit Heads + 8 Executives) for a full-scale operation. This may be lean for a national-level 24/7 crisis communication capability. Cross-training and surge capacity planning are not addressed.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** The document does not address potential channel failures (e.g., social media platform outages during a crisis) or redundant channel strategies. A fallback protocol for primary channel failure would strengthen resilience.

## 6. Crisis Communication SOP

### 6.1 Workflow

1. **Immediate Holding Statement** (Target: <30 minutes) — Unit 4 issues initial official statement via social media and agency portal confirming the disruption, threat isolation actions, and commitment to periodic updates
2. **Single Spokesperson Activation** — Only CCO, CEO, or designated Minister may issue press statements. Technical staff strictly prohibited from off-the-record commentary
3. **Community Mass Broadcast** (Pillar 4) — If citizen devices/data are affected, Unit 7 activates Community Champions network to disseminate immediate security steps via WhatsApp/Telegram infographics in plain language

### 6.2 Key Design Principles

- **4H Method** for emergency press conferences (presumably: Here's What Happened, What's Being Done, What You Should Do, How to Get Help — though not explicitly defined in the document)
- Holding statement bank pre-prepared for multiple cyber scenarios (Ransomware, Data Breach, Critical Infrastructure Attack)
- War Room Communication activation on national-level critical incident signal

**Assessment [HIGH]:** The 30-minute holding statement target is appropriately aggressive and aligns with international best practice. The pre-prepared statement bank is a mature approach.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** The crisis SOP describes the communication flow but does not detail the technical-to-communication handoff protocol — i.e., how technical incident information is translated into cleared public statements. This interface is where most crisis communication failures occur. A technical-liaison role or cleared technical advisor to the War Room should be explicitly defined.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** The 4H Method is referenced but not defined. An appendix or glossary definition would improve operational clarity.

## 7. KPI & Performance Measurement Matrix

| Division | Metric | Measurement Tool | KPI Target |
|---------|--------|-----------------|------------|
| Corporate Operations (Units 1,2,3) | Share of Voice (SoV) & Sentiment Analysis | Meltwater/Brand24/Google Analytics | >85% Positive-to-Neutral media coverage; 25% quarterly social media engagement growth |
| Strategic Impact (Units 4,5,6) | Crisis Turnaround Time & Policy Alignment | Operations minute logs / Government document audit | Holding statement <30 min; 100% Cabinet briefing papers approved without major correction |
| Community Resilience (Unit 7) | Public Cyber Literacy Index & Community Reach | National surveys / Digital reach matrix | 30% improvement in public cyber literacy scores; minimum 1,000 Community Champions trained in Year 1 |

**Assessment [HIGH]:** The KPI framework is outcome-based and auditable. The 30-minute crisis response KPI is measurable and ambitious. The 1,000 Community Champions target is concrete and verifiable.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** The KPI framework lacks intermediate milestones (e.g., quarterly literacy improvement targets). The 30% literacy improvement baseline is not defined — improvement relative to what starting point? A baseline measurement in Q1 should be formally required.

**Assessment [LOW]:** The "100% Cabinet papers approved without major correction" KPI is process-oriented rather than outcome-oriented. It measures bureaucratic compliance rather than strategic impact of government communications.

The CCO at RM 18,000/month (Jusa C) may need supplementary allowances to compete with private-sector PR/corporate communications salaries. A talent retention strategy should be explicitly addressed.

**Assessment [MEDIUM]:** Sector 2's RM 120,000 for enterprise social listening may underfund the requirement. Meltwater enterprise licenses for a national government agency typically cost more. This line item may need supplementation through inter-agency sharing (e.g., with MCMC or JAPEN).

**Assessment [LOW]:** The contingency fund at RM 350,000 (8.7%) may be insufficient for a sustained national-level cyber crisis requiring weeks of War Room operations, sustained SMS broadcasting, and multiple emergency press conferences. Historical crisis communication costs suggest this should be closer to 12–15% of total budget.

## 11. Key Observations & Strategic Implications

### 11.1 Strengths

1. **Communication as strategic instrument, not PR support function** — The document correctly positions communication as a core capability, not a service function. This is the right framing for a national security entity.

2. **Post-merger cultural integration built in** — Unit 2's explicit mandate to unify PTPKM and CSM cultures shows awareness that technical mergers fail without cultural integration.

3. **Community resilience as a co-equal pillar** — Most national cyber frameworks treat public literacy as an afterthought. This framework gives it equal weight to sovereign authority and crisis response.

4. **Single Point of Truth doctrine** — The restriction of official statements to CCO/CEO/Minister only is essential for maintaining credibility during crisis. The prohibition on technical staff giving off-the-record commentary is particularly important.

5. **Pre-prepared holding statement bank** — Having statements ready for ransomware, data breach, and critical infrastructure scenarios before incidents occur is international best practice.

6. **Modest, defensible budget** — RM 4M for a national cyber communication division is lean. The cost efficiency strategies (asset sharing, free airtime, in-house production) are realistic.

7. **Platform-to-audience mapping** — The social media strategy correctly maps platforms to audiences (LinkedIn for CNII, TikTok for youth, WhatsApp for community networks).

### 11.2 Gaps & Recommendations

1. **Technical-to-communication interface undefined** — The crisis SOP describes the communication workflow but not how technical incident information is translated into cleared public statements. **Recommendation:** Define a Technical Liaison Officer role within the War Room who is cleared to receive technical briefings and translate them into communication-ready language.

2. **Channel redundancy not addressed** — No fallback protocol for primary channel failure (e.g., social media platform outage during crisis). **Recommendation:** Add a channel redundancy matrix identifying primary, secondary, and tertiary channels for each crisis communication scenario.

3. **Surge capacity for 24/7 operations** — 18 personnel may be insufficient for sustained 24/7 crisis operations. **Recommendation:** Define a surge staffing model that can be activated during extended national-level incidents, including cross-agency secondments.

4. **Talent acquisition challenge** — Public-sector salary scales may not attract top-tier crisis communication and digital media talent. **Recommendation:** Consider contract-based appointments for specialised roles (crisis communication specialist, digital content lead) with market-rate compensation.

5. **Baseline measurement for KPIs** — The 30% literacy improvement target lacks a defined baseline. **Recommendation:** Require a Q1 baseline measurement programme before the 30% improvement KPI is formally adopted.

6. **4H Method undefined** — Referenced but not explained. **Recommendation:** Add a glossary or appendix defining the 4H Method for emergency press conferences.

7. **Contingency fund adequacy** — 8.7% may be low for sustained crisis operations. **Recommendation:** Review against historical national-level crisis communication costs and consider increasing to 12–15%.

8. **PQC Sandbox communication strategy** — RM 300,000 is allocated for PQC Sandbox launch but the communication strategy for this critical national initiative is not detailed. **Recommendation:** Develop a dedicated PQC Sandbox communication plan as a sub-document.

9. **International communication coordination** — Unit 5 handles G2G and international relations, but the document does not address coordination with foreign embassies and international media during cross-border cyber incidents. **Recommendation:** Add an international crisis communication protocol.

10. **90-day plan detail missing** — The mobilisation plan is referenced as an infographic but lacks tabular milestones. **Recommendation:** Add a detailed 90-day Gantt chart with weekly milestones, owners, and deliverables.

## 14. Bottom Line

This is a well-crafted strategic communication framework that correctly positions communication as a core national security function rather than a PR support activity. The four-pillar architecture, seven-unit organisational design, and RM 4M OBB-aligned budget provide a solid foundation for CSCDC's communication capability.

The document is ready for implementation approval, subject to minor revisions addressing: (1) the technical-to-communication interface in crisis SOPs, (2) KPI baseline measurement, (3) channel redundancy planning, and (4) 90-day plan detail.

For DAF's purposes, the CSCDC framework presents a concrete engagement opportunity at the intersection of cybersecurity, government communication, and institutional capability building — directly aligned with at least three of his six workstream clusters.
