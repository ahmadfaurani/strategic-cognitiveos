# Summary

CSCDC's Unit 4 is tasked with building a cyber crisis communication War Room from scratch. The framework allocates RM 150K for playbook development but does not define the technical-to-communication handoff protocol — the most common failure point in crisis communication. DAF has operated political war rooms (N17 Semerah, N16 Sungai Balang, N41 Puteri Wangsa) with transferable methodology for rapid intelligence collection, holding statements, sentiment monitoring, and community mobilisation.

# Strategic Significance

The War Room is the highest-value, highest-urgency function in the framework. It activates during national-level cyber crises. If Aras Integrasi can position as the methodology architect for the War Room — translating political war room experience to cyber crisis context — this becomes a strategic capability partnership, not a vendor transaction.

# Intelligence Type

Market — methodology transfer and infrastructure opportunity

# Evidence

- Unit 4 function 3: "Mengaktifkan dan memimpin War Room Komunikasi" (Page 9)
- Holding statement <30 min KPI (Page 16) — requires pre-built infrastructure and rehearsed protocols
- RM 150K for Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook (Page 17, Sector 3)
- Gap #1 in analysis: technical-to-communication interface undefined
- DAF's political war room experience: N17 Semerah (26 PD analysis), N16 Sungai Balang (19 PD), N41 Puteri Wangsa (5-cornered fight)

# Assessment

**[MEDIUM]** The methodology transfer is a strong differentiator. Few people in Malaysia have operated both political and cyber crisis war rooms. However, CSCDC may believe cyber crisis communication is fundamentally different from political communication (it is different in content, similar in architecture). Positioning must acknowledge the difference while emphasising the transferable architecture.

# Confidence Level

**MEDIUM** — The gap is documented and the methodology transfer is logical. Whether CSCDC leadership sees the parallel is uncertain.

# Implications

- War Room methodology is a premium consulting service, not a commodity
- The playbook development (RM 150K) could be an entry point to a broader engagement
- War Room infrastructure (physical + digital) is a separate procurement from methodology
- This positions Aras Integrasi at the strategic level, not the tactical vendor level

# Recommended Actions

- Prepare a War Room Methodology Transfer one-pager (political → cyber architecture mapping)
- Include War Room design in the Aras Integrasi capability positioning brief
- Identify whether RM 150K playbook budget is for internal development or external consultancy
