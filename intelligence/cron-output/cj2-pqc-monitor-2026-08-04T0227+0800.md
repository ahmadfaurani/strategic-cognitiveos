### Finding 1: CNSA 2.0 — NSA's Enforceable NSS PQC Requirements & NATO-Internal BSI Divergence (NEW)
- **Source:** https://postquantum.com/cnsa-2-0/complete-guide/
- **Date:** Guide updated May 14, 2026; CNSA 2.0 V2.1 (current) dated Dec 2024
- **Summary:** CNSA 2.0 is the NSA's operationally-specific, enforceable PQC algorithm suite for National Security Systems — distinct from NIST standards (suggestions) or OMB memos (inventory). **From Jan 1, 2027, every new NSS acquisition must support CNSA 2.0 algorithms or cannot be deployed**, enforced via procurement eligibility, NIAP validation, and the Risk Management Framework. The approved suite: **ML-KEM-1024** (FIPS 203, key establishment, highest param set only), **ML-DSA-87** (FIPS 204, signatures, highest param set only), **AES-256** (FIPS 197), **SHA-384/512** (FIPS 180-4), and **LMS/XMSS** (NIST SP 800-208) for firmware/software signing. Three versions evolved: V1.0 (Sept 2022, pre-standard names), V2.0 (April 2024, added exclusions, rejected QKD for NSS), V2.1 (Dec 2024, current — FIPS designations, prohibited HashML-DSA, stated NSA won't add future NIST PQC standards). **Key exclusions and what they signal:** SLH-DSA/FIPS 205 excluded (large ~17KB signatures; NSA confident in lattice security — no non-lattice fallback needed); **Germany's BSI disagrees and recommends SLH-DSA as a hedge** — a direct NATO-internal divergence on PQC strategy. FN-DSA/Falcon (FIPS 206) excluded (implementation-error susceptibility). HashML-DSA prohibited. QKD rejected for NSS. Implementations using draft CRYSTALS-Kyber/Dilithium specs are NOT compliant — only final FIPS 203/204 conformant builds qualify. Transition timeline by category: software/firmware signing support from 2025, exclusive use by 2030; other categories through 2030-2035.

## Strategic Implications for Aras Integrasi / CSCDC Engagement

1. **The NSA–BSI divergence on SLH-DSA is the most actionable new intelligence for Malaysia's PQC Sandbox design.** NSA excludes SLH-DSA (confident in lattice security, ~17KB signatures are an interoperability burden); Germany's BSI recommends it as a non-lattice hedge. Any Malaysian PQC Sandbox must take a position on this debate. A sandbox that tests *both* ML-DSA and SLH-DSA (and HQC as a code-based backup) would be more internationally credible and could serve as a neutral ASEAN evaluation ground bridging the NSA and BSI approaches — a genuine differentiator for ASEAN positioning (PIR-OPP005-010).

2. **The January 1, 2027 U.S. NSS acquisition mandate is the nearest-term international deadline.** Malaysia's PQC Sandbox launch (with RM 300K conference budget) should be calibrated against this: by Jan 2027, all U.S. national-security procurements must be PQC-capable. Malaysian CNII operators supplying or interoperating with U.S. defence/security systems will face downstream requirements. This creates urgency for a Malaysian CNII PQC migration timeline (PIR-OPP005-008) even if no domestic mandate exists yet.

3. **QKD rejection by NSA is a clear signal for the sandbox scope.** NSA explicitly rejects QKD for National Security Systems. If Malaysia's PQC Sandbox is tempted to include QKD (given quantum-key-distribution vendor interest in Asia), the NSA position provides authoritative grounds to deprioritise QKD in favour of standard PQC algorithms — aligning the sandbox with the dominant international direction (PIR-OPP003-005).

4. **The June 2026 governance stack (NSPM-12 → EO 14412/14413 → OMB M-26-15) shows PQC is now enforceable, not advisory.** The Quantum Computing Cybersecurity Preparedness Act (statutory, cannot be revoked by EO, congressional reporting through 2029) means U.S. PQC migration is legally durable across administrations. For CSCDC's international-partner engagement, this means the U.S. PQC commitment is a long-term structural fact, not a political preference — strengthening the case for Malaysia to build enduring PQC partnerships now.

5. **The PQCC roster provides a ready-made industry/expert partner map.** Founding members (MITRE, SandboxAQ, PQShield, IBM Quantum, Microsoft) and advisory board (Stebila, Katz, Montgomery, Schwabe) represent the core of the global PQC industry-academia ecosystem. For PIR-OPP005-009 (talent pipeline), these named organisations and individuals are the most direct route to PQC expertise for any Malaysian capacity-building or sandbox-partnership outreach.
