### Finding 3: NIST HAWK Algorithm Withdrawn After AI-Discovered Vulnerability (July 28, 2026)
- **Source:** https://www.nist.gov/pqc
- **Date:** July 28, 2026 (NIST page update reflecting withdrawal)
- **Summary:** NIST reported that **Anthropic used an AI model to discover a vulnerability in the HAWK lattice-based digital-signature algorithm** (a candidate under NIST's ongoing additional-signature standardisation process). The HAWK development team subsequently **withdrew the algorithm**; it will not be standardised or deployed. NIST stressed this does **not** affect the already-finalised ML-KEM (FIPS 203) or ML-DSA (FIPS 204), which use different mathematical foundations that remain secure. This is the **first publicly disclosed use of AI to break a PQC candidate** — a Sovereign-AI-relevant signal that AI capability is now a factor in cryptographic assurance, not just a consumer of it.

### Finding 4: NIST PQC Standards Status — Finalised Algorithms & 2035 Deprecation
- **Source:** https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography ; https://www.nist.gov/pqc
- **Date:** August 2024 (standards released); page current as of July 2026
- **Summary:** NIST released its first three finalised PQC standards in **August 2024**: **FIPS 203 (ML-KEM, module-lattice key encapsulation)**, **FIPS 204 (ML-DSA, module-lattice digital signatures)**, **FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA, stateless hash-based signatures)**. In **March 2025**, NIST selected **HQC** (code-based) as a fifth algorithm/backup for general encryption. In **May 2026**, nine candidates advanced to the third round for additional digital signatures. Per **NIST IR 8547**, NIST will **deprecate and ultimately remove quantum-vulnerable algorithms from its standards by 2035**, with high-risk systems transitioning much earlier. IETF is incorporating PQC algorithms into core internet protocols including TLS. The NCCoE runs a Migration to PQC Community of Interest and is publishing migration guidance.
