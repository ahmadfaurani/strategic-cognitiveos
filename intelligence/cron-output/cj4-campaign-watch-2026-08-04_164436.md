## Executive Summary

This cycle collected 23 substantive findings across the full PIR spectrum. The Malaysian anti-deepfake landscape has matured significantly since early 2026: Parliament passed the **Cybercrimes Bill 2026** (July 1, 2026) and the government is tabling a companion **AI Governance Bill (RUU Tadbir Urus AI)**. MCMC enforcement has scaled to 40,000+ content removals and a 93% case resolution rate. Malaysia is spearheading **ASEAN-level deepfake guidelines** through MCMC. No dedicated national "anti-deepfake awareness campaign" in the public-facing creative sense has been identified — existing programs (CyberSAFE, Safe Internet Campaign, Sebenarnya.my, AIFA Chatbot, My Cyber Hero) are institutional/educational rather than creative campaigns. This represents a significant **white space for CSCDC's RM 80K anti-deepfake campaign allocation**. On the creative agency front, no cybersecurity-specific government creative panel was found, but AD Malaysia and Walk Production are proven government-sector creative agencies. On content studio infrastructure, MIMOS (Trusted Intelligence Platform), Iskandar Malaysia Studio, and MDEC's MYVIRTUO program represent existing infrastructure CSCDC could leverage.

## Strategic Analysis: In-House vs. Outsourced Content Production

### Current State Assessment
- **No dedicated public-facing anti-deepfake creative campaign** has been identified in Malaysia. Existing programs (CyberSAFE, Safe Internet Campaign, Sebenarnya.my, AIFA Chatbot, My Cyber Hero) are institutional, educational, or tool-based — not creative/media campaigns.
- **CyberSecurity Malaysia's social media presence is nascent**: TikTok account has only 209 followers and 274 likes; YouTube presence exists but engagement appears limited.
- **MCMC's approach is enforcement-first** (takedowns, penalties, licensing) rather than creative awareness-first.

### In-House Path (RM 100K Content Studio)
- **Existing infrastructure to leverage:** Iskandar Malaysia Studio (multilingual crews), MDEC MYVIRTUO (virtual production with government financial support), MIMOS TIP (detection technology), CyberSecurity Malaysia's existing brand
- **Advantages:** Sovereign control, data security, multilingual capability (Malaysia's BM/EN/CN proficiency), alignment with Digital Trust Strategy 2026-2030
- **Risk:** RM 100K is insufficient for full studio build-out; would require partnership/leverage of existing infrastructure (IMS, MYVIRTUO) rather than ground-up construction

### Outsourced Path (Creative Agency)
- **Identified candidates:** AD Malaysia (proven government experience: JAKIM, KPDNKK, ANM, MARDI; multilingual BM/EN; procurement-compliant), Walk Production (40 in-house specialists, GLC/government experience, full creative stack including TikTok/video)
- **Advantages:** Faster time-to-market, creative expertise, established procurement relationships, no capital expenditure
- **Risk:** Less control over messaging, ongoing retainer costs, potential misalignment with national security messaging requirements

### Recommended Hybrid Model
CSCDC should pursue a **hybrid approach**: partner with an established creative agency (AD Malaysia or Walk Production) for campaign creative production while building lightweight in-house capability leveraging existing government infrastructure (MDEC MYVIRTUO, Iskandar Malaysia Studio partnerships). Aras Integrasi can position as methodology partner defining the campaign framework, content standards, and measurement methodology while the creative agency executes production.
