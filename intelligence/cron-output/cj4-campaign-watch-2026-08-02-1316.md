## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This cycle produced **21 findings** across Malaysian government, ASEAN, and international sources. Key takeaways for CSCDC strategy:

1. **Anti-deepfake campaign NOT yet a dedicated standalone product.** Malaysia has substantial existing awareness infrastructure (MCMC Internet Safety Campaign 2026, Online Safety Campaign Roadshow, CyberSAFE/Safer Internet Day), but deepfake-specific public campaigning is currently absorbed into general "online safety / misinformation" framing rather than a branded anti-deepfake push. **CSCDC's RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign would be entering a real but unfilled niche** — leverage, not duplicate, existing MCMC/CyberSecurity Malaysia assets.
2. **Strong in-house signal from Putrajaya.** PM Anwar mandated (5 June 2026) that all future government digital services be built in-house (data sovereignty, source-code access, civil-servant talent). This is a directional tailwind for CSCDC's in-house Content Studio over pure outsourcing — though the mandate targets software/infrastructure, not creative content per se.
3. **Deepfake threat is acute and current.** Jan 2026 saw the largest coordinated AI deepfake blackmail operation against Malaysian lawmakers (≥10 politicians incl. Comms Minister Fahmi Fadzil). This creates real political demand for credible anti-deepfake capability CSCDC can serve.
4. **Regulatory tailwinds:** Cybercrimes Bill 2026 (passed Dewan Rakyat 1 July 2026), Online Safety Act 2025 (in force 1 Jan 2026), and ASEAN deepfake guidelines (Malaysia/MCMC leading, drafting as of July 2026).
5. **Creative-agency landscape gap:** No open-source evidence of a Malaysian creative agency with documented government cyber/defence campaign track record. NAGA DDB Tribal is the top creative performer (4As Agency of Year 2025) but publicly known for telco/FMCG work. CSCDC may need to either (a) upskill an in-house unit, or (b) bring a strong creative agency + pair it with a methodology partner (Aras Integrasi positioning).
