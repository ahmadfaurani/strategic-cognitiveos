## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This cycle produced high-value intelligence across the full crisis-protocol stack. The single most significant discovery: **NACSA Chief Executive Ir. Dr. Megat Zuhairy Megat Tajuddin concurrently serves as Overall Coordinator of the ASEAN Regional CERT Task Force** — placing Malaysia's national cyber agency at the operational helm of regional incident-response coordination. This directly de-risks CSCDC's cross-border coordination PIRs. Malaysia's crisis-protocol architecture is now demonstrably mature: the **National Cyber Crisis Management Plan (NCCMP)** and **Arahan MKN No. 24** ("Dasar dan Mekanisme Pengurusan Krisis Siber Negara") together define a before/during/after cyber-crisis framework with a 5-level national threat scale, sector leads for 11 NCII sectors (appointed under Cyber Security Act 2024 / Act 854), and a multi-agency response stack (NC4 → MyCERT/CDOC/MCMC-NSC → PDRM). The **MCSS 2025–2030 explicitly commits to establishing a "cyber security crisis communication framework"** — directly validating CSCDC's RM 150K Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook initiative. A ISIS Malaysia–US Embassy tabletop exercise (Sept 2025) surfaced the critical recurring weakness CSCDC must solve: agencies responded in **silos**, were **reactionary**, and lacked inter-agency coordination protocols — the exact gap a War Room methodology is designed to close. Internationally, Malaysia participated in ACID 2025 (first physical ASEAN CERT drill) and NATO Locked Shields 2026 (41 nations, 4,000+ defenders) benchmarks an integrated technical + strategic-communications drill standard.

### Finding 3: Arahan MKN No. 24 — "Dasar dan Mekanisme Pengurusan Krisis Siber Negara" (National Cyber Crisis Management Policy & Mechanism)
- **Source:** http://marine21.marine.gov.my/appl/JPICT/JPICT012019/AGENDA%20104%20Arahan%20MKN%20No.%2024%20-%20MOT%20FINAL.pdf
- **Date:** MKN directive (presentation to Marine Department); companion disaster directive Arahan MKN No. 20
- **Summary:** Arahan MKN No. 24 establishes the uniform, proactive policy and mechanism for monitoring and managing cyber crises, covering **before, during, and after** a cyber crisis, and defining CNII agencies' roles. It rests on **6 Principles**: (1) National Cyber Crisis Management Structure; (2) National Cyber Threat Levels; (3) Computer Emergency Response Teams (CERT); (4) Cyber Security Protection Mechanism; (5) **Response, Communication & Coordination Procedures**; (6) **Readiness Programmes**. Policy objective: protect CNII through a comprehensive public-private framework, increase readiness, increase capability, ensure uniform response, reduce impact. The directive explicitly states that if a cyber crisis escalates to physical disaster/public-order threat, it is handled under **Arahan MKN No. 20/18/21** (disaster/terrorism/public order mechanisms). The 8 policy pillars include "Cyber Security Emergency Readiness" (led by MKN): strengthening national CERT, developing effective incident reporting mechanisms, periodic vulnerability assessment programmes. **This is the MKN framework CSCDC's drill must align with — Principle 6 (Readiness Programmes) and Principle 5 (Communication Procedures) map directly to CSCDC's RM 200K drill and RM 150K playbook deliverables.**

- **Date:** Portal data 3 Aug 2026; threat level last set 5 Apr 2024
