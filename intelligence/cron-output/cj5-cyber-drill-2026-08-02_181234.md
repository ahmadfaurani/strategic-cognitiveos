## Executive Summary

This cycle produced substantial, high-value intelligence directly material to CSCDC Unit 4's War Room Komunikasi mandate and the RM 150K / RM 200K budget lines. Key outcomes:

- **Malaysia's national cyber crisis architecture is statutory, not merely directive-based.** The Cyber Security Act 2024 (Act 854, effective 26 Aug 2024) supersedes NSC Directive No.26 and anchors the National Cyber Crisis Management Plan (NCCMP). Arahan MKN No.24 remains the operational directive under which X-MAYA drills are conducted. This dual statutory+directive framework is the single most important benchmark for CSCDC's playbook design.
- **X-MAYA is the established national cyber drill** (since 2008, joint MKN/NSC + CyberSecurity Malaysia), running under Arahan MKN No.24. It is communication + technical + coordination integrated — directly relevant to PIR-OPP007-001 (drill scope).
- **Malaysia holds a leadership role in regional drills:** NACSA CEO Dr Megat Zuhairy Megat Tajuddin is Overall Coordinator of the ASEAN Regional CERT Task Force; Malaysia chaired the inaugural Taskforce meeting (Aug 2024). Malaysia participated in ACID 2025 (20th iteration, first physical, Oct 21-22 2025, Singapore).
- **Locked Shields 2026** (NATO CCDCOE, concluded 24 Apr 2026) set the global benchmark: 41 nations, 4,000+ participants, focus on crisis management + critical infrastructure + disinformation/political pressure — directly transferable to War Room methodology.
- **Inter-agency coordination is structurally defined** via the NC4-led National CERT ecosystem (NC4/NACSA + MyCERT/CSM + CDOC/MINDEF + MCMC NSC), with PDRM as the enforcement arm.
- **2024 was a watershed breach year** (MyKad 17M records, ATM/Wisma Putra/KDN GitHub exfil, Prasarana RansomHub 316GB) — providing concrete crisis-communication lessons for the playbook.

## FINDINGS

### Finding 1: National Cyber Crisis Management Plan (NCCMP) — Statutory Crisis Communication Framework
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/nccmp.php ; https://www.weekly-echo.com/nacsa-strengthens-national-cyber-security-preparedness-with-nccmp/ (Bernama, 14 Mar 2025)
- **Date:** 14 March 2025 (Bernama report); NCCMP ongoing/enhanced
- **Summary:** NACSA's NCCMP is the national-level plan covering procedures for **detection, response, communication and coordination** during cyberattacks across all 10 CNII sectors (defence & security; banking & finance; info & comms; energy; transport; water; health; government services; emergency services; food & agriculture). It is the main reference for Sector Leads and CNII agencies to develop SOPs. Per the Bernama report, on a malicious-code incident: (1) NCII entity must immediately report to NACSA CEO + NCII sector head under Section 23(1) Act 854 + Notification Regulations 2024; (2) contain, restore, forensics; (3) NACSA collaborates with PDRM for enforcement. NCCMP is being enhanced in line with Act 854 enforcement and NC4 upgrade. Malaysia improved from 6th (2023) to 12th (2024) globally as cyberattack target per Microsoft Digital Defence report.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-002, PIR-INIT-CSCDC-006, PIR-OPP007-002, PIR-OPP002-007
- **Confidence:** HIGH

### Finding 2: Cyber Security Act 2024 (Act 854) — Statutory Anchor for Crisis Response & Drills
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/act854.php ; https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/12/malaysias-new-cyber-security-act-2024-a-summary-and-brief-comparative-analysis
- **Date:** Gazetted 26 Jun 2024; in force 26 Aug 2024
- **Summary:** Act 854 supersedes NSC Directive No.26 and establishes the National Cyber Security Committee (JKSN, chaired at Ministerial level), NACSA CEO powers, NCII sector leads + entities. Key provisions: Section 23 mandates incident notification (failure = MYR 500K fine / up to 10 yrs); Section 22 mandates risk assessments/audits; **Section 24 empowers NACSA CEO to direct NCII entities to participate in cyber exercises** (non-compliance = MYR 100K fine). Extra-territorial application. Licensing regime for SOC monitoring + penetration testing services. This is the legal basis making drill participation mandatory — directly relevant to PIR-OPP007-004 (participants) and PIR-OPP007-005 (success metrics).
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP007-002, PIR-OPP002-002, PIR-OPP007-004, PIR-OPP007-005, PIR-OPP002-007
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Tag:** [VERIFIED]

### Finding 3: X-MAYA — National Cyber Crisis Exercise (Arahan MKN No.24)
- **Source:** https://www.flickr.com/photos/nacsamalaysia/albums/72157669744292968 ; https://www.mkn.gov.my/web/ms/2020/01/30/nacsa-developing-master-plan-on-cyber-security/ ; https://www.parlimen.gov.my/files/jindex/pdf/JDR03042019.pdf ; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rq0D4GTEt8Q
- **Date:** First held 2008; iterations 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2013, X-Maya 6 (2017); Nov 2020 iteration planned (100 CNII agencies)
- **Summary:** X-MAYA (Latih Amal Krisis Siber Negara) is Malaysia's simulated, coordinated national cyber crisis drill — a **joint effort between the National Security Council (MKN/NSC) and CyberSecurity Malaysia**, implemented under **Arahan MKN No.24** (per Parliament Hansard 3 Apr 2019). It assesses cyber security emergency readiness of Malaysia's Critical National Information Infrastructure (CNII) agencies and tests procedures + technical expertise, identifying weaknesses/gaps. Designed to familiarise CNII agencies with cyber incident handling mechanisms. X-Maya 6 was conducted in 2017. The Nov 2020 iteration was planned to involve ≥100 CNII agencies (per DPM Dr Wan Azizah / MKN statement). The exercise is **integrated** (technical + procedural + coordination), not purely technical.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP007-001, PIR-OPP007-003, PIR-OPP007-004, PIR-OPP007-009, PIR-OPP002-005, PIR-OPP007-010

### Finding 7: 2024 Malaysia Data Breach Landscape — Crisis Communication Lessons
- **Source:** https://www.flawtrack.com/blogs/data-breaches-affecting-malaysia-in-2024
- **Date:** 2024 (review published 2024/2025)
- **Summary:** 2024 was a watershed for Malaysian cybersecurity with systemic breaches: (1) **MyKad Compromise** — alleged leak of 17M records (names, addresses, national ID numbers) to dark web; (2) **Government data exfiltration** — Malaysian Armed Forces (ATM), Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Wisma Putra), Ministry of Home Affairs (KDN) datasets published on GitHub (geopolitical/embarrassment motive); (3) **Prasarana Malaysia ransomware** — RansomHub group, 316GB exfiltrated (public transport operator — critical infrastructure); (4) **Maybank** — alleged customer data sale on illicit forums; (5) **High-profile account hijackings** — KWSP (EPF), SPR (Election Commission), Malaysia Gazette, by 'Solana' group (misinformation/scam vectors eroding trust in official channels). Lessons: failure to enforce foundational controls at scale; need for proactive Attack Surface Management, PDPA breach-notification strengthening, public-private threat intel sharing, MFA/phishing sims/network segmentation/patch management. **These incidents are concrete case studies for the holding-statement / 30-minute KPI playbook and War Room activation thresholds.**

## ANALYSIS & INFERENCE (Analyst-Assessed, Not Open-Source Verified)

The following address PIRs where open sources are silent or partial, drawing on the verified findings above plus the CSCDC operational context.

**PIR-OPP002-001 (RM 150K allocation type):** No public disclosure of the RM 150K Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook allocation exists. Inference: Given (a) Act 854 makes NCCMP a NACSA-led, inter-agency instrument; (b) the playbook must integrate with Section 23 notification + NCCMP communication procedures; and (c) MKN coordinates national crisis — the most defensible model is **inter-agency collaboration led by NACSA, with external consultancy engaged for specialist drafting/peer-review of holding-statement templates**. Pure external consultancy risks producing a playbook disconnected from the statutory NCCMP; pure internal development risks missing best-practice crisis-comms methodology (e.g., the 30-min holding-statement KPI is a private-sector/PR-industry standard). RM 150K is consistent with a consultancy-led drafting + inter-agency validation workshop model, not a full standalone build. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-OPP002-003 (physical War Room space):** No open source confirms a CSCDC-dedicated War Room. NACSA HQ is co-located with MKN at the Perdana Putra Building (PM's Department, Putrajaya); NC4 is the operational command centre. Inference: CSCDC's War Room Komunikasi most plausibly leverages/extends the NC4 or MKN crisis-ops footprint rather than a greenfield build, unless CSCDC is institutionally separate from NACSA. If CSCDC is a distinct unit, a dedicated space must be built/fitted — this is the highest-cost variable. **Confidence: LOW (no source).**

**PIR-OPP002-004 (SULIT-cleared briefers):** No open source. Inference: Because NACSA sits under MKN/PM's Department and handles NCII national-security incidents, SULIT clearance for War Room briefers is the default expectation; briefers handling NCII defence & security sector data or classified forensics would require it. The briefing roster should map to the 10 NCII sector leads + NACSA CEO office + PDRM liaison. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-OPP002-006 (holding statement bank):** No public holding-statement bank disclosed. The NCCMP explicitly covers "communication" procedures, and the 2024 breach cases (MyKad, ATM/Wisma Putra/KDN, Prasarana) are live templates. Inference: a holding-statement bank should be tiered by CNII sector + incident type (data breach, ransomware, account hijack/misinformation, critical-service outage) with the 30-minute KPI as the drafting SLA. Status of an existing bank is unknown; the RM 150K line likely funds its formalisation. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-OPP002-008 (activation threshold):** The NC4 threat-level scale (LOW→MODERATE→CAUTION→HIGH→CRITICAL) provides a candidate threshold framework, and Act 854 Section 23 mandates reporting. Inference: War Room activation likely keys off the NC4 threat level reaching HIGH/CRITICAL or a confirmed NCII-sector incident with public-confidence or cross-sector contagion risk — mirroring how X-MAYA exercises stress-test escalation. The 2024 multi-sector breaches show the threshold must account for misinformation/loss-of-trust (KWSP/SPR hijacks) not just data loss. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-OPP007-007 (public comms about drill):** X-MAYA has historically been low-publicity (NACSA Flickr/YouTube documentation rather than broad public announcements); ACID has formal press releases (CSA, Bernama). Inference: a CSCDC national drill should pre-plan a public-communication track (transparency builds public resilience) distinct from the SULIT technical track, modelling on ACID's press engagement. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-OPP007-008 (RM 200K sufficiency):** Benchmarking — ACID 2025 ran 36 responders over 2 days (Singapore-funded infrastructure); Locked Shields 2026 ran 4,000+ over 3 days with NATO/industry funding (orders of magnitude larger). Malaysia's X-MAYA involves 100+ CNII agencies. RM 200K is **sufficient for a national X-MAYA-style tabletop + limited live-fire exercise with existing CSM/NC4 infrastructure**, but **insufficient for a full Locked Shields-scale live-fire integrated exercise**. A phased model (tabletop in cycle 1 → partial live-fire in cycle 2) is realistic within RM 200K if CSM infrastructure is leveraged in-kind. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-OPP007-010 (post-drill improvement):** X-MAYA's stated purpose is to "identify weaknesses and gaps in procedures and technical expertise" — implying a findings→remediation loop, but no public post-drill improvement framework was found. Inference: CSCDC should codify a formal After-Action Review (AAR) → NCCMP SOP update → next-drill scenario design loop, borrowing Locked Shields' "lessons identified → real-world readiness" philosophy. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

**PIR-INIT-CSCDC-005 (political→cyber War Room methodology transfer):** No public source on CSCDC specifically. The methodology transfer is sound: X-MAYA's integrated (technical+communication+coordination) design and Locked Shields' disinformation/political-pressure testing validate that cyber crisis response IS a war-room function. DAF's political war room experience (rapid message discipline, stakeholder mapping, 24-hour news-cycle management, opponent narrative counter) transfers directly to: (a) holding-statement drafting speed, (b) misinformation counter-narrative ops (cf. 2024 KWSP/SPR hijacks), (c) inter-agency briefing choreography, (d) media holding during forensic uncertainty. The political war room's "control the narrative before facts are complete" maps to the 30-minute holding-statement KPI. **Confidence: MEDIUM (inference).**

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC

1. **CSCDC's playbook should be drafted as an NCCMP-aligned, Act 854-compliant instrument**, not a standalone PR document. The RM 150K buys best-fit if scoped as: NCCMP communication-procedure formalisation + sector-tiered holding-statement bank + inter-agency validation workshops + external peer-review of the 30-minute KPI.
2. **The National Cyber Drill (RM 200K) should be positioned as the X-MAYA successor/enhancement under Arahan MKN No.24**, leveraging CSM's existing CyberDrill methodology and NC4 infrastructure in-kind — not a greenfield Locked Shields clone.
