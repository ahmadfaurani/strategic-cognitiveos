### Finding 1: Malaysia's Cybercrimes Bill 2026 Passes Parliament — Deepfakes Explicitly Criminalised
- **Source:** https://malayandaily.com/article/parliament-passes-cybercrimes-bill-2026-to-combat-deepfakes-and-digital-abuse ; https://malaytime.com/article/parliament-passes-cybercrimes-bill-2026-with-safeguards-against-digital-abuse
- **Date:** 1 July 2026 (Dewan Rakyat passage via voice vote); reported 8–9 July 2026
- **Summary:** The Dewan Rakyat endorsed the 61-clause Cybercrimes Bill 2026 on 1 July 2026 after debate among 48 legislators. It establishes a dedicated statutory framework criminalising the **creation and distribution of deepfakes** and digitally manipulated non-consensual intimate images, moving victims away from reliance on general defamation/harassment laws. DPM Ahmad Zahid Hamidi provided safeguards: the bill does not supersede the Official Secrets Act 1972, does not grant blanket computer-access authority, and requires written notice + reasonable investigative grounds for data disclosure. Malaysia now sits alongside Singapore (POFMA) and Indonesia (EIT Law) with a synthetic-media-specific statute. Tech platforms operating in Malaysia face new offence categories and reporting obligations requiring investment in content moderation and evidence preservation. **This creates the legal narrative anchor CSCDC's anti-deepfake campaign has been missing — "deepfakes are now a crime; here's how to recognise and report them."**
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP008-001, PIR-OPP008-006, PIR-OPP008-009

### Finding 2: ASEAN Drafting Deepfake-Specific Social-Media Guidelines (MCMC Spearheading) + ASEAN AI SAFE Declaration (Malaysia Lead Proponent)

- **Date:** 22 July 2026 (IRC 2026, KL); Declaration adopted 26 October 2025 (47th ASEAN Summit, KL)
- **Summary:** At the International Regulators Conference 2026 (Shangri-La KL, 21 July 2026), ASEAN Secretariat official Hazremi Hamid confirmed member states are developing **guidelines on safe social-media use to address deepfake content and AI-driven misinformation**, with **MCMC spearheading implementation**. The guidelines cover procedures for handling inaccurate information, digital-literacy strengthening, **clear labelling of AI-generated content**, and public awareness/training. The ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) provides baseline provisions. Separately, the **Declaration on the Establishment of an ASEAN AI Safety Network (ASEAN AI SAFE)** was adopted 26 Oct 2025 at the 47th ASEAN Summit in KL, with **Malaysia as Lead Proponent** — a voluntary, non-binding regional mechanism under ADGSOM/WG-AI to coordinate AI safety, exchange best practices, and build capacity, acknowledging cross-border AI risks including misinformation and content manipulation. The expanded ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics (generative AI) was published Jan 2025 at ADGMIN 2025, explicitly listing deepfakes among six major risks. **Malaysia is the regional convener on AI/deepfake governance — CSCDC can position its campaign as supporting a Malaysia-led ASEAN initiative, a strong inter-agency legitimacy frame.**

- **Summary:** MIMOS Berhad (national applied R&D agency under MOSTI) is developing deepfake-detection technology localised for the Malaysian context, arguing detection AI trained on Western faces/English speech/Latin-script documents has **documented performance gaps on Malaysian faces, Bahasa Malaysia voice synthesis, and Malay-language fabricated documents** — making localised training "an engineering requirement, not a policy preference."
