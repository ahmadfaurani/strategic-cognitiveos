### Finding 3: NIST Additional Digital Signature Schemes — Round 3 Announced, NISTIR 8610 Published (NEW)
- **Source:** https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/pqc-dig-sig
- **Date:** Page updated July 29, 2026; Round 3 candidates announced May 14, 2026
- **Summary:** NIST's Additional Digital Signature Schemes project page (updated 29 Jul 2026) confirms Round 3 candidates were announced on **14 May 2026**, with **NISTIR 8610** (Status Report on the Second Round) now available. The process: 40 algorithms submitted (Round 1, June 2023 deadline) → 14 candidates advanced (Round 2) → Round 3 selected May 2026. NIST's particular interest is **general-purpose signature schemes NOT based on structured lattices** with **short signatures and fast verification** — because the already-standardised ML-DSA and SLH-DSA cover lattice-based and hash-based approaches, and no non-lattice-based signature schemes remained after the first three rounds of the original PQC competition. FALCON has been designated as **FN-DSA** (FIPS 206, IPD issued early 2026). Project leads: Dr. Lily Chen, Dr. Dustin Moody, Dr. Yi-Kai Liu. This confirms NIST is actively diversifying the PQC signature algorithm pool beyond structured lattices — relevant to any PQC Sandbox that should test multiple algorithm families.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP005-001 (technical scope — algorithm families under standardisation), PIR-OPP005-002 (timeline — Round 3 May 2026, FIPS 206 IPD early 2026)

### Finding 4: DHS PQC Roadmap — Organisational Migration Template (NEW)
- **Source:** https://www.dhs.gov/quantum
- **Date:** Last updated January 28, 2025
- **Summary:** DHS, partnering with NIST, has published a roadmap to help organisations protect data and systems from quantum computing risks. The roadmap provides **7 concrete steps**: (1) Engage standards organisations on algorithm/protocol changes; (2) Inventory critical datasets (identify what data is at risk of future decryption); (3) Inventory all cryptographic systems; (4) Identify acquisition/cybersecurity/data security standards requiring PQC updates; (5) Mark quantum-vulnerable systems; (6) Prioritise systems based on organisational functions; (7) Develop a transition plan. System prioritisation factors include: whether the system is a high-value asset, what it protects (key stores, passwords, root keys, signing keys, PII), inter-system communications, federal entity information sharing, critical infrastructure sector support, and data protection longevity. CISA is conducting a macro-level assessment of priority **National Critical Functions** to identify where transition work is underway, where the greatest risk resides, and what sectors require federal support. DHS warns against buying premature PQC solutions before NIST standards are finalised. This 7-step template is directly transferable to Malaysia's CNII operator PQC migration planning.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP005-008 (CNII migration timeline — concrete template), PIR-OPP005-001 (technical scope — system prioritisation methodology), PIR-OPP005-003 (private sector participation — critical infrastructure engagement model)

## Strategic Implications for Aras Integrasi / CSCDC Engagement

1. **The U.S. PQC policy ecosystem is far broader than EO 14412 alone.** This cycle's extraction of the CISA PQC Initiative page reveals at least **6 additional policy instruments** (EO 14413, EO 14306, NSM-10, 6 U.S.C. § 1526, OMB M-23-02, DHS Roadmap) plus interagency coordination across CISA/NIST/NSA/GSA/OMB/ONCD. For Malaysia's PQC Sandbox, this means the U.S. engagement model is not a single point of contact but a multi-agency architecture — CSCDC should plan for multi-track engagement.

2. **G7 PQC guidance is a new multilateral alignment opportunity.** The G7 Cyber Expert Group's Financial Sector PQC Roadmap provides internationally-coordinated guidance directly relevant to Malaysia's CNII financial sector operators. Aligning the PQC Sandbox with G7 guidance (even as a non-G7 member) would lend international legitimacy and provide a tested migration framework. This is particularly relevant for PIR-OPP005-010 (ASEAN positioning) — Malaysia could position itself as the ASEAN bridge to G7 PQC standards.

3. **DHS 7-step roadmap is directly transferable to CNII operators.** The DHS-NIST joint roadmap's 7 concrete preparation steps (engage standards orgs → inventory critical data → inventory crypto systems → identify standards for update → mark quantum-vulnerable → prioritise → develop transition plan) with system prioritisation factors is a ready-made template for Malaysia's CNII PQC migration guidance. CSCDC could adapt this directly.

4. **Singapore's lack of a visible PQC programme page confirms Malaysia's first-mover window.** Direct extraction of Singapore CSA returned 404s at quantum-specific URLs, suggesting Singapore has not yet published a dedicated PQC landing page. Combined with the absence of any ASEAN-wide PQC initiative, this confirms Malaysia has a genuine first-mover opportunity to establish the region's first PQC Sandbox.

5. **NIST's active search for non-lattice-based signatures signals the algorithm landscape is still evolving.** Round 3 of the additional digital signature process (May 2026) means the PQC algorithm landscape will continue to expand through 2026-2027. Any PQC Sandbox should be designed for **cryptographic agility** (ability to swap algorithms) rather than locking into a single algorithm, given the HAWK withdrawal precedent.
