- **Source:** https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/04/21/1135652/weaponized-deepfakes-ai-artificial-intelligence/
- **Date:** April 21, 2026
- **Summary:** MIT Technology Review documents the shift of deepfakes from "theoretical warning" to "malicious reality." Key case studies and data points: (a) A 2023 study found **98% of deepfakes were pornographic, 99% depicted women**; (b) Grok (xAI) users created millions of sexualized images since the "edit image" function launched — 81% depicted women; (c) **Trump administration** regularly produces/shares AI-generated images — e.g., White House shared an altered image darkening a civil rights lawyer's skin; (d) **Texas AG Ken Paxton** shared a deepfake video showing his primary opponent dancing with Rep. Crockett (never happened, undisclosed as fake). Proposed solutions and limitations: technical safeguards (bypassable via open-source models), user protective actions (unrealistic at scale), legislation (requires strict enforcement). The article warns of worsening risks ahead of US midterms and weakened federal election-integrity agencies. This is the **first international case study material** collected, partially addressing PIR-OPP008-010, and provides campaign messaging angles (gender-targeted deepfakes, political weaponisation, detection limitations) directly applicable to CSCDC's BM/EN/CN content strategy.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC (DELTA FROM PREVIOUS CYCLE)

1. **CyberSAFE is a potential partner/template for CSCDC's Content Studio.** The discovery that CyberSecurity Malaysia already runs a segmented multimedia awareness programme (CyberKids/Youth/Parent/Organization, with videos, posters, games, quizzes, wallpapers, social media, ambassadors, baseline studies) significantly de-risks PIR-OPP004-002. CSCDC's RM100K Content Studio allocation could model on or partner with CyberSAFE rather than building from zero — or the Content Studio could be positioned as the deepfake-specialised extension of CyberSAFE's general awareness work.

2. **Hybrid in-house/outsourced model now has evidence on both sides.** In-house: CyberSAFE (CyberSecurity Malaysia). Outsourced: MSMART (MCMC, CIPS-certified tender/RFQ). The recommended hybrid (outsourced production + in-house editorial/methodology control, aligned with Aras Integrasi's positioning) is now supported by concrete examples of both modalities within the Malaysian government.

3. **ONSA's Jan 1, 2026 enforcement date** means any CSCDC campaign launched now operates under a live, enforced regulatory framework — not a draft. Deepfake-fraud content falls under ONSA's "financial fraud & scams" priority category. Campaign messaging should reference ONSA as the legal authority backing awareness efforts.

4. **International case studies now available** (MIT Tech Review, Apr 2026) provide ready-made campaign messaging: (a) gender-disproportionate impact of deepfakes (98% pornographic, 99% women), (b) political weaponisation examples, (c) detection-limitation reality. These translate directly into BM/EN/CN content angles.

5. **JPDP's active PDPA enforcement** (Maxis data breach probe) signals that any CSCDC campaign using personal data for micro-targeting (PIR-OPP008-006) must navigate active regulatory oversight — a broadcast/awareness approach (not micro-targeted) is likely safer and more compliant.

6. **Priority gaps for next cycle (unchanged from previous, with refinements):**
   - (a) Enumerate MSMART-registered creative agencies with cyber/defence portfolio (try direct extraction of 4As Malaysia, Malaysian Advertisers Federation)
   - (b) Deep-attempt CyberSecurity Malaysia advisories/events pages (failed to scrape this cycle)
   - (c) Identify deepfake-detection vendors (local + regional: Singapore, Thailand, Australia)
   - (d) Extract CyberSAFE's video/content library to assess quality and multilingual coverage
   - (e) Search for ASEAN Guide on AI Governance (2024) — may contain anti-deepfake provisions
   - (f) Itemise Budget 2026 cybersecurity/digital-safety allocations by agency
