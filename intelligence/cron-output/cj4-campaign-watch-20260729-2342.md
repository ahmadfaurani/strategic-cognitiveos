## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This cycle surfaced a **densely active anti-deepfake policy and campaign environment** in Malaysia as of mid-to-late July 2026. Key signals:

1. **Malaysia now has a legislative backbone, not just policy intent.** The **Cybercrimes Bill 2026** passed the Dewan Rakyat on **1 July 2026** (61 clauses targeting deepfakes, AI-generated abuse, digital impersonation). This is paired with the **Online Safety Act 2025 (ONSA)** and its **Risk Mitigation Code (RMC)**, effective **1 June 2026**, which mandates AI-content labelling and advertiser verification. CSCDC's RM 80K anti-deepfake campaign would now operate inside an active regulatory framework — opportunities for government partnership are strong.

2. **Existing campaigns are platform/agency-led, not a coherent national anti-deepfake narrative.** MCMC runs **KIS2026 (Kempen Internet Selamat 2026)** and the **Sebenarnya.my / AIFA** fact-check platform; TikTok runs **#ThinkTwice** with PDRM/MCMC/SC. These are multilingual (BM/EN/Mandarin/Tamil) but **fragmented** and **scam/online-safety focused**, with no single dedicated anti-deepfake public campaign. **A dedicated anti-deepfake campaign strategy does not yet exist** — CSCDC would be building largely from scratch but can stand on existing infrastructure.

3. **In-house content capability exists but is policy-focused, not production-focused.** The Ministry of Communications' **Content Development Division** coordinates creative-industry policy (Creative Industry Policy, MAC3, BCI2) but the Undersecretary post is **vacant** and the division is structured around policy/grants, not a working production studio. The decision of in-house vs outsourced creative remains genuinely open — strong argument for a **hybrid model** (in-house methodology + outsourced production).

4. **Detection technology is maturing domestically.** **MIMOS Berhad** is developing homegrown deepfake detection (localised for Malaysian faces/BM voice), and the Digital Ministry is co-developing an **AI forensic tool** with **CyberSecurity Malaysia** and **UKM**. This directly de-risks the content studio's verification posture.

5. **RM 32 million** allocated under Budget 2026 for cybercrime/scam response (NACSA Cyber Security & Cryptology Development Centre, NSRC under PDRM, digital forensics). CSCDC's RM 80K + RM 100K sits in a well-funded adjacent ecosystem but is modest relative to the national envelope — positioning as a **specialist methodology partner** (per Aras Integrasi's stance) is the realistic wedge.

6. **ASEAN-level coordination is accelerating.** ASEAN is drafting deepfake/social-media safety guidelines (announced 22 July 2026 at the International Regulators Conference, KL); the ASEAN AI Safety Network declaration was signed Oct 2025. Regional alignment work is a near-term CSCDC positioning opportunity.

- **Date:** RMC effective 1 June 2026
- **Summary:** Under ONSA (Act 866) the **Risk Mitigation Code** forces licensed online platforms to: (1) conduct risk assessments (incl. heightened-risk periods like elections); (2) improve reporting/moderation; (3) **verify advertisers** (MyKad/passport/business registration — third-party verification allowed); (4) **label AI-generated/manipulated content** and give users tools to disclose AI-altered content; (5) review algorithms/recommendation systems; (6) provide user safety tools. 4. **Micro-targeting is constrained but legal.** ONSA/RMC advertiser-verification + AI-labelling + algorithm-review are the operating guardrails. CSCDC campaigns must run through verified-advertiser channels and label AI-generated creative — design this in from the start.

6. **Singapore is the blueprint.** IMDA's combination of (legal backbone + dedicated public-education campaigns + research hub + accredited-vendor procurement lane) is directly portable. Malaysia has the legal backbone and detection tech; the missing piece is the *cohesive single national anti-deepfake campaign* — that gap is CSCDC's wedge.
