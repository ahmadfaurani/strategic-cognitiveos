### Finding 1: Parliament of Malaysia e-PRS Research Note — Deepfake Threats to Political Stability (March 2026)
- **Source:** https://e-prs.parlimen.gov.my/eprs/uploads/results/Nota_Isu_Khusus_-_Ancaman_Teknologi_Deepfake_Terhadap_Kestabilan_Politik_Malaysia_Mac_2026.pdf
- **Date:** March 2026 (Parliamentary Research and Information Service, e-PRS)
- **Summary:** The Parliament of Malaysia's own research service published a dedicated issue paper (Nota Isu Khusus) analysing deepfake technology's threat to political stability and national security. Key NEW data and concepts:

  **Financial impact:** RM5.62 billion in losses from deepfake-related fraud (2023–2025 cumulative) — a distinct aggregate figure adding to prior-cycle MIMOS data (RM1.58B in 2024; RM2.9B in 2025).

  **MCMC takedown statistics by category (2022–2025):**

  | Category | Takedown Requests | Successfully Removed | Success Rate |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Synthetic Media / Deepfake | 3,074 | 2,354 | 77% |
  | Fake News | 55,218 | 46,966 | 85% |
  | All Harmful Content | 697,061 | 641,304 | 92% |

  **58,104 harmful posts remain accessible as of end-2025** — a significant residual threat.

  **MCMC (Jan–Aug 2025):** >400 deepfake contents taken down, most impersonating national leaders (PM, YDPA).

  **Four critical analytical themes:**
  1. **"Trust Exploitation"** — Deepfakes shifting from visual manipulation to exploiting public trust in top leadership (PM, YDPA)
  2. **"Sovereign AI Cloud"** — Protecting strategic data from foreign jurisdiction reach (extra-territorial)
  3. **"Safety by Design" (ONSA 2025)** — Social-media platforms must automatically label AI-generated content
  4. **"Detection AI"** — Defence can no longer rely on human detection; automated AI detection required

  **"Liar's Dividend" concept:** Guilty parties can deny authentic evidence as "fake," and misinformation is accepted as true in a politically divided society. Manipulation of PM's or YDPA's face/voice erodes the foundation of trust in highest institutions.

  **Election-integrity risk:** Deepfake videos of candidates making false racial statements or false confessions right before polling day — official denial time is very limited. Reference to neighbouring countries' experience with "AI slop" flooding timelines during final campaign hours.

  **ONSA 2025 in force 1 January 2026** — but tested by refusal of platforms (X, Grok) to comply with local standards.

  **Proposed:** AI Governance Act and a National AI Detection Hub (Hub Pengesanan AI Kebangsaan).

  **Real-world case:** Elderly couple in Kuala Lumpur travelled 300km after being deceived by a fake deepfake video of a cable car service.

  **CSM + UKM collaboration:** Developing AI detection methodology (confirms prior-cycle F4 finding from a parliamentary source).

### Finding 3: Malaysia Gazette — Two-Layer Enforcement Architecture for AI (Gobind, 22 June 2026)
- **Source:** https://malaysiagazette.com/2026/06/22/ruu-tadbir-urus-ai-perkukuh-kawalan-deepfake-lindungi-mangsa-eksploitasi-seksual-gobind/
- **Date:** 22 June 2026 (Dewan Rakyat Q&A, response to Wong Shu Qi, Kluang-PH)
- **Summary:** Digital Minister Gobind Singh Deo confirmed a deliberate **two-layer legislative approach** to AI misuse, providing NEW structural clarity on how the Cybercrimes Bill and AI Governance Bill interrelate:

  | Layer | Mechanism | Purpose |
  |---|---|---|
  | Layer 1 | Existing laws (incl. new Cybercrimes Bill) | Prosecute those who abuse AI content |
  | Layer 2 | AI Governance Bill (RUU Tadbir Urus AI) | Prevent & ensure accountability across entire AI development lifecycle |

  The AI Governance Bill is **not limited to AI-generated content** — it addresses systemic risks across the entire AI system lifecycle, covering data governance, transparency, risk assessment, and accountability of developers and implementers. It controls threats like deepfakes and synthetic content "from the earliest stage" through governance settings rather than post-hoc prosecution.

  **Three existing statutes cited as Layer 1 enforcement instruments:**
  - Akta Kesalahan-Kesalahan Seksual terhadap Kanak-Kanak 2017 (Sexual Offences Against Children Act) — covers AI-generated child sexual exploitation material
  - Akta Keselamatan Dalam Talian 2025 (Online Safety Act / ONSA) — mechanism for harmful/illegal content on online platforms
  - Akta Komunikasi dan Multimedia 1998 (CMA) — addresses spread of harmful content including AI-generated/manipulated content

  Both bills (RUU Jenayah Siber + RUU Tadbir Urus AI) are scheduled for the current parliamentary session.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC (Unit 3 / Anti-Deepfake Campaign) — Incremental Update

1. **The Parliament research note is the single most valuable document for CSCDC's campaign narrative.** The e-PRS paper (F1) is Parliament's own assessment of the deepfake threat — citing it gives CSCDC's campaign unmatched institutional authority. The "Liar's Dividend" concept, the election-integrity framing, and the four analytical themes (Trust Exploitation, Sovereign AI Cloud, Safety by Design, Detection AI) are ready-made campaign messaging frameworks. The RM5.62B (2023-2025) figure adds a cumulative loss narrative alongside MIMOS's annual figures.

2. **Categorised MCMC takedown data enables targeted campaign messaging.** For the first time, CSCDC has breakdown by content type: 3,074 deepfake-specific takedown requests (77% success), 55,218 fake news (85%), 697,061 total harmful content (92%). The 58,104 residual posts still accessible is a powerful "the threat is real and ongoing" data point. The 77% deepfake success rate (lower than fake news 85% and overall 92%) can frame messaging: "deepfakes are harder to detect and remove — that's why you need to know how to spot them."

3. **The two-layer enforcement architecture provides campaign structure.** F3's two-layer framework (prosecute + prevent) maps directly onto a two-pronged campaign: (a) "Deepfakes are now a crime — penalties up to RM 500K / 7 years" (deterrence), and (b) "Here's how the government is building AI governance to protect you" (reassurance). This is a more sophisticated campaign structure than prior cycles supported.

4. **The Committee on Combating Cybercrimes is a new inter-agency coordination body CSCDC should track.** Chaired by the Chief Secretary to the Government, with NACSA CEO as Secretary — this is the highest-level cyber coordination body in Malaysia. CSCDC's campaign should align with this Committee's priorities; the Committee itself could be a natural advocacy target for CSCDC's anti-deepfake campaign budget.

5. **CSM's merger into the Cybersecurity & Cryptology Development Centre changes the detection-vendor landscape.** CSCDC's prior assumption that CSM+UKM is a standalone detection tool may need updating — CSM is now part of a merged entity with cryptology capabilities. The proposed National AI Detection Hub (F1) could be housed in this merged centre. CSCDC should track this institutional evolution for any content-studio detection-technology partnerships.

6. **Platform resistance to ONSA (X, Grok refusing to comply) is a campaign vulnerability and opportunity.** The Parliament note documents that ONSA's auto-labelling mandate is being tested by platform non-compliance. Campaign messaging can frame this as "you can't rely on platforms to label AI content — you need to develop your own detection skills," which supports CSCDC's awareness mission.

7. **No change to the core strategic posture:** anti-deepfake remains an unfilled branded niche within a robust but general online-safety ecosystem; in-house Content Studio is directionally supported by Putrajaya's in-house mandate; the hub-and-spoke model (in-house core + agency peaks + Aras Integrasi methodology) remains the working recommendation. The legal and institutional architecture has thickened further but does not change the build-vs-buy direction.
