### Finding 1: JURUS Digital Credential — 3-Tier Badge-Based Cybersecurity Competency Pathway by MCCO/rawSEC + USM CYRES (NEW — CRITICAL)
- **Source:** https://jurus.mcco.org.my/ ; NCSS 2026 programme (https://cydes.my/) — "JURUS: Award the Pilot Graduates" (Day 1, 2:45 PM, 7 July 2026)
- **Date:** Pilot graduates awarded at NCSS 2026 on 7 July 2026; programme active through July 2026
- **Summary:** JURUS ("Jurutera Siber RawSEC" — RawSEC Cyber Engineer) is a capability-based digital credential programme operated by MCCO/rawSEC in **strategic partnership with the Cybersecurity Research Centre (CYRES), Universiti Sains Malaysia (USM)**. It builds a structured cybersecurity competency pathway with **publicly verifiable UUID-based digital badges**. The programme has **3 competency tiers**: T1 Analyst (Pengendali Asas / Basic Operator), T2 Professional (Jurutera Integrasi / Integration Engineer), T3 Specialist (Arkitek Ketahanan / Resilience Architect). The **national pilot enrolled 100 participants**. Philosophy: "Learn by building, prove by doing" — engineering-focused (system hardening, recovery, resilience) rather than exploitation-only. Learning model: **70% practical, 20% mentoring, 10% theory**. Six modules: System Engineering, Network & Security, Database Security, Application Security, Security Monitoring, Business Continuity. Assessment includes live-demo walkthroughs, evidence-based checklists, hardening audits + log review, oral presentations, and MTD/MTO measurement in business continuity. Graduates were formally commissioned at NCSS 2026. **This is the most directly relevant existing domestic model for a Community Champions certification/accreditation framework** — badge-based, tiered, university-partnered, industry-aligned, and publicly verifiable.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-007 (certification/accredification — badge-based 3-tier credential with public verification), PIR-OPP006-004 (training delivery — 70-20-10 practical model), PIR-OPP006-006 (curriculum content — 6 technical modules), PIR-OPP006-009 (measurement/KPIs — capability-based assessment, MTD/MTO metrics), PIR-OPP006-005 (partners — MCCO/rawSEC + USM CYRES), PIR-OPP006-003 (selection — 100 pilot participants, structured progression), PIR-CSCDC-008 (programme structure — 3-tier competency model), PIR-INIT-CSCDC-008 (curriculum design — existing structured curriculum + assessment framework as reference model)

### Finding 2: UK CyberFirst → TechFirst Transition — £187M Government Investment, 4 Sub-Programmes Including TechLocal Community Seed Funding (NEW — MAJOR INTERNATIONAL MODEL UPDATE)

- **Summary:** The UK's flagship CyberFirst programme (NCSC) is transitioning to the **TechFirst programme** under the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT), backed by **£187 million** government investment. Four sub-programmes: (1) **TechYouth** — £24M, targeting 1 million secondary school pupils by 2028 via school-based learning and online platforms (builds on CyberFirst Girls Competition + CyberFirst Explorers); (2) **TechGrad** — £96.8M, supporting 1,000 undergraduates/year in AI, Cyber, Quantum, CS (builds on CyberFirst Bursary); (3) **TechExpert** — £48.4M, £10k grants to 500 PhD students/year; (4) **TechLocal** — £18M, **community-level seed funding where regional panels select local providers to deliver training and outreach**. Focus areas: AI, Cyber, Quantum, Computer Science. Emphasis on diversity, social mobility, equitable access. **TechLocal is the most directly relevant element for CSCDC** — a decentralised community-funding model where regional panels select local training providers, ensuring equitable access across geographies. This is a scalable, locally-adaptive model for deploying 1,000 community champions across Malaysia's diverse regions. Available reports: CyberFirst Social Value Report 2026 (2.57 MB), CyberFirst Annual Report 2025-26 (3.59 MB), CyberFirst Pipeline Diagram (KS2 through KS5).

### Finding 4: Australian ASD Cyber Security Partnership Program — 3-Level Partnership Model (NEW — FILLS PRIOR CYCLE'S AUSTRALIAN GAP)
- **Source:** https://www.cyber.gov.au/asds-cyber-security-partnership-program (updated 26 June 2026); https://www.cyber.gov.au/learn-basics
- **Date:** Updated 26 June 2026
- **Summary:** Australia's ASD operates a **3-level Cyber Security Partnership Program**: (1) **Individuals** — anyone with interest in cybersecurity can sign up for the ACSC Alert Service (real-time threat info); (2) **Business Partners** — Australian businesses with ABN receive bi-monthly newsletter, alert service, and event invitations; (3) **Network Partners** — professionals from government, industry, universities, research organisations, and critical infrastructure, requiring a signed confidentiality agreement. Network Partners receive threat intelligence/IoCs, collaboration forums (sector-specific and cross-sector), training/workshops, resilience-building support, and access to physical offices (Adelaide, Canberra, Brisbane, Melbourne, Perth, Sydney) plus virtual outreach (Darwin, Hobart). ASD also provides public "Learn the Basics" resources: 6 core actions (update devices, backups, MFA, passphrases, recognise scams, watch for threats), a cyber security quiz, multi-language posters (English, Hindi, Chinese, Arabic, Indonesian), and a free Alert Service. **Australia does not operate a specific "community champion" or volunteer-ambassador programme** — it uses a structured partnership/network model with individual, business, and network tiers. This is a distinct model: institutional-partnership-based rather than individual-champion-based.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-010 (Australian model — fills prior cycle gap), PIR-OPP006-005 (partnership model — academic/research/not-for-profit institutions eligible), PIR-OPP006-004 (virtual outreach for remote regions — Darwin, Hobart), PIR-OPP006-009 (tiered engagement — individual/business/network with different benefit levels)

## Analytical Assessment & Implications for Aras Integrasi

1. **JURUS is the single most important new intelligence this cycle.** It is the closest existing domestic model to what a CSCDC Community Champions certification framework could look like: badge-based, 3-tier (Analyst→Professional→Specialist), publicly verifiable (UUID), university-partnered (USM CYRES), industry-aligned, 70-20-10 practical learning, evidence-based assessment. While JURUS is technical-engineering-focused (not community-awareness), its *structural design* — tiered badges, public verification, competency-based progression, university partnership, pilot-then-scale (100→national) — is directly transferable. **Aras Integrasi should study JURUS as a reference architecture for the Community Champions credentialing model and engage MCCO/rawSEC + USM CYRES as potential curriculum/credentialing partners.**

2. **UK TechFirst TechLocal is the best international funding/delivery model for scaling to 1,000.** The £18M TechLocal model — community-level seed funding where regional panels select local providers — is a decentralised, locally-adaptive approach that maps well to Malaysia's federal-state structure and the Sarawak rural-engagement precedent. CSCDC could adapt this: allocate seed funding to state/regional panels that select and fund local champion-training providers, ensuring geographic equity (Peninsular, Sabah, Sarawak).

3. **Singapore's NSSE adds a novel experiential-delivery option.** The National Simulated Scams Exercise — exposing participants to actual scam tactics in a controlled environment — is a high-impact, memorable training method that could be adapted for champion-led community workshops. Combined with Singapore's 6-audience segmentation model, this gives CSCDC a clear audience-targeting framework.

4. **Australia's gap is now explained, not just absent.** Australia uses an institutional-partnership model (Individuals/Business/Network Partners) rather than an individual-champion/ambassador model. This is a deliberate design choice — it trades grassroots reach for institutional depth. CSCDC should note this as a comparator but the UK and Singapore models remain more directly applicable to a community-champions approach.

5. **The 1,000 Community Champions programme remains pre-launch — now confirmed across two cycles.** The absence is increasingly significant: NCSS 2026 (the flagship venue) has passed, JURUS pilot graduates were awarded there, but no champions programme was announced. This strengthens the assessment that curriculum development is greenfield and Aras Integrasi's positioning as a curriculum/programme-design partner has genuine runway. The policy mandate (MCSS 2025-2030 whole-of-society), partner ecosystem, certification models (JURUS badges, Global ACE, PAJSK), and KPI templates (ACF 75%/50%, JURUS evidence-based) all exist as ready building blocks.

6. **Next-cycle collection priorities:** (a) Monitor for any post-NCSS 2026 follow-on announcements (the summit was 7-9 July; agency follow-up initiatives may emerge in Aug-Sep 2026); (b) Deep-dive JURUS curriculum modules and assessment criteria — request or seek published JURUS badge standards; (c) Track MCCO/rawSEC + USM CYRES engagement with NACSA/CSCDC — is JURUS being positioned as the credentialing vehicle for a broader community programme? (d) Extract UK CyberFirst Annual Report 2025-26 and Social Value Report 2026 for detailed KPI/curriculum data; (e) Continue monitoring for "Juara Siber Komuniti" or "Community Champions" terminology in Malay-language sources.
