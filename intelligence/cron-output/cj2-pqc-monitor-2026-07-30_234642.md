- **Date:** Standards published 13 August 2024; HQC selected 11 March 2025; page updated June 2026
- **Summary:** NIST published three PQC standards on 13 Aug 2024: **FIPS 203** (ML-KEM, formerly CRYSTALS-Kyber — key encapsulation, replaces RSA/ECDH/DH); **FIPS 204** (ML-DSA, formerly CRYSTALS-Dilithium — digital signatures, replaces RSA-PSS/ECDSA); **FIPS 205** (SLH-DSA, formerly SPHINCS+ — hash-based signatures). **FIPS 206** (FN-DSA/FALCON, NTRU lattice) is in development. **HQC** (code-based) was selected for standardisation on 11 March 2025. Additional digital signature schemes are in Round 2. ML-KEM-768 is NIST's primary TLS recommendation; Google Chrome 131 ships X25519MLKEM768 hybrid by default. ### Finding 10: NSA Post-Quantum Position — CNSA 2.0, No QKD
- **Source:** https://www.nsa.gov/Cybersecurity/Post-Quantum-Cybersecurity-Resources/
- **Date:** CNSS Policy 15 released 4 March 2025
- **Summary:** The NSA announced its quantum-resistant algorithm selections via **CNSS Policy 15** (4 March 2025) and the CNSA 2.0 Suite, which mandates ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA for government use with **no classical asymmetric algorithms allowed for new systems**. NSA **does not recommend** Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) or Quantum Cryptography for National Security Systems, citing five technical limitations (partial solution, special-purpose equipment, increased costs/insider risk, validation difficulty, DoS risk). NSA views PQC as "more cost effective and easily maintained" than QKD and does not anticipate certifying QKD products. Full CNSA 2.0 compliance target: 2035.
