## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This cycle captured a **high-density intelligence environment** around Malaysia's anti-deepfake legislative and awareness landscape. The Dewan Rakyat passed the **Cybercrimes Bill 2026** (July 1) with 61 clauses explicitly criminalising deepfake creation/distribution. MCMC is actively enforcing the **Online Safety Act 2025 (ONSA)** against platforms hosting deepfake paid advertising. A **Parliamentary research paper** (March 2026) quantifies RM5.62 billion in deepfake-linked financial fraud losses (2023–2025). At the ASEAN level, Malaysia is leading the drafting of regional deepfake guidelines and championed the **ASEAN AI Safety Network** declaration (Oct 2025). No evidence of a dedicated standalone "anti-deepfake awareness *campaign*" (distinct from general internet-safety programming) was found — indicating CSCDC's RM 80K allocation targets a **genuine white space**. The MCMC's KIS2026 / Safe Internet Campaign (2025–2027) is multilingual (BM/EN/CN/Tamil) and grassroots-focused but is a general digital-literacy programme, not deepfake-specific. **No evidence of external creative agency appointment for cyber/defence campaigns** was found via open sources — the government appears to execute awareness content largely in-house (MCMC/CyberSecurity Malaysia/NACSA) with some private-sector partnership for specific events.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC

1. **WHITE SPACE CONFIRMED:** No dedicated anti-deepfake public awareness campaign exists in Malaysia despite RM5.62B in quantified losses and explicit criminalisation. CSCDC's RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign targets a genuine gap.

2. **MULTILINGUAL PRECEDENT EXISTS:** KIS2026 already produces content in BM/EN/CN/Tamil — CSCDC's multilingual requirement (BM/EN/CN) is feasible and has government precedent. CSCDC can build on this template.

3. **BUILD VS BUY LEANING TOWARD HYBRID:** Government executes awareness content in-house (MCMC/CSM/NACSA) but social media engagement is extremely weak (TikTok 209 followers). A pure in-house Content Studio (RM 100K) would need significant creative talent investment. A hybrid model — in-house production capacity + creative agency partnership for high-impact viral assets — aligns with government precedent (My Cyber Hero's private-sector partnership model) while addressing the engagement gap.

4. **REGULATORY ALIGNMENT REQUIRED:** Any CSCDC campaign must align with: Cybercrimes Bill 2026 (definitions of deepfake offences), ONSA 2025 (AI content labelling), MCSS 2025–2030 (national strategy), and PDPA (if micro-targeting). The "AI-Generated"/"AI-Enhanced" labelling requirement is a natural hook for awareness content.

5. **ASEAN POSITIONING OPPORTUNITY:** Malaysia is leading ASEAN deepfake guidelines and the ASEAN AI Safety Network. CSCDC's campaign could position as a regional showcase, leveraging Malaysia's ASEAN AI SAFE Lead Proponent status.

6. **ARAS INTEGRASI METHODOLOGY ROLE VALIDATED:** The government's awareness approach is currently reactive (takedowns, enforcement) rather than preventive (education, literacy). Aras Integrasi can position as the methodology partner that shifts the paradigm from reactive enforcement to preventive education — filling the strategic gap identified in the Parliamentary research paper (Finding 7).
