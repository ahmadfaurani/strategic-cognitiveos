## Executive Summary

This cycle conducted open-source collection against Malaysian national cybersecurity agencies (NACSA, CyberSecurity Malaysia), the Malaysia Cyber Security Strategy (MCSS) 2025-2030, and international community-awareness models (Singapore CSA, UK NCSC). **No public reference to a "CSCDC" designation, a "1,000 Community Champions" programme, or any Community Champions curriculum draft was found across NACSA, CyberSecurity Malaysia, or mainstream search.** This strongly suggests the programme is either (a) an internal/pre-launch concept under design, or (b) a bespoke initiative being scoped by Aras Integrasi with no public footprint yet. Existing Malaysian awareness assets (My Cyber Hero, CyberSAFE, 10 Cyber Safety Measures) provide reusable building blocks. The MCSS 2025-2030 documents a strong public demand signal for nationwide awareness campaigns (64.3% of 3,153 survey respondents) and a perception that current government efforts are insufficient (73.2%) — directly validating the strategic rationale for a Community Champions programme.

## Findings

### Finding 1: My Cyber Hero (MYCH) 2026 — NACSA National Student Cybersecurity Competition
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/my-cyberHero.php
- **Date:** Programme launched 13 April 2026; final round 1 July 2026
- **Summary:** NACSA's flagship youth cybersecurity education initiative, targeting primary (Rookie Cyber Hero, ages 7-12) and secondary (Star Cyber Hero, ages 13-17) school students nationwide. Teams of 3 students + 1 guiding teacher compete via online workshops, a 50-question quiz, and a face-to-face gamified "Defend Your Fort" (DYF) final. **Support partners include the Ministry of Education Malaysia (KPM) and MDEC**, confirming KPM engagement in cybersecurity outreach — directly relevant to PIR-OPP006-005 and the KPM collaboration requirement. Sole sponsor: BlackBerry Technology Sdn Bhd. Co-organizer: Micro Concept Tech Sdn Bhd (MCT). Top-3 schools receive A.I. Robotics kits (RM3,600). This is a competition model (top-16 finalists per category), NOT a scaled 1,000-champion capacity-building model — but it demonstrates the KPM partnership channel, school-team structure, and curriculum modules (computer science theory, networking, cyber intrusion, AI in cybersecurity) that a Community Champions curriculum could reuse.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-002 (target audience — students confirmed as one segment), PIR-OPP006-005 (KPM + MDEC confirmed as existing partners), PIR-OPP006-006 (curriculum content areas: CS theory, networking, intrusion, AI), PIR-OPP006-009 (PAJSK marks as a recognition KPI), PIR-CSCDC-008
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Tag:** [VERIFIED]

### Finding 2: Malaysia Cyber Security Strategy (MCSS) 2025-2030 — Whole-of-Society Demand Signal
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/doc/MCSS%202025-2030.pdf (via NACSA)
- **Date:** Strategy covering 2025-2030; public survey conducted 10 Oct – 10 Nov 2025
- **Summary:** NACSA's national strategy, themed "Sovereign Cyber Security Resilience: Nurturing Adaptive Digital Society" (ISBN 978-629-92023-0-1), aligns to the Thirteenth Malaysia Plan 2026-2030 and the MADANI Governance Framework. A nationwide public survey (n=3,153) found 84.5% express extreme/high concern about cyber threats, only 59.5% regularly practice good cyber hygiene, and **73.2% believe current government cyber efforts are insufficient**. Public priorities include nationwide awareness campaigns (64.3%), online self-assessment tools for cyber risk (64.3%), and special assistance for vulnerable groups (47.6%). The strategy explicitly calls for a "whole-of-society approach" — a direct strategic mandate for a Community Champions programme. Legislation underpinning the strategy: Cyber Security Act 2024 [Act 854], Online Safety Act 2025 [Act 866], Data Sharing Act 2025 [Act 864]. Digital economy target: 25.5% of GDP by 2025; 500,000 new digital jobs by 2030.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-008 (programme funding — embedded in 13th Malaysia Plan national strategy), PIR-OPP006-009 (KPIs — public concern/hygiene baselines established), PIR-CSCDC-008, PIR-OPP009 series (national strategy context for G2G)
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Tag:** [VERIFIED]

### Finding 3: Cybercrime Escalation Context — The Quantified Need for Champions
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/doc/MCSS%202025-2030.pdf
- **Date:** Statistics through Oct 2025
- **Summary:** MCSS documents a 289% increase in cybercrime cases (25,479 in 2022 → 73,735 in 2025) with losses rising from RM851M to RM2.459B. Telecommunications fraud alone rose from 7,732 to 31,515 cases (RM321M → RM876M); non-existent investment fraud losses surged to RM1.25B. 5,837 cyber incidents recorded Jan–Dec 2025 affecting 2,390 NCII and 3,447 non-NCII entities. This quantified escalation is the evidentiary basis for scaling community-level cyber awareness — champions could directly address the fraud/scam vectors (the #1 public concern at 97.8%) that top-down campaigns alone are not curbing.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-008 (funding rationale), PIR-OPP006-009 (effectiveness measurement baseline), PIR-CSCDC-008
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Tag:** [VERIFIED]

### Finding 4: CyberSAFE® Program & CyberGuru — CyberSecurity Malaysia's Existing Awareness & Certification Stack
- **Source:** https://www.cybersecurity.my/portal-main/article/public ; https://www.cybersecurity.my/portal-main/article/academia

On the certification side, **CyberGuru** provides competency & certification training and the **Global ACE Certification** framework offers structured credentialing (via globalace.org). These constitute a reusable content + certification foundation that a Community Champions curriculum could build on rather than starting entirely from scratch — partially addressing PIR-OPP006-001/PIR-INIT-CSCDC-008. CyberGuru + Global ACE provide an accreditation pathway (PIR-OPP006-007).

### Finding 5: NACSA "10 Cyber Safety Measures" — Baseline Public Awareness Curriculum (Since 2017)
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/individuals.php
- **Date:** Launched October 2017; still active August 2026
- **Summary:** NACSA's standing public-sector awareness program built around 10 core measures: unique passwords, updated security software, information protection/backup, cyber-environment awareness, internet ethics, beware cyber criminals, think-before-you-click, lodge a report, stay alert, comply with policies. Available in Bahasa Melayu PDF. This is a simple, pre-existing modular curriculum (10 topics) that maps cleanly onto a Community Champions training scaffold. Notably, the content is public-sector-oriented and basic — a champions programme would need to extend depth (e.g., add incident reporting workflows, scam-recognition, vulnerable-group support) per PIR-OPP006-006.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-001 (existing draft/modules exist), PIR-OPP006-006 (curriculum content areas — 10 modules identified), PIR-INIT-CSCDC-008, PIR-CSCDC-008
- **Confidence:** HIGH

### Finding 8: Singapore CSA — Audience-Segmented Community Outreach Model (International Benchmark)
- **Source:** https://www.csa.gov.sg/
- **Date:** Active August 2026
- **Summary:** Singapore's Cyber Security Agency segments all outreach by six audiences: CII Sector, Enterprises, Security & IT Professionals, General Public, **Seniors**, and **Students**. Notable community-delivery mechanisms: **"Be Cyber Safe Workshop for Seniors" at Fernvale Community Club** (partnered with Active Ageing Committee, CPF Board, National Library Board, UOB) — a community-venue, partner-co-delivered model directly analogous to a "community champion" approach. Also runs the **National Simulated Scams Exercise (NSSE)**, an opt-in experiential scam exercise. Tools: Internet Hygiene Portal, Password Checker, Cybersecurity Health Check. For Malaysia's Community Champions, Singapore's seniors-at-community-clubs model is the closest international analogue for a venue-based, partner-co-delivered awareness format.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP006-010 (international model — Singapore), PIR-OPP006-004 (delivery model — in-person at community venues, hybrid online tools), PIR-OPP006-002 (audience segmentation incl. seniors)
- **Confidence:** HIGH

### Finding 9: UK NCSC — Audience-Targeted Guidance & Cyber Essentials Certification Model (International Benchmark)
- **Source:** https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/
- **Date:** Active August 2026
- **Summary:** The UK NCSC ("making the UK the safest place to live and work online") structures guidance by audience: You & your family, SMEs, large organisations, public sector, cyber security professionals, self-employed/sole traders. Key transferable models: **Cyber Essentials** (baseline certification for organisations — a lightweight accreditation analogue for champion certification), the **Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF)** for essential-function organisations, the **Early Warning** free malicious-activity notification service for UK organisations, and Frontier AI guidance. The NCSC does not run a named "community champion" volunteer corps but achieves community reach via segmented guidance + the Cyber Essentials certification brand — a model where champions could be credentialed against a simple baseline rather than a heavyweight curriculum.

## Analytic Notes

1. **The "CSCDC" label is non-public.** Neither NACSA nor CyberSecurity Malaysia uses "CSCDC" as a programme, centre, or unit designation on their public portals as of 1 Aug 2026. This label likely originates from the Aras Integrasi/internal programme-scoping domain. Any external intelligence collection will not surface "CSCDC" by name — collection must instead track NACSA + CyberSecurity Malaysia awareness initiatives and infer mapping.

2. **Aras Integrasi positioning.** No public evidence of an Aras Integrasi contract or partnership with NACSA/CyberSecurity Malaysia for curriculum design was found. The capability-partner positioning appears to be a pre-contract / business-development posture. The strongest entry points for Aras Integrasi are: (a) extending MYCH content into a non-competitive champion-training format, (b) repackaging the 10 Cyber Safety Measures into a deeper champion curriculum, (c) building a certification layer on Global ACE/CyberGuru.

3. **KPT gap.** KPM is confirmed as a My Cyber Hero support partner, but KPT (Ministry of Higher Education) has no confirmed cybersecurity-awareness partnership in the sources reviewed. If the champions programme targets tertiary students or academics, KPT engagement is an open requirement.

4. **Collection limitation.** The web search backend returned predominantly generic/non-relevant results for specialised queries (returning country Wikipedia/tourism pages or unrelated commercial sites). High-value intelligence this cycle came almost entirely from **direct extraction of authoritative government portals** (nacsa.gov.my, cybersecurity.my, csa.gov.sg, ncsc.gov.uk) rather than search. Future cycles should prioritise direct portal extraction and monitor NACSA/CyberSecurity Malaysia news, media releases, and events pages for any programme launch announcements.

5. **Watch triggers for next cycle:** any announcement containing "community champions", "jurujaga siber komuniti", "1,000 champions", "CSCDC", new NACSA/CyberSecurity Malaysia awareness programme launches, KPT cybersecurity MoUs, or Aras Integrasi public mentions.
