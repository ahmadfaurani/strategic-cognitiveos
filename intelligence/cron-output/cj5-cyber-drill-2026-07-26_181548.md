### Finding 4: Malaysia Cyber Security Act 2024 (Act 854) supersedes Arahan MKN No. 26 — current legal framework for national cyber crisis management
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/
- **Date:** Act gazetted 26 June 2024; NACSA–MKN joint statement 22 March 2024
- **Summary:** The **Cyber Security Act 2024 (Act 854)**, gazetted by the Attorney General's Chambers on 26 June 2024, is Malaysia's current primary cyber security legislation. It **supersedes/cancels NSC Directive No. 26 (Arahan MKN No. 26) on "Pengurusan Keselamatan Siber Negara" (National Cyber Security Management)**. This is the operative answer to the "Arahan MKN frameworks" question — the legacy directive governing national cyber security management was **Arahan MKN No. 26**, now replaced by statute. NACSA and MKN issued a **joint media statement on 22 March 2024** formalising the transition. The Act establishes licensing for cyber security service providers (managed SOC monitoring + penetration testing) under the Chief Executive of NACSA.

## ANALYSIS — KEY IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC

1. **Drill design capability exists domestically.** CyberSecurity Malaysia has demonstrable EXCON experience (APCERT Drill 2025) and sits on the APCERT Steering Committee. The RM 200K National Cyber Drill need **not** default to external consultancy — an **inter-agency collaboration model (NACSA policy + CSM/MyCERT EXCON + MKN coordination)** is both feasible and precedented. This bears directly on PIR-OPP002-001 (Playbook allocation) and PIR-OPP007-003 (drill facilitator).

2. **Legal framework has just modernised.** Arahan MKN No. 26 (the legacy directive for national cyber security management) was **superseded by Act 854 (Cyber Security Act 2024)**. Any Playbook or drill protocol must be built on **Act 854**, not the old directive. This is a critical currency point for the RM 150K Playbook.

3. **A national exercise precedent exists but is not a standing programme.** NC4 2022 ran under MKN, but its microsite is defunct and no NC5 follow-on was found — suggesting national cyber crisis drills are **ad hoc, not institutionalised on a recurring cadence**. CSCDC's RM 200K drill could either revive/regularise the NC series or align to APCERT's annual cycle.

4. **War Room physical space exists within the architecture.** MKN's **Pusat Pengoperasian Operasi Negara** (Situation Room, Putrajaya) and NACSA's colocated role under MKN provide an existing physical coordination node. CSCDC's War Room Komunikasi can plug into this rather than build greenfield — though a CSCDC-dedicated space was **not** confirmed in open sources (PIR-OPP002-003 remains PARTIAL).

5. **Integrated-scope is the international standard.** Locked Shields (4,000 pax / 41 nations, technical+operational+strategic+legal+comms) and Crossed Swords (240 pax / 40 nations, offensive+defensive+CNI) confirm the global norm is **integrated**, not communication-only or technical-only. CSCDC's drill should be **integrated**; the RM 150K Playbook is the *communication* track of that integrated whole.

6. **Budget reality check (RM 200K).** Locked Shields-scale live-fire exercises cost orders of magnitude more (4,000 participants, multinational infrastructure). **RM 200K is realistic for a national tabletop/virtual exercise (APCERT's 2025 drill was virtual)** but **not** for a full live-fire national drill. CSCDC should scope RM 200K toward a **tabletop/virtual integrated exercise** and treat live-fire as a future, larger-budget phase.

7. **Crisis-communication cadence is already running.** MKN Komunikasi Strategik publishes cyber content weekly (verified in-cycle, 26 July 2026). The holding-statement bank (PIR-OPP002-006) can be built on top of MyCERT's 200-advisory/year output and MKN's existing strategic-communication machinery — not from zero.
