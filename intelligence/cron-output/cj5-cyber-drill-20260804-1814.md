# CJ5 — National Cyber Drill & Crisis Protocol Monitor Report

**Collection Cycle:** 2026-08-04 18:14 (+08 Asia/Kuala_Lumpur)
**Agent:** National Cyber Drill & Crisis Protocol Monitor (CSCDC Intelligence System)
**Scope:** National cyber drill exercises, crisis communication protocols, War Room methodology benchmarks relevant to CSCDC Unit 4 crisis response capability.

---

## Executive Summary

This cycle surfaced significant, verifiable intelligence on Malaysia's national cyber crisis management architecture and international cyber drill benchmarks. Three structural pillars underpin Malaysia's framework: (1) **NSC Directive No. 24** (Arahan MKN No. 24 — National Cyber Crisis Management Policy and Mechanism, launched Nov 2013), now operationalised through the **Cyber Security Act 2024 [Act 854]**; (2) the **NCCMP (National Cyber Crisis Management Plan)** detailing detection/response/communication/coordination procedures across CNII sectors; and (3) **NC4 (National Cyber Coordination and Command Centre)**, a physical command centre located in the Perdana Putra Building, Putrajaya, established under Act 854. The **X-MAYA** national cyber crisis exercise series (held 2008–2017, editions 1–6) is the established national drill, jointly run by the National Security Council (MKN) and CyberSecurity Malaysia. Internationally, the **ASEAN CERT Incident Drill (ACID)** reached its 20th iteration in October 2025 (first physical edition), and **NATO Locked Shields 2025** engaged 4,000+ participants from 41 nations. A Malaysia-specific crisis communication "30-Minute Rule" (internal alert → fact gathering → holding statement within 30 minutes) directly validates CSCDC's 30-minute holding statement KPI. RM 200K for a National Cyber Drill is consistent with organisational-level CyberSecurity Malaysia drills but is materially below the scale of a full national X-MAYA exercise.

- **Source:** https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2481253 ; https://www.sicw.gov.sg/events/21-oct/asean-cert-incident-drill-acid/
- **Date:** 21–22 October 2025
- **Summary:** The 20th ASEAN CERT Incident Drill (ACID) was held physically for the first time at the ASEAN-Singapore Cybersecurity Centre of Excellence, in conjunction with Singapore International Cyber Week 2025. It brought together 36 incident responders from 10 ASEAN member states, 5 dialogue partners, and Timor-Leste. Dr Megat Zuhairy Megat Tajuddin — CEO of Malaysia's NACSA and ASEAN Regional CERT Task Force overall coordinator — highlighted the milestone.

### Finding 2: NATO Locked Shields — World's Largest Live-Fire Cyber Defence Exercise (2025/2026)

- **Date:** Locked Shields 2025 (April 2025); Locked Shields 2026 (current iteration)

### Finding 3: National Cyber Crisis Management Plan (NCCMP) — NACSA
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/nccmp.php
- **Date:** Ongoing (page last updated 4 August 2026)
- **Summary:** The NCCMP was developed by NACSA to provide procedures at the national level for detection, response, **communication, and coordination** in the event of cyberattack for protection of the Critical National Information Infrastructure (CNII). It covers 10 critical domains: defence and security; banking and finance; information and communications; energy; transportation; water; health; government services; emergency services; and food and agriculture. The NCCMP provides detailed steps for all parties involved in national cyber crisis management and serves as the main reference for Sector Leads and CNII agencies/organisations in developing and maintaining their own Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). NACSA is located within the National Security Council, Prime Minister's Department, Perdana Putra Building, Putrajaya. **Relevance to CSCDC:** The NCCMP already defines a "communication and coordination" layer that a Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook must interface with — the RM 150K playbook is likely an enhancement/sector-specific overlay rather than a greenfield build.

### Finding 4: NC4 — National Cyber Coordination and Command Centre (Cyber Security Act 2024)
- **Source:** https://www.nc4.gov.my/about-us ; https://www.nc4.gov.my/contact-us
- **Date:** Act 854 in operation 26 August 2024; NC4 portal active 2026
- **Summary:** Per the Cyber Security Act 2024 [Act 854], NC4 is a centre established for cyber crisis management, including monitoring cyber threats to the country's critical systems, ensuring readiness/response/mitigation at strategic and tactical levels. **Peace-time roles:** (1) monitor national cyber security situation; (2) measure readiness level; (3) issue early warnings/advisories to NCII agencies; (4) inform the National Cyber Threat Level to the **National Cyber Crisis Management Committee** and the **National Cyber Crisis Management Working Group**; (5) plan and improve the NCCMP and response/communication/coordination procedures. **Crisis-time roles:** (1) coordinate/manage national-level cyber crisis; (2) report to stakeholders consistently until threat returns to Level 1 (Low); (3) declare Medium Threat Level; (4) provide info security experts; (5) act as technical advisor to the National Cyber Crisis Management Committee and Working Group. **Physical location:** Level LG & G, West Wing, Perdana Putra Building, Federal Government Administrative Center, 62502 Putrajaya. NC4 also publishes real-time alerts (e.g., NC4-ALR-2026-000005). **Relevance to CSCDC:** A physical national command centre already exists; CSCDC's "War Room Komunikasi" would either co-locate with or coordinate into NC4 rather than build standalone infrastructure.

### Finding 5: Cyber Security Act 2024 [Act 854] — Statutory Framework
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/act854.php ; https://www.nacsa.gov.my/index.php/act854.php
- **Date:** Gazetted 26 June 2024; in operation 26 August 2024
- **Summary:** The Cyber Security Act 2024 was gazetted by the Attorney General's Chambers on 26 June 2024 and came into operation on 26 August 2024. It is a major milestone strengthening Malaysia's cyber defenses. Key features: establishes the **National Cyber Security Committee**; outlines duties/powers of the Chief Executive of NACSA; defines functions/duties of NCII sector leads and NCII entities; addresses management of cyber security threats and incidents related to NCII; regulates cybersecurity service providers through licensing. The Act **repealed NSC Directive No. 26 (National Cyber Security Management)**, which was superseded by Act 854. (Note: NSC Directive No. 24 — the National Cyber Crisis Management Policy and Mechanism — remains the operative crisis-response directive.) Under Act 854, NACSA gained statutory powers for the first time, including authority to direct audits and risk assessments, mandate incident reporting, and designate NCII sectors. Eleven CNII sectors are now covered (expanded from 10 to include "trade and industry").

### Finding 6: X-MAYA — National Cyber Crisis Exercise Series
- **Source:** https://www.flickr.com/photos/nacsamalaysia/albums/72157669744292968 ; https://www.digitalnewsasia.com/security/malaysian-government-formulates-national-cybercrisis-policy?page=2 ; https://www.researchpublish.com/upload/book/paperpdf-1610446467.pdf
- **Date:** X-MAYA editions held 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2013 (X-MAYA 5), 2017 (X-MAYA 6)
- **Summary:** X-MAYA is Malaysia's National Cyber Crisis Exercise — a simulated, coordinated cyber drill jointly run by the **National Security Council (MKN/NSC)** and **CyberSecurity Malaysia**. First held in 2008, it assesses the cyber security emergency readiness of Malaysia's CNII and tests the effectiveness of SOPs under **NSC Directive No. 24: The National Cyber Crisis Management Policy and Mechanism**. X-MAYA 5 (2013) had 98 public and private agencies across 10 CNIIs; X-MAYA 6 (2017, held March 6–7) had 108 teams from 91 CNII agencies, with closing ceremony at Putrajaya International Convention Centre (PICC) officiated by the Minister in the PM's Department and attended by 800 guests. The exercise comprises drill day, debrief session, and closing ceremony. An academic post-assessment framework based on the **Kirkpatrick Training Model** has been applied to evaluate X-MAYA 5 participants. **Note:** No publicly verifiable evidence of X-MAYA editions after 2017 was found in this cycle — the gap may reflect classification, naming changes, or absorption into Act 854 processes. **Relevance to CSCDC:** X-MAYA is the established national drill brand; the RM 200K National Cyber Drill may either revive/extend X-MAYA or run as a parallel CSCDC-specific exercise.

### Finding 7: NSC Directive No. 24 — National Cyber Crisis Management Policy and Mechanism
- **Source:** https://www.digitalnewsasia.com/security/malaysian-government-formulates-national-cybercrisis-policy?page=2
- **Date:** Launched 27 November 2013 by Deputy Prime Minister Muhyiddin Yassin
- **Summary:** Arahan MKN No. 24 ("Policy and Mechanism of the National Cyber Crisis Management") is the executive directive outlining Malaysia's strategy for cyber crisis mitigation and response through public and private collaboration and coordination. It defines the roles and responsibilities of all CNII agencies. **Six main principles:** (1) a national cyber crisis management structure; (2) national cyber-threat levels; (3) Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT); (4) cyber security protection mechanisms; (5) **response, communication and coordination procedures**; and (6) a readiness programme. It was launched at the closing of X-MAYA 5 (2013). CyberSecurity Malaysia provided technical support, leveraging experience organising cyber drills for OIC-CERT and APCERT. This is the foundational "Arahan MKN" framework for cyber crisis. (Subsequently, NSC Directive No. 26 on National Cyber Security Management was issued and then repealed/superseded by the Cyber Security Act 2024; Directive No. 24 on crisis management remains operative.)
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-002 (MKN existing protocols), PIR-OPP007-002 (Arahan MKN frameworks), PIR-OPP002-007 (coordination procedures), PIR-OPP002-008 (threat levels = activation thresholds)

### Finding 8: Malaysia Crisis Communication "30-Minute Rule" — Fortress Malaysia Framework
- **Source:** https://www.hindustaniexpress.in/building-an-unshakeable-crisis-management-planning-fortress-malaysia/
- **Date:** 30 June 2025
- **Summary:** A Malaysia-focused crisis management planning framework articulates a "30-Minute Rule" communication protocol that **directly matches CSCDC's 30-minute holding statement KPI**: (1) Internal Alert (5 mins) — WhatsApp Group Activation (CEO, SPEAR team, Legal); (2) Fact Gathering (15 mins) — initial assessment form (What, Where, Impact); (3) **Holding Statement (30 mins)** — pre-approved templates in Malay/English/Chinese + review; (4) Regulatory Notification (60 mins) — Bursa, KPDNHEP, PDPA Commissioner (if breach); (5) Stakeholder Cascade (90 mins). It proposes a **SPEAR Framework** for crisis leadership (Strategic/PR-Comms/Execution/Advisory/Response Coordinator) and a **Physical War Room** with secure LAN lines and backup generator, plus pre-drafted holding statements, CEO apology videos, and a dark site. Training cadence: tabletop quarterly, full simulation bi-annual. Post-crisis uses a **BAIK Methodology** (Brief/Audit/Implement/KPI-tracking). **Relevance to CSCDC:** This validates the 30-minute KPI as a Malaysia-recognised standard and provides a transferable SPEAR leadership structure analogous to DAF's political War Room roles. The multi-language (Malay/English/Chinese) template requirement is directly applicable to a national holding statement bank.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-006 (holding statement bank), PIR-OPP002-001 (playbook structure), PIR-INIT-CSCDC-005 (War Room methodology transfer), PIR-OPP002-008 (activation threshold timing), PIR-OPP007-009 (rehearsal frequency)

### Finding 9: National Cyber Security Entities Coordination Architecture (NC4/MyCERT/CDOC/MCMC NSC)
- **Source:** https://www.mcmc.gov.my/en/resources/guidelines/security-trust-governance
- **Date:** Current (MCMC guidelines, ongoing)
- **Summary:** Malaysia's national cyber incident response architecture comprises four entities: (1) **NC4** (National Cyber Coordination and Command Centre) under NACSA — serves as the National Computer Emergency Response Team; (2) **MyCERT** under CyberSecurity Malaysia (CSM); (3) **CDOC** (Cyber Defence Operations Centre) under the Ministry of Defence; and (4) **MCMC NSC** (MCMC Network Security Centre) under MCMC. The architecture protects 10 CNII sectors. MCMC also operates **Sebenarnya.my** (fact-checking platform) and administers the **Online Safety Act 2025 (ONSA)** — relevant to the public communication dimension of cyber crisis. MCMC has issued 9 technical codes covering information/network security, incident management (TC-G015), cloud service provider selection, IoT security, business continuity, and cyber insurance — providing a regulatory layer for sector-specific crisis procedures. NACSA's key partner organisations include CyberSecurity Malaysia (operational arm), MCMC, the **Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM) — commercial crime investigation department**, and Bank Negara Malaysia (financial sector). **Relevance to CSCDC:** The inter-agency coordination map is already defined; a CSCDC War Room would plug into the NC4-led structure with MCMC (communications/misinformation), PDRM (cybercrime/law enforcement), and MKN/NACSA (national coordination) as primary nodes.

### Finding 10: SULIT Security Clearance — Government Personnel Vetting

- **Summary:** The Malaysian Public Service Department (JPA) circular "SULIT 376/Bhg.10/5" (2 May 1979) requires that officers pass security clearance screening (tapisan keselamatan) before being officially appointed to the public service — either before confirmation or after confirmation. - **Source:** https://asean.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/01-ASEAN-Cybersecurity-Cooperation-Paper-2021-2025_final-23-0122.pdf

- **Summary:** The ASEAN Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy (2021–2025) builds on the 2017–2020 strategy with five initiative areas: (1) Advancing Cyber Readiness Cooperation; (2) Strengthening Regional Cyber Policy Coordination; (3) Enhancing Trust in Cyberspace; (4) Regional Capacity Building; and (5) International Cooperation. ## Analytical Notes for CSCDC Planning

1. **RM 150K Playbook (PIR-OPP002-001):** The communication layer is not a greenfield build — NCCMP and NSC Directive No. 24 already define response/communication/coordination procedures. The RM 150K is best allocated to a **sector-specific or CSCDC-tailored enhancement** (holding statement templates, multi-language banks, War Room Komunikasi SOP overlay) developed in **inter-agency collaboration** with NACSA/NCCMP custodians rather than pure external consultancy or pure internal development. External consultancy risk: duplicating existing NCCMP content.

2. **RM 200K National Cyber Drill (PIR-OPP007-008):** Benchmark against X-MAYA 6 (2017): 108 teams, 91 agencies, 2-day drill + debrief + PICC closing with 800 guests. RM 200K is realistic for a **focused, tabletop-heavy, or single/three-sector drill** but is below the likely cost of a full 11-sector national X-MAYA. Recommend scoping the RM 200K drill as an integrated (technical + communication) exercise with a defined subset of CNII sectors, leveraging CSM/MyCERT facilitation.

3. **War Room methodology transfer (PIR-INIT-CSCDC-005):** The SPEAR Framework and 30-Minute Rule provide a validated Malaysian structure. DAF's political war room experience (rapid response, message discipline, holding statements, media management) maps directly onto the PR/Comms Lead and Response Coordinator SPEAR roles. The transfer adds: (a) cyber-specific technical advisory (CDOC/MyCERT integration), (b) multi-language holding statement templates, (c) NC4 threat-level escalation procedures.

4. **Physical War Room (PIR-OPP002-003):** NC4 at Perdana Putra Building, Putrajaya, is the existing national command centre. CSCDC should seek co-location, Memorandum of Understanding, or dedicated session allocation within NC4 rather than building standalone infrastructure — this aligns with Act 854 governance and avoids redundancy.

5. **Drill scope recommendation (PIR-OPP007-001):** International best practice (Locked Shields, ACID) is **integrated** — technical defence + legal + communication + strategic decision-making. CSCDC's RM 200K drill should be integrated (not communication-only), with a tabletop component (cross-border coordination, public communication) and a technical component (incident detection/response), evaluated via a Kirkpatrick-style model consistent with X-MAYA precedent.

6. **Post-2017 X-MAYA gap:** No publicly verifiable evidence of X-MAYA editions after 2017. Possible explanations: (a) classification/SULIT, (b) renaming/restructuring under Act 854, (c) hiatus. CSCDC should clarify the current status of the national drill series with NACSA to avoid duplication or misalignment.
