## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Malaysia's anti-misinformation posture is **legislation-led (ONSA 2025) and enforcement-led**, NOT campaign-led. The dominant MCMC "campaigns" in 2026 are (a) the **under-16 social media age restriction** and (b) **anti-online-scam action via ONSA** — there is **no dedicated anti-deepfake awareness campaign** detected. The existing anti-misinformation infrastructure (Sebenarnya.my fact-check portal, content takedown regime, ONSA penalties up to RM10M) gives CSCDC a ready platform to layer a deepfake-specific campaign onto, but a deepfake-specific creative strategy appears to be **starting largely from scratch**. Government procurement for creative work runs through MCMC's **MSMART** supplier portal (CIPS-certified, open tender/RFQ) — indicating an **outsourcing-friendly pathway** exists, but no content-studio or creative-agency appointment for cyber/deepfake campaigns was found. Politicians (incl. PM Anwar/PH) are already using **TikTok for campaign messaging**, validating the platform for awareness content. No Malaysia-specific deepfake detection vendors or ASEAN-level anti-deepfake initiative surfaced this cycle.

### Finding 1: Online Safety Act 2025 (ONSA) — Malaysia's primary anti-misinformation legislative framework
- **Source:** https://mcmc.gov.my/onsa
- **Date:** In force 2025; enforcement ramped Feb–Jun 2026
- **Summary:** ONSA 2025 imposes duties on licensed online service providers to mitigate harmful content (incl. financial fraud/scams, CSAM, harassment, hate incitement), mandate accessible reporting mechanisms, and protect child users. Non-compliance fines up to **RM10 million** (announced by Comms Minister Fahmi Fadzil, Feb 21 2026). ONSA presented to the Yang di-Pertuan Agong Sultan Ibrahim on Mar 30 2026. This is the legal backbone any CSCDC anti-deepfake campaign must operate within — deepfake content used for fraud falls under ONSA's "financial fraud & scams" priority harmful-content category.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC

1. **Build on existing rails, don't reinvent.** ONSA 2025 + Sebenarnya.my + the takedown regime are live infrastructure. A CSCDC anti-deepfake campaign should position as the *deepfake-specific layer* on top of these, not a parallel effort — this answers PIR-OPP008-001 (largely from scratch on the deepfake creative, but NOT from scratch on infrastructure).
2. **Outsourcing is the path of least resistance** per MCMC's own procurement model (MSMART, CIPS-certified, open tender/RFQ). For PIR-OPP008-002/PIR-OPP004-002: engaging an MSMART-registered creative agency is faster than building in-house production; the RM100K Content Studio allocation may best fund a *lightweight in-house coordination/quality-control* capability rather than full production. Recommend hybrid: outsourced production + in-house editorial/methodology control (aligns with Aras Integrasi's "methodology partner, not creative vendor" positioning).
3. **TikTok is validated and politically safe** for government messaging — use it for the youth-targeting arm of the anti-deepfake campaign.
4. **Regional white space:** No ASEAN anti-deepfake initiative exists — a Malaysian campaign can claim regional thought-leadership and feed ASEAN APSC cyber-security cooperation (PIR-OPP008-007 opportunity).
5. **Priority gaps for next cycle:** (a) Enumerate MSMART-registered creative agencies with cyber/defence portfolio; (b) Re-attempt CyberSecurity Malaysia awareness campaigns via alternate URLs/news; (c) Identify deepfake-detection vendors (local + regional, e.g., Singapore/Thailand); (d) Confirm CSCDC/Unit-3 content-studio build vs buy decision; (e) Itemise agency-level anti-misinfo budgets.
