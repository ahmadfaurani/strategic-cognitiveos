### Finding 1: Malaysia Cyber Security Strategy (MCSS) 2025-2030 Published
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/doc/MCSS%202025-2030.pdf
- **Date:** Published by NACSA / National Security Council, Prime Minister's Department (2025-2030 strategy window; ISBN 978-629-92023-0-1)
- **Summary:** NACSA's MCSS 2025-2030 sets the sovereign national framework for cyber resilience under three principles (Sovereign, Resilient, Adaptive) and a Government-Business-Community (G-B-C) model. It records 5,837 cyber incidents in 2025 (2,390 affecting NCII entities) and documents a cybercrime surge to RM2.459B losses (Jan-Oct 2025), a 289% case increase vs 2022. The strategy aligns with the Thirteenth Malaysia Plan 2026-2030 and explicitly commits to operational readiness, inter-agency coordination, and drill/exercise programs. 84.5% of surveyed citizens expressed high/extreme concern about cyber threats; 73.2% believe government efforts insufficient — underscoring the public-communication gap CSCDC's Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook would address.

### Finding 2: National Cyber Crisis Management Plan (NCCMP) — NACSA's Core Crisis Protocol
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/nccmp.php
- **Date:** Active as of 28 July 2026 (page last updated)
- **Summary:** NACSA maintains the **National Cyber Crisis Management Plan (NCCMP)**, which codifies national-level procedures for **detection, response, communication, and coordination** during cyberattacks against Critical National Information Infrastructure (CNII) across 10 sectors (defence & security; banking & finance; information & communications; energy; transportation; water; health; government; emergency services; food & agriculture). The NCCMP is the primary reference for Sector Leads and CNII agencies to develop/maintain their Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs). This is the closest existing analogue to a "crisis communication protocol" at national level and is the framework a CSCDC playbook would integrate with — strongly suggesting inter-agency collaboration rather than standalone internal development for the RM 150K allocation.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-001, PIR-OPP002-002, PIR-OPP002-007, PIR-OPP007-012, PIR-INIT-CSCDC-006

### Finding 3: MKN (Majlis Keselamatan Negara) — Legal Authority & Directives Framework
- **Source:** https://www.mkn.gov.my/
- **Date:** Active portal, 28 July 2026
- **Summary:** MKN is Malaysia's lead agency coordinating national security policy since 1971, operating under **Arahan MKN No. 1**, which derives from the **Emergency (Essential Powers) Act 1979** (Akta Darurat). MKN coordinates national crisis response and is the parent body of NACSA (both located in the Perdana Putra Building, Putrajaya). The "Arahan MKN" directive series governs national crisis/disaster management mechanisms. The historical cyber-crisis exercise X-Maya was explicitly designed to test **NSC Directive No. 24 (The National Cyber Crisis Management Policy and Mechanism)** — confirming MKN's directive framework is the baseline any national cyber drill must align with. NOTE: Cybersecurity Act 2024 has superseded NSC Directive No. 26, but Directive No. 24 (crisis management mechanism) remains the operative drill-testing instrument per X-Maya documentation.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-002, PIR-OPP007-012, PIR-INIT-CSCDC-006
- **Confidence:** HIGH

### Finding 4: X-Maya National Cyber Crisis Exercise Series — Established National Drill Template
- **Source:** https://www.flickr.com/photos/nacsamalaysia/albums/72157669744292968 ; https://zenodo.org/records/1107786/files/10002012.pdf
- **Date:** X-Maya series ran 2008, 2009, 2010, 2011, 2013, 2017 (X-Maya 6 on 6-7 March 2017)
- **Summary:** X-MAYA is Malaysia's flagship simulated, coordinated **annual cyber drill**, a joint effort between the National Security Council (MKN/NSC) and CyberSecurity Malaysia (CSM), first held in 2008. Its objective: assess CNII emergency readiness against cyberattacks AND test the effectiveness of the SOP for **NSC Directive No. 24: The National Cyber Crisis Management Policy and Mechanism**. Participation scaled rapidly: X-Maya 1 (2008)=11 agencies → X-Maya 5 (2013)=96 agencies → X-Maya 6 (2017)=108 teams from 91 CNII agencies (~800 guests at the Putrajaya International Convention Center closing ceremony). Academic study (Ahmad, Johnson, Storer — Univ. of Glasgow) measured Organisation Cyber Resilience across the 10 CNII sectors using the WEF C-Suite Executive checklist, finding statistically significant differences in resilience among sectors (p<0.05, medium effect). This is the proven integrated (technical + communication + coordination) drill model the CSCDC RM 200K National Cyber Drill should extend or co-brand with.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP007-001, PIR-OPP007-003, PIR-OPP007-004, PIR-OPP007-005, PIR-OPP007-009, PIR-OPP007-010, PIR-OPP002-009, PIR-INIT-CSCDC-006
- **Confidence:** HIGH
- **Tag:** [VERIFIED]

### Finding 7: NATO Locked Shields 2025 & 2026 — International Drill Benchmark
- **Source:** https://ccdcoe.org/exercises/locked-shields/ ; https://ccdcoe.org/news/2025/locked-shields-2025-showcased-nations-commitment-to-defending-cyberspace/ ; https://www.centri.org/blog/posts/locked-shields-2026-nato-cyber-defence-exercise
- **Date:** Locked Shields 2025 (April 2025); Locked Shields 2026 (ongoing; Centri blog dated 01/05/2026)
- **Summary:** Locked Shields is the world's largest/most complex live-fire cyber defence exercise, hosted annually by NATO CCDCOE since 2010. **2025:** 41 nations, 4,000+ participants, 17 multinational Blue Teams defending virtualized national systems (power grids, battle management, satellite, 5G); tested on legal assessment, **communication**, disinformation, forensics, and national-level decision-making under pressure. New 2025 features: cloud infrastructure, quantum computing injects, AI-driven narratives, redesigned collaboration-scoring. **2026:** ~4,000 participants from 41 nations (one source cites 35 countries); Centri contributed bespoke forensics scenarios (CI/CD pipeline compromise, serverless crypto-mining, supply-chain attack, 4G-dongle exfiltration, AI-powered multi-stage payload). Locked Shields is the gold-standard benchmark for an **integrated** (technical + strategic + communication) drill and validates CSCDC's position that the RM 200K National Cyber Drill should be **integrated** scope, not communication-only.

### Finding 8: Inter-Agency Coordination Architecture — NC4 / MyCERT / CDOC / MCMC NSC
- **Source:** https://www.mcmc.gov.my/en/resources/guidelines/security-trust-governance ; https://www.nc4.gov.my/ ; https://www.nacsa.gov.my/
- **Date:** Active, 28 July 2026
- **Summary:** Malaysia's national CERT ecosystem is a four-body layered structure: (1) **NC4** (National Cyber Coordination and Command Centre) under NACSA — serves as the **National CERT** and operates a live public threat portal (nc4.gov.my) with threat-level indicators, advisories, and malware telemetry; (2) **MyCERT** under CyberSecurity Malaysia (CSM); (3) **CDOC** (Cyber Defence Operations Centre) under MINDEF; (4) **MCMC NSC** (MCMC Network Security Centre) under MCMC. MCMC also coordinates with PDRM on cybercrime enforcement (e.g., the jointly launched Cyber Crime Enforcement Course at the Inter-Agency Retreat on Cyber Crimes 2025, Oct 2025). This four-body structure defines the inter-agency coordination protocol that a CSCDC War Room must plug into — NACSA/NC4 is the national lead, with MCMC (communications/regulatory) and PDRM (law enforcement) as coordination partners. MCMC's 9 Technical Codes (under CMA98) and 11 public advisories provide the regulatory/communication backbone.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-007, PIR-INIT-CSCDC-006, PIR-OPP002-002, PIR-OPP002-008
- **Confidence:** HIGH

### Finding 9: Cybersecurity Act 2024 (Act 854) — New Statutory Crisis/Notification Regime
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/ ; https://ncryptmalaysia.com/resources/whitepapers/nacsa-csa-compliance-playbook
- **Date:** Gazetted 26 June 2024; in force
- **Summary:** The Cybersecurity Act 2024 (Act 854) was gazetted by the Attorney General's Chambers on 26 June 2024 and **supersedes NSC Directive No. 26** (The National Cyber Security Management). It introduces structural obligations: NCII designation, incident notification regimes for NCII sectors, and licensing of Cybersecurity Service Providers (CSPs) covering managed-SOC monitoring and penetration testing. An external consultancy (nCrypt Malaysia) has published a "NACSA Cybersecurity Act 2024 Compliance Playbook" — a 22-page, 12-month sequenced programme with board papers, risk-committee briefings, and incident-notification workflows — demonstrating that the **external-consultancy playbook model is an established market product** in Malaysia. This directly evidences that RM 150K for a Cyber Crisis Communication Playbook could plausibly be allocated to external consultancy (analogous to nCrypt's compliance playbook) OR inter-agency collaboration (building on NACSA's NCCMP) — the allocation decision remains a CSCDC internal one.
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP002-001, PIR-OPP002-002, PIR-OPP007-012
- **Confidence:** HIGH

## Analyst Assessment & Recommendations for CSCDC

1. **RM 150K Playbook (PIR-OPP002-001):** The market precedent (nCrypt's Cybersecurity Act 2024 Compliance Playbook) shows external consultancy can deliver a sequenced 12-month playbook at a comparable scope. Recommended model: **hybrid** — external consultancy for framework/templates + inter-agency alignment with NACSA's NCCMP for authoritative protocols. Pure internal development risks reinventing the NCCMP.

2. **RM 200K Drill (PIR-OPP007-008):** Sufficient for a scoped/sectoral **integrated** drill (technical + communication + coordination) via CSM's CyberDrill service or a co-branded X-Maya extension. Insufficient for a full 100-agency national X-Maya-scale exercise. Recommend scoping the drill to a representative subset of CNII sectors with a clear escalation-pathway communication track.

3. **War Room methodology transfer (PIR-INIT-CSCDC-005):** The political→cyber transfer is structurally sound. MKN's Arahan framework and Locked Shields both validate that modern crisis methodology unifies situational awareness, holding statements, inter-agency coordination, and disinformation response — the same core loops as a political war room. DAF's N17/N16/N41 experience maps cleanly onto: rapid statement turnaround (30-min KPI), stakeholder mapping, message discipline under pressure.

4. **CSCDC-specific gaps requiring internal sourcing (not OSINT):** PIR-OPP002-003 (physical War Room space), PIR-OPP002-004 (SULIT-cleared briefers), and PIR-OPP002-006 (holding-statement bank drafting status) cannot be resolved from open sources and require CSCDC internal records.

5. **Positioning opportunity:** Malaysia's leadership of the ASEAN Regional CERT Task Force (NACSA CEO as Overall Coordinator) creates an opening for Aras Integrasi to position CSCDC's methodology as a transferable regional asset — not just a national capability.
