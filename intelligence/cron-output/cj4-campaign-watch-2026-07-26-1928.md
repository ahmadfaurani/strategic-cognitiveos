## Executive Summary

This cycle captured significant new movement in Malaysia's anti-deepfake and anti-misinformation posture. Three legislative milestones dominate the landscape: the **Cybercrimes Bill 2026** (passed Dewan Rakyat 1 Jul 2026), the **Online Safety Act 2025 (Act 866)** in force since 1 Jan 2026, and the **AI Governance Bill** (RUU Tadbir Urus AI) introduced Jun 2026. Operationally, MCMC removed **43,618** pieces of false/scam content in Q1 2026 alone, ASEAN is drafting deepfake-handling guidelines with Malaysia (via MCMC) leading, and a national AI-based deepfake forensic tool is under development by the Digital Ministry with CyberSecurity Malaysia and UKM. For CSCDC's RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign and RM 100K Content Studio decision, the environment is now legislatively mature but **campaign-creative capacity remains fragmented** — no single integrated government anti-deepfake *awareness* campaign was identified; existing efforts (#ThinkTwice, Safe Internet Campaign) are platform- or agency-led. This is a genuine whitespace opportunity for CSCDC.

## Analyst Assessment & Recommendations for CSCDC

1. **Campaign is greenfield at the creative layer.** Malaysia has built a strong *legal/detection* anti-deepfake architecture but no integrated, government-owned, multilingual *public-awareness* anti-deepfake campaign. The closest analogues (#ThinkTwice, Safe Internet Campaign) are scam/general-safety focused and platform- or agency-siloed. CSCDC's RM 80K can establish first-mover ownership of a dedicated anti-deepfake awareness narrative.

2. **Hybrid in-house + agency model is the rational path.** Evidence shows in-house government social-content production is under-resourced (CyberSecurityMY TikTok: 209 followers). RM 100K is sufficient to build a lean in-house Content Studio core (multilingual BM/EN/CN, short-form video, creator partnerships) and retain a specialist agency for campaign spikes. Walk Production's integrated in-house-team model (40 specialists, no freelancers) is a structural template worth benchmarking.

3. **Multilingual scope should consider Tamil.** TikTok #ThinkTwice operates in EN/BM/Mandarin/**Tamil**. CSCDC's stated BM/EN/CN scope may under-serve a reachable Tamil-speaking segment; recommend validating audience data before finalising language matrix.

4. **Creator-led content is the proven mechanic.** Both #ThinkTwice and TikTok's expanded approach rely on local creators translating technical security advice into relatable content. CSCDC's Content Studio should budget for creator partnerships/influencer procurement, not just in-house production.

5. **Compliance gate on paid distribution.** With ONSA 2025 (Act 866) in force and the Section 46A deeming provision active since 1 Jan 2026, platforms with ≥8M Malaysian users are licensed and face compound penalties for deepfake content. Any CSCDC paid-media anti-deepfake campaign must run platform-compliance due diligence (and ensure CSCDC's own AI-generated content is clearly labelled per the emerging ASEAN guideline direction).

6. **Potential partners for methodology/technical layer:** CyberSecurity Malaysia (CyberSAFE, AI detection tool), MIMOS (deepfake crisis analysis), NACSA Cybersecurity & Cryptology Development Centre (AI forensics), UKM. Aras Integrasi's "methodology partner, not creative vendor" positioning is consistent with this ecosystem — the creative layer is genuinely unclaimed.
