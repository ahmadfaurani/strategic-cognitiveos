## Key Analytical Assessments

### 1. HAWK Withdrawal — AI Discovers Cryptographic Vulnerability (PIR-OPP005-001, PIR-OPP005-005, PIR-OPP005-009, PIR-CSCDC-006)

The withdrawal of HAWK from NIST's Additional Digital Signatures process on July 28, 2026, following an AI-discovered vulnerability by Anthropic, is the most significant new development this cycle. Key analytical implications:

- **Algorithm Risk Now Demonstrated, Not Theoretical:** HAWK survived Round 1 (40 candidates) and Round 2 (14 candidates) evaluation over approximately 3 years before an AI model found a vulnerability. This demonstrates that PQC algorithm risk extends beyond the initial selection process — algorithms can be compromised even after years of expert review. Malaysia's PQC Sandbox must incorporate ongoing algorithm risk monitoring and crypto-agility as core design principles.

- **AI-Cryptography Convergence Validated:** The NQAIC (National Quantum and AI Initiative Centre) vision — combining quantum computing, AI, and cryptography — has been empirically validated. An AI model discovered a cryptographic vulnerability that human cryptanalysts had not found during years of review. This is a proof point for the strategic rationale of integrating AI capability with PQC testing. Malaysia's Sovereign AI architecture should consider incorporating AI-assisted cryptanalysis capabilities.

- **Diversification Rationale Strengthened:** NIST's explicit goal of diversifying beyond structured lattices (which underpin ML-KEM and ML-DSA) is now more compelling. With HAWK (a lattice-based signature) withdrawn, 8 candidates remain across 4 non-lattice mathematical categories (isogeny, MPC-in-the-head, multivariate, symmetric). Malaysia's PQC Sandbox should evaluate algorithms from diverse mathematical families to avoid single-point-of-failure risk.

- **Talent Pipeline Implication:** Malaysia needs expertise not only in PQC implementation but in AI-assisted cryptographic analysis. This is a new capability dimension that the PQC talent pipeline (PIR-OPP005-009) must address. The NQAIC initiative may need to incorporate AI-cryptanalysis research alongside quantum computing.

### 2. U.S. PQC Migration Timeline as International Benchmark (PIR-OPP005-002, PIR-OPP005-008)

### 3. NIST Algorithm Portfolio — Complete Picture (PIR-OPP005-001, PIR-OPP005-006)

The NIST PQC standardization landscape is now clearer:

**Finalized Standards (August 2024):**
- FIPS 203: ML-KEM (lattice-based KEM) — replaces RSA/ECDH
- FIPS 204: ML-DSA (lattice-based signature) — replaces RSA/ECDSA
- FIPS 205: SLH-DSA (hash-based signature) — conservative backup

**In Progress:**
- FIPS 206: FN-DSA/FALCON (lattice-based signature, smaller signatures) — draft early 2026, finalization late 2026/early 2027
- HQC (code-based KEM) — draft 2026, finalization 2027 — backup to ML-KEM
- Additional Digital Signatures Round 3: 8 active candidates across 4 non-lattice categories

**Key Observation:** The finalized standards (ML-KEM, ML-DSA) and FALCON are all lattice-based. The Additional Signatures process explicitly seeks non-lattice alternatives for diversification. The HAWK withdrawal (lattice-based) reinforces the need for this diversification. Malaysia's PQC Sandbox should test algorithms from multiple mathematical families, not just the lattice-based FIPS standards.
