## Executive Summary

This cycle produced significant structural intelligence on the Cyber Security Cryptology Development Centre (CSCDC). The CSCDC was formally established on **4 June 2026**, witnessed by PM Anwar Ibrahim, as a **centralised entity unifying PTPKM (Pusat Teknologi dan Pengurusan Kriptologi Malaysia) and CyberSecurity Malaysia (CSM)** under the Prime Minister's Department, regulated by NACSA and the National Security Council. No public-facing draft of a dedicated "1,000 Community Champions" curriculum was located; the programme appears to sit within the broader MCSS whole-of-nation / capacity-development pillar, with CyberSAFE® as the existing awareness vehicle. On ASEAN positioning, Malaysia is **leading the ASEAN Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy 2026–2030** and hosted CYDES 2025 (12 bilateral meetings, 6 university MoUs). International community-champion analogues were documented (Singapore SG Cyber Safe Seniors/Students, UK CyberFirst/Community Champions, Australia CYBER CENTA Cyber Ambassador).

### Finding: MCSS 2025–2030 Approved — Communities Reframed as "Active Collaborators"
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/doc/MCSS%202025-2030.pdf ; https://www.nst.com.my/opinion/columnists/2026/02/1385552/malaysias-cyber-security-transformation-unifying-and-elevating
- **Date:** Feb 2026 (published/finalised); launched at CYDES 2025 (July 2025)
- **Summary:** The Malaysia Cyber Security Strategy (MCSS) 2025–2030 (ISBN 978-629-92023-0-1), authored under NACSA CEO Dr. Megat Zuhairy Megat Tajuddin, carries the theme *"Sovereign Cyber Security Resilience: Nurturing Adaptive Digital Society."* It expands the "cyber security is a shared responsibility" concept and explicitly **reframes communities as active collaborators** in national cyber security efforts, fostering sustained collaboration between government, industry, academia, and the public. The strategy emphasises sovereignty (Malaysians designing/developing/sustaining own capabilities), NC4 strengthening, a National Cryptography Policy (MyKriptografi, published Feb 2026, Quantum-Safe by 2030), and an international-cooperation shift from information-sharing to technology co-development. A NACSA survey of 3,153 participants found 73.2% believe government efforts are insufficient and only 59.5% regularly practise good cyber hygiene — establishing the demand case for community champions. Threat data: 5,837 incidents (2025), cybercrime losses RM2.46B (2025, up 289% in cases since 2022).

## Strategic Assessment (for Aras Integrasi positioning)

1. **Window of opportunity is open.** The CSCDC is newly established (June 2026) and the MCSS 2025–2030 whole-of-nation pillar explicitly invites community collaboration but lacks a published Community Champions curriculum. This is a greenfield capability-partner opening — consistent with the brief that no draft yet exists.
2. **Anchor proposal on MCSS language.** Any curriculum/partner pitch should map directly to MCSS pillars (sovereignty, NC4, MyKriptografi/PQC, international co-development, community-as-collaborator) and the documented demand case (73.2% perceive insufficient government effort; only 59.5% practise cyber hygiene; RM2.46B cybercrime losses).
3. **Leverage existing assets, avoid reinventing.** CyberSAFE®, CyberGuru, Global ACE, CyberSAFE L.I.V.E Galeri, and the ASEAN My Cyber Hero model are the reusable baseline. A champions curriculum should extend, not duplicate, these.
4. **Delivery-model recommendation (evidence-backed).** Hybrid model with face-to-face-first for vulnerable groups (elderly per Senior Cyber Ambassador precedent), school channel (per ASEAN My Cyber Hero/SG Cyber Safe Students), and professional/leader channel (per MCCO/SherpaSec communities).
5. **Partnership channel is proven.** NACSA's CYDES 2025 MoU mechanism (6 universities) is the established route for KPM/KPT and education-institution engagement.
6. **ASEAN dimension is live.** Malaysia leads the ASEAN Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy 2026–2030; CSCDC could deliver community-capacity modules regionally, differentiating against Singapore's ACCP/ASCOE by emphasising community-level (vs official-level) reach.
