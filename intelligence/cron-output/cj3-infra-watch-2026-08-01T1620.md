- **Date:** Act gazetted 26 June 2024; in force 26 August 2024
- **Summary:** The Cyber Security Act 2024 establishes the National Cyber Security Committee (chaired by the PM), defines NCII sector leads and entity obligations, and mandates a licensing regime for cybersecurity service providers (penetration testing and managed SOC monitoring). ### Finding: MyGPKI — Government Public Key Infrastructure Authentication Platform
- **Source:** https://mygpki.gov.my/gpki_portal/ ; https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/my-initiative/cyber-security-and-disaster-response-and-recovery/keselamatan-siber/perkhidmatan-prasarana-kunci-awam-kerajaan-gpki
- **Date:** GPKI implemented since 2002; portal updated 25 September 2025; MyGPKI seminars/trainings held through 2026
- **Summary:** MyGPKI is the Malaysian Government Public Key Infrastructure service managed centrally by MAMPU (now under Jabatan Digital Negara / JDN), complying with the Digital Signature Act 1997 and Digital Signature Regulations 1998. It provides digital certificates (2-year validity) for two purposes: (1) identity authentication and non-repudiation of digital signatures, and (2) encryption/decryption of information. Certificate mediums include soft certificates, roaming certificates, and USB crypto tokens. As of December 2016, 26,074 user certificates and 171 server certificates had been issued. Services include user/server digital certificate management, advisory/consultation, and centralized online PKI operations. MyGPKI actively conducts regional training (Zon Utara May 2026, Sarawak April 2026), confirming it remains the mandated government authentication platform. **The encrypted alert portal must integrate with MyGPKI for authentication.**
- **PIR(s) Addressed:** PIR-OPP003-009, PIR-OPP003-001

### Finding: Official Secrets Act 1972 (Act 88) — Information Classification Levels
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Secrets_Act_1972 ; https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/LOM/EN/Act%2088.pdf
- **Date:** Act 88, commenced 1 October 1972; amended 1984, 1986
- **Summary:** The Official Secrets Act 1972 (Akta Rahsia Rasmi 1972) is the Malaysian statute governing protection of classified government information. Section 2 defines official secrets as any document specified in the Schedule (Cabinet documents, national security/defense/international relations documents) and any other official document classified as 'Top Secret', 'Secret', 'Confidential', or 'Restricted' by a Minister, Menteri Besar/Chief Minister, or designated public officer. Section 2B provides for appointment of officers to classify documents; Section 2C provides for declassification. Penalties for wrongful communication (Section 8) are severe. **The four classification levels under OSA are: Top Secret, Secret, Confidential, and Restricted. Note: "SULIT" is the Malay equivalent of "Confidential." The encrypted alert portal must handle at minimum SULIT/Confidential and potentially Secret/Rahsia level information, requiring OSA-compliant handling and likely hosting on sovereign government infrastructure.**

### Finding: Malaysia National PQC Readiness Roadmap — 2025-2030 Migration Plan
- **Source:** https://postquantum.com/security-pwc/malaysia-nacsa-pqc-readiness-roadmap/ ; https://www.nst.com.my/business/corporate/2025/11/1308127/malaysia-takes-quantum-leap-cybersecurity-readiness
- **Date:** Announced 29 October 2025 by Ministry of Digital and NACSA
- **Summary:** Malaysia became the first Southeast Asian country to publish a structured national Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) transition framework. The roadmap was unveiled at the PKI Consortium's PQC Conference in Kuala Lumpur (600+ participants, 30+ countries). It is currently non-binding (no mandatory deadlines or specific algorithm requirements) and aligns with NIST's standardized PQC algorithms. The roadmap has three pillars: (1) Technical Readiness — migration plan spanning 2025-2030, proof-of-concept deployments, and a **Sandbox initiative** for testing quantum-safe implementations in government systems; (2) Strategic Alignment — policy-to-practice coordination, NIST/EU alignment, public-sector pilot programs, early adoption of hybrid cryptography, and cryptographic agility frameworks; (3) Human Readiness — workforce development with UN agencies, ASEAN counterparts, and academic institutions. NACSA CEO Ir. Dr. Megat Zuhairy emphasized it as a strategic framework, not a regulatory mandate. **For CSCDC: PQC-readiness is not yet mandated but is a clear national strategic direction. The portal should be designed with cryptographic agility to enable future PQC migration. Being PQC-ready from launch would position CSCDC as an early adopter aligned with the national roadmap.**
