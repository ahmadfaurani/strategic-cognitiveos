## Executive Summary

This cycle surfaced significant institutional intelligence on the **Cyber Security Cryptology Development Centre (CSCDC)**, formally established on **4 June 2026** under the Prime Minister's Department, consolidating PTPKM and CyberSecurity Malaysia (CSM). However, **no public information was found on the specific "1,000 Community Champions" programme or the RM 100K curriculum budget** — a critical negative finding suggesting this initiative is at an internal/conceptual stage and not yet publicly announced. Substantial intelligence was gathered on adjacent awareness/community programmes (CyberSAFE, NACSA My Cyber Hero, NICTSeD, MCCO, SherpaSec) that constitute the existing ecosystem a Community Champions programme would build upon, plus Malaysia's leadership of the ASEAN Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy 2026-2030 and bilateral G2G engagement (notably the Malaysia-Singapore cybersecurity roundtable). International community-champion models from Singapore (SG Cyber Youth/Div0), UK (Cyber Champions), and Australia (Cyber Centa Ambassador/ASD ACSC) were profiled as benchmarks.

## Strategic Assessment for Aras Integrasi Positioning

1. **Greenfield curriculum opportunity confirmed:** No public Community Champions curriculum draft exists. The RM 100K curriculum budget is an internal allocation not yet publicly tendered. Aras Integrasi can position as a capability partner to design from scratch, leveraging existing Malaysian building blocks (MYCH modules, CyberSAFE content, NICTSeD discourse format) and international benchmarks (UK Cyber Champions volunteer model, Singapore YCEP progression pathway, Australia intergenerational ambassador model).

2. **Partner ecosystem is mature and accessible:** KPM (MOE) is already an operational partner via NICTSeD and MYCH — the inter-agency collaboration needed for Community Champions is precedent-backed. MCCO (500+ members, JURUS certification pipeline) and SherpaSec (volunteer grassroots) are ready NGO partners. MDEC and BlackBerry demonstrate corporate co-sponsorship precedent.

3. **Hybrid delivery is the dominant model:** MYCH and NICTSeD both use hybrid (online + in-person) delivery. A Community Champions programme should follow this pattern for scalability to 1,000 champions across Malaysia.

4. **Policy justification is strong:** MCSS 2025-2030 survey data (73.2% see government efforts as insufficient; 64.3% want nationwide awareness campaigns; RM2.459B cybercrime losses in 2025) provides robust evidence base for programme funding expansion beyond RM 100K.

5. **CSCDC G2G and ASEAN positioning is active:** CSCDC under PM's Department, with NACSA/NSC operational arms, Malaysia leading ASEAN Cybersecurity Cooperation Strategy 2026-2030, and NCSS/CYDES as engagement platforms — the institutional architecture for cyber diplomacy is operational and expanding. Community Champions could serve as a domestic capacity-building complement to Malaysia's regional human capital growth pillar.
