### Finding 1: MCSS 2025-2030 Formally Establishes CSCDC as National Cyber Security & Cryptology Technical Arm
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/doc/MCSS%202025-2030.pdf
- **Date:** Published 2025 (strategy covering 2025-2030)
- **Summary:** The Malaysia Cyber Security Strategy (MCSS) 2025-2030, published by NACSA under the National Security Council, explicitly lists under Strategic Goal 1 (Enhance Cyber Security Governance and Ecosystem): "Establish cyber security & cryptology technical arm (CSCDC)." This is the strongest public confirmation of CSCDC's institutional mandate. The strategy's vision is "A Secure Nation. An Empowered Future" with core principles Sovereign, Resilient, Adaptive. It aligns with the Thirteenth Malaysia Plan (2026-2030) and MADANI framework. The strategy reports 5,837 cyber security incidents in 2025 and a 289% jump in cybercrime cases (to 73,735) with RM2.46B in losses.

### Finding 2: MCSS Public Survey Identifies Nationwide Awareness Campaigns as Top Citizen Priority (69.7%) and Special Assistance for Vulnerable Groups (69.3%)
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/doc/MCSS%202025-2030.pdf
- **Date:** 2025 (survey of 3,153 respondents)

### Finding 4: NACSA's My Cyber Hero (MYCH) 2026 — National Cybersecurity Education Initiative for Schools with KPM & MDEC Partners
- **Source:** https://www.nacsa.gov.my/my-cyberHero.php
- **Date:** Launched 13 April 2026 (finals 1 July 2026); page last updated 29 July 2026
- **Summary:** My Cyber Hero 2026 is NACSA's national education initiative targeting primary (7-12 yrs) and secondary (13-17 yrs) students. Organized by NACSA with co-organizer Micro Concept Tech (MCT), sole sponsor BlackBerry Technology, and support partners KPM and MDEC. Structure: online workshops + qualification quiz (50 questions, 60 min, modules on CS theory, networking, intrusion, AI in security) → 16 finalist teams per category → in-person finals using "Defend Your Fort" gamification platform. Teams of 3 students + 1 teacher. All participants receive certificates and PAJSK national recognition. Cash prizes (up to RM4,000/team). This demonstrates a hybrid (online + in-person) delivery model and a certification/recognition framework — directly relevant to Community Champions design. Note: ASEAN My Cyber Hero 2025 expanded this from Malaysia-only to ASEAN-wide.

## ANALYSIS: KEY GAPS & IMPLICATIONS FOR ARAS INTEGRASI

1. **No public "1,000 Community Champions" programme found.** The specific CSCDC Community Champions initiative does not appear in any public source. This strongly suggests it is at an early/internal stage. The strongest adjacent building blocks are: ESN module (content framework), MCMC MIV (delivery/scale precedent), My Cyber Hero (hybrid + recognition model), and the Malaysia Cyber Security Academy (accreditation).

2. **Curriculum not starting from scratch.** The ESN module's six pillars (Digital Fluency, Ethics, Health/Well-being, Commerce, Privacy & Security, Rights & Responsibilities) provide a ready-made curriculum skeleton for a school-facing Community Champions track. KPM collaboration is already live via CyberSAFE in Schools. Aras Integrasi's value-add would be adapting/deepening this for community leaders and professionals.

3. **Partner ecosystem is maturing.** CSM-CP (Sept 2025), MCCO (April 2025), and the Malaysia Cyber Security Academy (Q2 2025) are all new institutional channels. MCCO + rawSEC is the most direct NGO partner candidate; MIV/MCMC is the operational delivery precedent; the Academy is the accreditation route.

4. **ASEAN positioning is crowded.** Singapore's ASCCE/ACCP and the ASEAN-Japan AJCCBC already dominate ASEAN cyber capacity building. CSCDC's ASEAN positioning (PIR-OPP009) must find a differentiated niche — likely Malaysia-specific community mobilisation or cryptology/sovereign capability given the "cryptology technical arm" mandate in MCSS.
