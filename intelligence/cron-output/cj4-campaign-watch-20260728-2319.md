## Executive Summary

This cycle produced **high-signal intelligence** across all 12 PIRs. Malaysia's anti-deepfake posture has shifted decisively from policy debate to enacted legislation and institutional build-out in the last 6 months:

- **Cybercrimes Bill 2026** passed Dewan Rakyat on **1 July 2026** (61 clauses, criminalises deepfakes & non-consensual intimate images).
- **Online Safety Act 2025 (Act 866)** in force since **1 Jan 2026** — imposes up to **RM10M** penalties on platforms, with 1-hour removal windows for priority harmful content.
- A new **Cybersecurity and Cryptology Development Centre** was established **3 June 2026**, merging CyberSecurity Malaysia and the Malaysian Cryptology Technology and Management Centre under NACSA — a major institutional consolidation directly relevant to CSCDC's in-house vs outsource decision.
- **NACSA–MCMC–IBM MoU (9 July 2026)** — PM-witnessed, establishes a National Quantum and AI Centre of Excellence (NQAIC) for "Sovereign AI."
- **ASEAN** is drafting social-media guidelines on deepfakes (announced 22 July 2026), with **Malaysia/MCMC as the spearheading lead** — and Malaysia is Lead Proponent of the **ASEAN AI Safety Network** (declared 26 Oct 2025).
- **MIMOS** is publicly making the sovereignty case for *in-house, localised* deepfake detection (foreign tools underperform on Malaysian faces / Bahasa Malaysia).

**Strategic implication for CSCDC:** The institutional direction is strongly toward **in-house/sovereign capability building**, not pure outsourcing. The RM 100K Content Studio + RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign allocation aligns with a **hybrid model**: sovereign/academic technical capability (NACSA/CSM/UKM/MIMOS/IBM) for detection infrastructure, with MCMC-style in-house campaign execution (KIS2026 model) — leaving room for niche creative outsourcing. **No dedicated anti-deepfake campaign strategy is yet evident** — CSCDC would be entering at strategy-development stage, building on the now-enacted legal framework.

## Analytical Assessment — In-House vs Outsource (PIR-OPP008-002 / PIR-OPP004-002)

The weight of evidence this cycle indicates **Malaysia is decisively building in-house/sovereign capability** for the technical detection and infrastructure layer, while campaign *execution* follows an MCMC-style in-house model (KIS2026) with niche outsourcing room:

| Layer | Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Detection tech / AI forensics | **In-house / sovereign** | Digital Ministry AI forensic tool (CSM + UKM); MIMOS localised detection; NQAIC (NACSA-MCMC-IBM); new Cybersecurity & Cryptology Development Centre under NACSA |
| Platform regulation / takedown | **In-house (MCMC)** | ONSA 2025, 78,741 takedown requests, 560 cases resolved 2025 |
| Awareness campaigns | **In-house (MCMC/CyberSecurity Malaysia)** | KIS2026 (MCMC-run, multilingual), CyberSAFE® outreach, TikTok/YouTube channels (currently low engagement) |
| Creative content production | **Hybrid — likely in-house studio + niche outsourcing** | RM 100K Content Studio + RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign budgets; no external creative agency appointment found; Aras Integrasi = methodology partner |

**For CSCDC specifically:** The RM 100K Content Studio allocation suggests an **in-house multilingual production capability** is the intended destination, consistent with the national "Sovereign AI" trajectory. Outsourcing, if any, would most plausibly be for specific creative bursts (e.g., a flagship anti-deepfake campaign video series) to a top-tier agency (Leo Burnett KL / Ogilvy Malaysia) rather than standing creative retainers. **This is a strategic opportunity for CSCDC to position its Content Studio as the multilingual BM/EN/CN production hub within the sovereign capability stack.**
