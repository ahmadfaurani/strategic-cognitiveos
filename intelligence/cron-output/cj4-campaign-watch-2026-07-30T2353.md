## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Malaysia's anti-deepfake posture has crystallised rapidly in 2026. Three concurrent forces are reshaping the landscape CSCDC must operate within: (1) a wave of **new legislation** — the Cybercrimes Bill 2026 (passed Dewan Rakyat 1 July), the Online Safety Act 2025 (ONSA, Act 866) subsidiary instruments, and the AI Governance Bill (RUU Tadbir Urus AI) — that criminalises deepfakes and creates enforcement hooks; (2) a **national awareness apparatus** centred on MCMC's KIS2026 grassroots campaign (multilingual: BM/EN/Mandarin/Tamil) and CyberSecurity Malaysia's CyberSAFE® programme; and (3) a **government capability build-out** including an AI forensic deepfake detection tool (CyberSecurity Malaysia + UKM), a forthcoming Ministry of Digital "Digital Trust Strategy" (due before end-2026), and Budget 2026 allocations (RM32M for cybersecurity/child protection; NACSA Cyber Security & Cryptology Development Centre). No dedicated "anti-deepfake campaign strategy" of the kind CSCDC envisions appears to exist yet — existing efforts are awareness/scam-prevention oriented and fragmented across MCMC, CyberSecurity Malaysia, and NACSA. This signals a **gap CSCDC can fill**, but also a crowded institutional field with no single creative-agency lead appointed. The in-house-vs-outsource decision is unresolved; the Ministry of Communications already operates a Content Development Division, which is the most likely host for in-house multilingual production.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS FOR CSCDC

1. **The anti-deepfake *campaign* is the white space.** Malaysia has invested heavily in *law* (Cybercrimes Bill, ONSA, AI Governance Bill) and *awareness* (KIS2026, CyberSAFE), but there is **no dedicated anti-deepfake creative campaign** with multilingual BM/EN/CN short-form video. CSCDC's RM80K campaign + RM100K studio can credibly claim first-mover status here.
2. **In-house is the pragmatic default at this budget.** RM100K cannot sustain a top-tier agency retainer (Leo Burnett/Naga DDB). The Ministry of Communications' **Content Development Division** + CyberSecurity Malaysia's existing (under-performing) TikTok/YouTube presence is the natural in-house base. A *hybrid* — in-house studio + specialist freelance/agency on a project basis — fits the budget and Aras Integrasi's methodology-partner positioning.
3. **Multilingual template already exists.** KIS2026 already runs BM/EN/Mandarin/Tamil — CSCDC's BM/EN/CN requirement aligns with proven government precedent, reducing political/linguistic risk.
4. **Regulatory window is permissive but closing.** Micro-targeting is loosely regulated (PDPA + AI ethics, no dedicated statute), but ONSA subsidiary instruments now let MCMC penalise platforms hosting deepfake *paid ads* — meaning a CSCDC campaign must be scrupulously platform-compliant to avoid being caught in its own enforcement net.
5. **ASEAN alignment is a force multiplier.** Malaysia/MCMC is *spearheading* ASEAN deepfake guidelines — framing CSCDC's campaign as the national implementation of an ASEAN mandate gives institutional cover and co-funding potential (Scam Ready ASEAN / Google.org).
6. **Vendor partnership lane open.** No Malaysian deepfake-detection vendor has a government cyber/defence contract of record; pairing campaign content with detection tech (Smartech/Agmo local, or Pindrop/iProov international) could strengthen any CSCDC pitch.
