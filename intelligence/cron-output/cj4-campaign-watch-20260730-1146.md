## Executive Summary

This cycle surfaced significant, time-sensitive intelligence. Malaysia is in the midst of a legislative and regulatory surge against AI deepfakes and misinformation — the **Cybercrimes Bill 2026** passed Parliament on 1 July 2026, and ASEAN is actively drafting deepfake/social-media safety guidelines with **Malaysia (MCMC) spearheading** the effort (announced 22 July 2026 at IRC 2026 in Kuala Lumpur). Critically for CSCDC: **no dedicated, public-facing anti-deepfake awareness campaign was identified.** Existing efforts (MCMC's KIS2026, CyberSecurity Malaysia's CyberSAFE) address misinformation generically but do NOT specifically target deepfakes as a distinct public-education theme. This represents a clear **white-space opportunity** for CSCDC's RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign. Detection capability is being built in-house/nationally (MIMOS, CyberSecurity Malaysia+UKM, IBM-NACSA-MCMC partnership), suggesting the technology backbone is forming but the public communications layer is not. Government social media presence for cybersecurity (CyberSecurity Malaysia TikTok: 209 followers) remains nascent — underscoring the multilingual content-production gap CSCDC's RM 100K Content Studio could fill.

## Strategic Implications for CSCDC / Aras Integrasi

1. **FIRST-MOVER WINDOW OPEN:** No dedicated anti-deepfake public awareness campaign exists in Malaysia. KIS2026 and CyberSAFE cover generic misinformation, not deepfakes specifically. CSCDC's RM 80K Anti-Deepfake Campaign would be a market-leading, category-defining initiative. Align with ASEAN guidelines (Malaysia is the spearhead — CSCDC could position as the campaign-execution partner of record).

2. **IN-HOUSE DIRECTIONALITY IS STRONG:** Budget 2026, MIMOS's self-reliance thesis, the NACSA-MCMC-IBM MoU, and the RM 100K Content Studio allocation collectively signal that government prefers building national/in-house capability. Aras Integrasi's positioning as a *methodology partner* (not creative vendor) aligns well — CSCDC likely wants in-house production with methodology/think-pairing support.

3. **MULTILINGUAL CONTENT IS A PROVEN REQUIREMENT:** KIS2026 already produces BM/EN/Mandarin/Tamil content. CSCDC's BM/EN/CN multilingual requirement is validated and aligns with existing MCMC practice. The Content Studio should match this 4-language baseline (Tamil inclusion recommended even if CN is the stated focus).

4. **DETECTION ≠ COMMUNICATION:** Malaysia is actively building deepfake DETECTION technology (MIMOS, CSM+UKM, IBM), but the public COMMUNICATIONS/awareness layer is essentially absent. This is the precise gap CSCDC's campaign fills — and it is defensible as complementary (not duplicative) to government tech efforts.

5. **SOCIAL MEDIA ENGAGEMENT GAP IS SEVERE:** CyberSecurity Malaysia's TikTok has ~209 followers. The government's short-form video capacity is negligible. A CSCDC Content Studio producing high-quality multilingual TikTok/YouTube content would face minimal incumbent competition.

6. **RECOMMENDED NEXT-CYCLE COLLECTION:** (a) Extract the Parliament e-PRS deepfake threat note (March 2026); (b) Deep-dive ePerolehan/MyProcurement for named creative-agency government panels; (c) Direct outreach/intelligence on Leo Burnett, Ogilvy, Muma cyber/defence portfolio; (d) Confirm micro-targeting regulation specifics via Personal Data Protection Department.
