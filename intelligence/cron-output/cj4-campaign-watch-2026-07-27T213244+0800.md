## Analyst Assessment & Recommendations for CSCDC

1. **AIFA + Sebenarnya.my is the missing rail — integrate, don't replicate.** The MCMC AIFA Chatbot (197K messages, 404/day, 1,016 fact-check articles) is the existing AI-powered fact-checking infrastructure. CSCDC's anti-deepfake campaign should drive audiences to verify via AIFA/Sebenarnya.my as the primary CTA, and reference the 24-hour-blocking regime under the Online Safety (Period) Regulations 2025 as evidence the system acts fast. This makes the campaign's "verify, don't just believe" message operationally real rather than aspirational.

2. **Two creative-agency partners are now on the table — Mutant is the stronger lead candidate.** Mutant Communications explicitly offers multilingual (EN/BM/Mandarin) transcreation, a MICE "Promote/Protect/Prove" framework with government-stakeholder comms and misinformation handling, and verified reach metrics (PepsiCo 4.3B, Hootsuite 1M+). Walk Production adds DBP Sah Bahasa compliance depth. Recommend CSCDC issue a capability enquiry to both requesting: (a) any cybersecurity/defence/government campaign case studies; (b) multilingual anti-deepfake campaign concept + budget against the RM 80K envelope; (c) DBP-compliant production workflow. Tag both as [UNVERIFIED] until independent portfolio confirmation.

3. **DBP Sah Bahasa compliance is a mandatory budget line, not optional.** Three BM priority rules (font size/colour/positioning), Sah Bahasa verification, and DBKL's 31 violation notices (Nov 2024) confirm enforcement is active. Every multilingual anti-deepfake asset (outdoor, digital, print) requires a DBP compliance check before launch — this is a real production cost and timeline item the RM 100K Content Studio must scope. For government/GLC communications, BM may be the lead language (opposite of private-sector workflow) — confirm lead-language preference at the brief stage.

4. **Micro-targeting regulatory framework is now identified — verify the primary statute.** The "2025 Election Offences Act amendments" (per The Malaysian Times) is the first open-source reference to the governing framework. This advances PIR-OPP008-006 from OPEN but the statute's existence/specifics must be verified against the primary text via the Attorney General's Chambers before CSCDC relies on it. The geotargeted-ads-by-constituency and WhatsApp-broadcast-segmentation models are directly transferable to a (non-electoral) government awareness campaign.

5. **The political-content fact-checking gap is structural — CSCDC can fill it carefully.** ISEAS confirms Sebenarnya.my and MyCheck explicitly exclude political content, and JomCheck is constrained by ACT 605 Rule 17(b) (university-faculty speech restrictions) and grant-funding unsustainability. CSCDC's Unit 3 anti-disinformation mandate (under non-electoral government/cyber framing) can credibly address this gap — but must design for the state-influence perception risk that constrained JomCheck (transparent methodology, independent advisory oversight, Aras Integrasi as methodology partner helps here).

6. **ASEAN mandatory AI-content labelling is the emerging standard — pre-empt it in campaign messaging.** The IRC 2026 KL announcement (mandatory AI-content labelling across ASEAN, DEFA framework) means CSCDC's campaign should educate the public to look for AI-content labels before the regional standard fully lands — turning regulatory compliance into a first-mover awareness advantage.

7. **The 2.13M-participant Safe Internet Campaign reach proves distribution capacity exists — co-promote, don't rebuild.** MCMC has already exceeded its 1M annual target mid-year (2,136,941 participants / 1,938 programmes as of 15 Jun 2026). CSCDC's campaign should plug into this existing programme delivery capacity rather than build parallel community activation. The NADI centre network + schools/HEI reach are ready distribution rails.
