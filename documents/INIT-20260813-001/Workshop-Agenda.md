# Workshop Agenda — PERJASA Government AI Systems Co-Design Workshop

WORKSHOP AGENDA
PERJASA Government AI Systems
Co-Design Workshop
A governed two-day strategic workshop to convert priority public-sector problems into validated AI prototypes and controlled pilot pathways.

1. Workshop mandate
The PERJASA Government AI Systems Co-Design Workshop is an implementation-oriented mechanism jointly driven by PERJASA and Aras Integrasi. It is not a conventional training programme. It brings government practitioners, agency process owners and technical specialists together to identify priority problems, design governed AI solutions, produce assessable technical outputs and select initiatives for controlled pilots.

2. Delivery model


3. Objectives and success conditions
Establish a shared government AI adoption direction across sovereign infrastructure, platforms, agency demand, practitioners and trust controls.
Identify and prioritise high-value operational use cases from participating agencies.
Build practical implementation capability through facilitated co-design and hands-on prototyping.
Provide governed GPU access and a safe development environment for workshop teams.
Embed cybersecurity, data governance, responsible AI and human oversight from the beginning.
Select initiatives that are sufficiently valuable, feasible and controlled to enter a 60–90-day pilot pathway.
4. Participation and team structure
The workshop is designed for 24–32 participants organised into four multidisciplinary teams. Each team should include agency process ownership, technical capability and governance representation.

5. Core participation nucleus


6. Pre-workshop preparation


Participant pre-work
Bring one evidence-based government or agency problem with an identified process owner.
Document the current workflow, affected users, pain points and expected operational outcome.
Identify available data sources, classifications, integrations and known constraints.
Propose two or three measurable success indicators for the intended solution.
Use public, synthetic or anonymised data by default. Sensitive government data requires prior approval and explicit controls.

Theme: From validated design to a pilot-ready government AI initiative  |  08:30–18:00

Demonstration format
Each team receives 12 minutes: seven minutes for the problem, prototype and implementation case; three minutes for security, governance and validation evidence; and two minutes for panel questions.

7. Evaluation and portfolio decisions
The evaluation panel will score each initiative out of 100. Progression requires a minimum score of 70, an identified agency owner and no unresolved critical security or governance issue.

Decision categories

8. Required team submission package
Validated problem statement and named operational owner.
Use-case canvas, target users and current-state/target-state workflow.
Working prototype, workflow demonstrator or validated technical architecture.
Solution architecture, data requirements and integration map.
Cybersecurity threat assessment and required controls.
Responsible-AI, privacy and human-oversight assessment.
Validation findings measured against the agreed success indicators.
Pilot charter with scope, owner, users, dependencies, resources and timeline.
30-, 60- and 90-day continuation actions.
9. Governance and delivery roles

10. Operating controls
Data: Public, synthetic or anonymised data is the default. Sensitive data requires explicit approval, purpose limitation and handling controls.
Access: GPU, model and workspace access is named, time-bound, monitored and limited to workshop purposes.
Security: Teams must not introduce unauthorised tools, credentials, datasets or external integrations into the workshop environment.
Human authority: AI outputs remain advisory unless an approved process explicitly authorises automation. Accountable human review must be defined.
Documentation: Architecture, data, decisions, risks and validation evidence must be recorded in the common workspace.
Intellectual contribution: Reusable patterns should be documented without disclosing protected agency information.

11. Post-workshop 90-day continuation

12. Workshop completion criteria
Four validated team outputs have been demonstrated and documented.
Every initiative has a recorded score and portfolio decision.
Pilot candidates have named agency owners and defined implementation actions.
Security, data-governance and responsible-AI findings are documented.
The practitioner-community structure and immediate coordination mechanism are confirmed.
The consolidated report owner and five-working-day publication deadline are accepted.
