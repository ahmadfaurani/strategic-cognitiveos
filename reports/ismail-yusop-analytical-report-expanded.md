## 2. INSTITUTIONAL ANALYSIS

### 2.1 Jabatan Komunikasi Komuniti (JKOM)

#### Historical Evolution

JKOM's predecessor agencies reveal a contentious history of government communication in Malaysia:

| Era | Agency | Period | Political Context |
|-----|--------|--------|-------------------|
| **1959-1960** | BHEK (Badan Hal Ehwal Khas) | 1959-1960 | Post-independence stability unit |
| **1960-2000** | CHEK (Cawangan Hal Ehwal Khas) | 1960-2000 | Information Department branch |
| **2000-2004** | JHEK (Jabatan Hal Ehwal Khas) | 2000-2004 | Cabinet-upgraded department |
| **2004-2018** | JASA (Jabatan Agung Sivik) | 2004-2018 | BN/UMNO propaganda apparatus |
| **2018** | Dissolved | May-Oct 2018 | PH government abolition |
| **2020-Present** | JKOM (Jabatan Komunikasi Komuniti) | 2020-present | Rebranded under PN/PH |

#### JASA Controversy (2004-2018)

The predecessor agency JASA became highly controversial:

- **Criticism:** Accused of serving as UMNO/BN propaganda machine rather than neutral government communication
- **Budget:** RM74 million annually with 1,046 staff (2015-2018)
- **Leadership Pay:** Director-General Puad Zarkashi earned RM20,592/month—higher than ministers (RM14,907/month)
- **Abolition:** PH government dissolved JASA in May 2018, citing excessive political content
- **Staff Transition:** 300 civil servants absorbed into other ministries; contract staff (mostly political appointees) terminated

#### JKOM Rebranding (2020-Present)

Under the Perikatan Nasional and subsequent Madani administrations:

- **Rebranding Rationale:** Distance from JASA's propaganda reputation
- **Budget Reduction:** Initial RM85.5 million request reduced to RM40.5 million (2020)
- **Stated Mission:** Community engagement, not political propaganda
- **Ministry:** Operates under Ministry of Communications (currently Minister Fahmi Fadzil)

#### JKOM Functions

### 2.3 Dewan Negara (Senate) Role

#### Malaysian Senate Structure

The Dewan Negara (House of Senators) consists of:
- **44 Senators:** Appointed by Yang di-Pertuan Agong (King) on Prime Minister's advice
- **26 Senators:** Elected by State Legislative Assemblies (2 per state)
- **Total:** 70 members (may vary slightly due to vacancies)
- **Term:** 3 years, maximum of 2 terms

#### Senator Selection Process

For State-Nominated Senators (Ismail's category):
1. **State Assembly Nomination:** State government proposes candidates
2. **Assembly Vote:** State legislators vote on nominees
3. **Federal Appointment:** Yang di-Pertuan Agong formally appoints
4. **Swearing-In:** Ceremony before Yang Dipertua Dewan Negara

#### Ismail's Senatorial Context (2018-2021)

**August 2018 Batch:**
- 12 new senators sworn in simultaneously
- Occurred during first PH federal government (post-GE14)
- PH still in minority in Senate despite federal victory

**Political Composition of Batch:**
- **PKR:** 3 senators (Ismail Yusop, Monalan Mohamad, Mohamad Imran Abd Hamid)
- **DAP:** 4 senators (including Lim Hui Ying, Nga Hock Cheh, Alan Ling, Adrian Banie Lasimbang)
- **Amanah:** 2 senators (Husam Musa, Raj Munni Sabu)
- **PPBM:** 2 senators (Datuk Razali Idris, Nasir Hashim)
- **Independent:** 1 senator (Theodore Douglas Lind, senior lawyer)

**Perak Connection:**
- Ismail nominated by Perak State Assembly
- Alongside Nga Hock Cheh (DAP Perak Committee Member)
- Suggests strong PKR presence in Perak state government at the time

#### Senate Functions

- **Legislative Review:** Examines bills passed by Dewan Rakyat (House of Representatives)
- **Amendment Power:** Can propose amendments but cannot block money bills
- **Oversight:** Questions ministers, debates policies
- **Representation:** Represents state and minority interests

**Limitations:**
- Cannot initiate money bills
- Limited power compared to elected lower house
- Often viewed as retirement or consolation position for politicians
