# N17 Semerah (P147 Parit Sulong) — Tier-2 BN-Leaning / Contestable

**Signal Date:** 2026-06-27  
**Escalation:** MEDIUM  
**Contest:** Three-cornered fight (BN, PH, PN)

**Seat Snapshot:**
- Incumbent: Mohd Fared Mohd Khalid (BN / UMNO), re-fielded — Johor EXCO for Islamic Religious Affairs
- PH candidate: Mohd Khuzzan Abu Bakar (PKR) — former Semerah ADUN and former Johor EXCO (won 2018)
- PN candidate: Halim Kepol (PAS)
- 2026 electorate: 47,431 registered voters (youth 18–29: 24.1%)
- 2022 majority: 4,041 votes / 14.5% margin
- 2022 turnout: ~60–61%
- Nomination Centre: MRSM Batu Pahat (June 27, 2026)
- Federal Presence: PH Communications Director Fahmi Fadzil accompanied Khuzzan — signals strategic priority

**Historical Pattern:**
- 2018: PH won by only 98 votes (0.3% margin) with 84% turnout
- 2022: BN recovered with 14.5% majority on lower turnout (~60%)
- Seat flipped PH→BN between 2018–2022; highly turnout-sensitive

**Demographics:**
- Malay: 75.1% (35,633 voters)
- Chinese: 23.2% (11,027 voters)
- Indian: 0.5% (256 voters)
- Youth 18–30: 24.7% (11,722 voters)
- Gender: Female 51.0%, Male 49.0%
- 26 polling districts across Batu Pahat–Parit Sulong rural/semi-rural belt

**Strategic Assessment:**
- **BN**: Incumbency + EXCO portfolio advantage. Strong rural Malay base (Peserai, Parit Maimon, Kampung Bintang = 51% of voters). Campaign: "Maju Johor" stability narrative + direct access via EXCO portfolio. Risk is Malay leakage to PN compressing margin.
- **PH**: Mohd Khuzzan is known local figure with 2018 win, but must reverse 2022 collapse (12,619 → 6,265 votes). Federal backing evident (Fahmi Fadzil at nomination). "Nostalgia play" — leverage 2018–2022 tenure to re-engage non-Malay voters who sat out 2022. Depends on Chinese turnout recovery (23.2% of electorate) and mixed PD performance.
- **PN**: Structural disruptor with 8,501-vote 2022 base. PAS brand resonates in rural Malay settings. Deep-rooted local PAS branches in semi-rural PDs (Lubok, Parit Kuda). If Halim Kepol exceeds 2022 base, BN margin collapses.

**The Split Math:** 2022 combined opposition vote (PN 8,501 + PH 6,265 = 14,766) exceeded BN's 12,542 by 2,224 votes. BN's safety relies entirely on continued opposition split.

**Critical Issues:**
- Malay vote split (BN vs PN) — decisive factor
- Chinese voter turnout — PH ceiling depends on this bloc
- Youth mobilisation (18–30 = 24.7%) — high turnout favours PH, low favours BN machinery
- Incumbent service delivery credibility (Mohd Fared's EXCO portfolio)
- Rural locality networks and religious-community credibility

**War Room Assessment:**
- BN Retention Probability: High (60–65%)
- PH Upset Probability: Low to Moderate (25–30%) — requires high turnout + Chinese consolidation + limited Malay leakage to PN
- PN Spoiler/Challenger Role: High — vote split dynamics can determine winner
- Key Battleground: Malay-majority PDs (Peserai, Parit Maimon) vs Chinese-concentration PDs (Kampung Pantai Timor, Shahbandar, Jalan Jenang, Pasar)
- Primary Watch Metrics:
  - PN/PAS vote share vs 2022 baseline (8,501 votes)
  - Chinese turnout in Kampung Pantai Timor (75% Chinese), Shahbandar (92%), Jalan Jenang (93%), Pasar (94%)
  - Youth turnout (18–30) across all 26 PDs
  - Malay vote split in Peserai, Parit Maimon, Kampung Bintang

**Operational Priority:** Turnout differential is the decisive variable. 2018 high turnout (84%) produced PH win; 2022 low turnout (60%) produced BN recovery. BN's 4,041-vote cushion is vulnerable if PN exceeds 2022 base and PH recovers Chinese/non-Malay turnout.

**Bottom Line:** N17 Semerah is BN-leaning but operationally competitive. BN enters with incumbent, EXCO advantage, and 2022 majority. PH has credible former ADUN but must rebuild lost vote share. PN is main disruptor: if Halim Kepol grows beyond 8,501 votes and converts Malay protest sentiment, Semerah tightens into genuine three-way contest.
