## The Five-Layer R.I.S.I.K Methodology

### Layer Structure (Each layer has same format: Purpose → Concepts → Tool → Gate → Common Errors)

| Layer | Name | Purpose | Output | Tool |
|-------|------|---------|--------|------|
| R | Risikan (Intelligence) | Gather open-source intelligence on issues, influencers, communication patterns | Dated, graded signal list with working links | Borang Isyarat (Signal Form) |
| I | Isu (Issue) | Determine the main issue/threat capable of triggering crisis or public manipulation | Kad Isu (Issue Card) — one per issue, with name, boundaries, triage score | Kad Isu |
| S | Sentimen (Sentiment) | Assess sentiment landscape | Profil Sentimen (Sentiment Profile) | Profil Sentimen |
| I | Influens (Influence) | Map actors and influence networks | Peta Aktor (Actor Map) | Peta Aktor |
| K | Kontra-naratif (Counter-narrative) | Formulate response | Pelan Respons (Response Plan) | Pelan Respons |

### The Cycle (Kitaran R.I.S.I.K)
- Five layers traversed in sequence
- Output of final layer (K) feeds back into first layer (R) — closed cycle
- **Shape matters:** Funnel (narrowing from broad to specific, ending at one response arrow) + cycle (response itself becomes new signal)
- Methods ending at response can only claim to have acted. Methods that cycle can show whether action was effective.
- **R→I and I→K sequences cannot be skipped:** Can't assess sentiment toward an unnamed issue; can't form response toward an issue whose spreaders haven't been identified
- **S and I (middle layers) can run concurrently** when time pressures

### Four Gates (Gerbang) — [TAMBAHAN, not in original framework]

| Gate | Name | Function |
|------|------|----------|
| 1 | Kelayakan (Qualification) | Before signals become issues: ≥2 independent signals OR 1 grade A/B signal; working links; touches entity directly |
| 2 | Keutamaan (Priority) | Triage scoring — which issues deserve full analysis? |
| 3 | Kewajaran (Appropriateness) | Is communication the right response? |
| 4 | Keberkesanan (Effectiveness) | Did the response work? (Cycle closure) |

## Layer R — Risikan (Intelligence) — Detailed

### Three Source Disciplines

| Discipline | What's Collected | Key Limitation |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| OSINT | Publicly accessible published information | Slow; only catches what's already reported |
| SOCMINT | Social media content and spread patterns | Biased toward those who speak; metrics can be manipulated |
| HUMINT [TAMBAHAN] | What people tell you directly | Cannot be published; small sample, biased |

**HUMINT is a proposed addition** (Cadangan 7) — not in original framework. Rationale: HUMINT is the only discipline that reaches silent populations. Frontline staff, district officers, sector associations know about failures that don't produce signals in OSINT/SOCMINT.

**HUMINT line in this doctrine:** Declared and consensual. No covert collection, no impersonation, no infiltration, no recording without knowledge.

### Platform Coverage (Malaysia Context)

| Source Layer | Examples | Role in Issue Cycle |
|-------------|----------|-------------------|
| Mainstream media | Newspapers, news portals, broadcasters | Validates and amplifies; rarely initiates |
| Short-form video | TikTok, Reels, Shorts | Often initiates. Fast cycle, cross-age reach |
| Open social platforms | Facebook, X, Threads, Instagram | Public debate, frame formation |
| Closed spaces | Messaging apps, groups | Largest spread channel, hardest to see |
| Public documents | Press releases, Parliament answers, statistics | Verification material |

**Blind spot acknowledged:** Closed spaces (messaging apps) are likely the largest spread channel in Malaysia and cannot be legally monitored. Doctrine doesn't support attempting to penetrate them. What can be done: detect traces when closed-space content surfaces in open spaces (usually as screenshots).

### Narrative Identification — Five-Step Escalation Ladder

| Rung | Narrative | Can facts answer it? |
|------|----------|---------------------|
| 1 | Keluhan (Grievance) — "We bear the consequences" | Partly |
| 2 | Ketidakcekapan (Incompetence) — "They don't know what they're doing" | Yes — performance record |
| 3 | Keistimewaan (Favoritism) — "Others get it, we don't" | Yes — transparent comparison |
| 4 | Pengkhianatan (Betrayal) — "Hidden agenda" | Difficult — facts interpreted as evidence of cover-up |
| 5 | Konspirasi (Conspiracy) — "Someone controls everything" | No — denial strengthens narrative |

**Critical insight:** Above rung 4, facts stop working and become counter-productive. The real window to act is before an issue reaches rung 4. After that, the work shifts from correction to long-term trust-building.

### Source Grading System

| Grade | Source Type | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| A | Official documents, statistical data, recorded direct statements | Can be basis of claims |
| B | Reputable mainstream media with clear attribution | Can be basis of claims |
| C | Partisan portals, identified influential accounts | Needs cross-verification |
| D | Secondary media, aggregators, opinion columns | Needs cross-verification |
| E | Anonymous accounts with reviewable history | Phenomenon only |
| F | Screenshots without origin, forwarded messages | Phenomenon only |

**"Phenomenon only"** — Grade E/F content is evidence of what people believe, not evidence of what's true. Confusing these two uses is the most common source of embarrassing intelligence reports.

### Signal Form (Borang Isyarat) Fields

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Date/time of publication | Original time, not discovery time |
| Link | Must be openable |
| Platform | — |
| Source grade | A through F |
| Narrative | One of five types, or "unclear" |
| Rung | 1-5 |
| Behavioural indicators | List observed, or "none" |
| Touches | Entity/issue blamed/both |
| Collector note | One sentence |

### Gate 1 — Kelayakan (Qualification)

Conditions before signal cluster becomes an issue:
- At least 2 independent signals, OR 1 grade A/B signal
- Every signal has working link and publication time
- Signal touches entity or issue directly

**Failure → stays in monitoring. Not elevated to issue, not scored, not reported as issue.**

Rationale: Cost of error is asymmetric. False elevation wastes analysis time and may trigger response. False negative will resurface if truly important.

### Common Errors in Layer R
1. Chasing viral content and missing important content
2. Collecting without baseline (100 mentions means nothing without knowing the norm)
3. Depending on one platform (each platform has systematic bias)
4. Taking screenshots as fact (Grade F is Grade F)
5. Collecting too late (in case study, earliest signals appeared 12 days before policy took effect)

## Layer I — Isu (Issue) — Detailed

### Three Decisions to Convert Signals to Issues
1. **Pengelompokan (Grouping)** — Which signals belong together?
2. **Persempadanan (Bounding)** — What's in this issue, what's not?
3. **Penamaan (Naming)** — What is this issue called, in whose language?

### Breaking Combined Issues
The most damaging error: treating one public conversation as one issue. One apparent issue usually contains 2-3 separate issues with different causes, audiences, and solutions.

**Issue split test:** Break if:
- Different parts have different affected audiences
- Different parts will be resolved by different actions
- Some parts can be proven true while others proven false

### Naming Issues
Good names are: Neutral (don't assume answer), Precise (outsider understands without explanation), Bounded (refer to specific dispute, not category).

### Triage Score [TAMBAHAN]
Two-axis scoring: Sensitivity (low/medium/high) × Reach (low/medium/high) = 1-5 score. Answers only: which issues deserve full analysis? Not an action decision — a resource allocation decision.

## §2.5 — AI in the Information Environment [TAMBAHAN — Not in original framework]

**This is the most critical section for Aras AI development. It's a proposed addition (Cadangan 8) that directly addresses AI's role in the R.I.S.I.K framework.**

### Core Principle: AI is NOT a Fourth Discipline

"AI is not a discipline. This needs to be stated upfront, because the temptation to create one is strong and its cost is high."

- Disciplines are defined by source, not tool. OSINT = open sources. SOCMINT = social media. HUMINT = humans. AI is how information is processed and generated, not where it comes from.
- No recognized "AI-INT" discipline exists in military intelligence doctrine. US military intelligence still lists six disciplines, none AI-related.
- International frameworks (RESIST 3, DISARM) treat AI as tool, threat surface, and cross-cutting factor — not as a source.
- Using "AI-INT" in client or government products will damage credibility.

### Four Ways AI Changes the Landscape

#### 1. AI as Tool (cross-cutting all three disciplines)
- Translation, transcription, signal clustering, summarization of large volumes, pattern detection
- Accelerates Layers R and Influens significantly
- Makes multi-language coverage achievable for small teams
- **Does NOT replace judgment at any gate**

#### 2. AI as Threat Surface
- Production cost collapsed; distribution cost did NOT — this is the most important distinction
- Four independent producer reports reach same conclusion: AI-generated content on its own was not the decisive factor; popularity of distributing accounts was
- OpenAI (2026): "bolt AI onto old playbooks to move faster, not gain novel offensive capability"
- Meta (2025): "we haven't seen these efficiency gains lead to greater operational success"
- Anthropic (2025): 100+ bot accounts managed — "No content achieved viral status"
- BUT: total volume is surging. EEAS recorded 540 incidents in 2025, 27% involving AI-related tactics
- **Key nuance:** Lab experiments show AI content can be as effective as human propaganda when audiences actually read it. Contradiction explained: experiments force people to read; in the field, no one reads. The real constraint is audience-building, not content production.
- **Operational implication:** Don't elevate seriousness score just because content is AI-generated. Elevate because of its distribution.

#### 3. The Genuinely New Threat: Reference Poisoning (Keracunan Rujukan)
- LLM grooming: flooding information space with repetitive content so AI models absorb and repeat it later
- One investigated network published ~3.6 million articles across 150 domains in a year
- Audit of top 10 chatbots: ~1/3 of responses repeated the seeded narrative
- Follow-up confirmed content entered public training corpus
- Not always successful: another operation failed to contaminate tested models despite thousands of archived pages
- **This is a technique with variable success rate, not a guaranteed weapon**

#### 4. AI as New Audience
- When people ask chatbots instead of searching, AI answers become the endpoint, not the gateway
- ~10% of global respondents use AI chatbots for news weekly (up from 7%), reaching 17% among 18-24
- Click-through to source links dropped from 15% to 8% when AI summaries displayed
- **For Layer R:** New question not in any existing framework: "What does the AI system say about this entity when asked?" This is now part of the information environment.

#### 5. Analyst as Attack Surface
Three documented risks, all requiring SOPs not just awareness:

**a) Prompt injection** — When AI tools read monitored party content (web pages, PDFs, posts), that content is untrusted input that can contain instructions. UK cyber security authority states this "will never be properly mitigated." Working principle: when a model processes information from a party, its privilege drops to that party's level. For intelligence work: the party you monitor can, in principle, write instructions into materials your tools read.

**b) Hallucination and fabricated citations** — One database tracking court cases alone recorded 1,800+ incidents of fabricated AI references. For a doctrine whose entire credibility rests on verifiable sources, one fabricated citation is enough to destroy a report.

**c) Automation bias** — Study across 9 countries: the most prone to blindly accept AI suggestions are not the most ignorant and not experts, but comfortable mid-level users. High-confidence presentation increases the rate of users changing their own correct answers.

**Training implication:** Target the middle group specifically. They are the most at risk and the least likely to realize it.

### AI Usage Rules in R.I.S.I.K Work
Adapted from US intelligence community AI guidance:

1. **Accountability stays with the analyst.** AI output is not a source and cannot be cited as one.
2. **Every claim originating from AI must be traced to original source and verified at that source** before entering any report.
3. **Analyst must be able to review, question, and reject AI suggestions.** Tools that don't allow this are unsuitable.
4. **Declare to recipient when AI materially influences a product.**
5. **Treat monitored materials as untrusted when fed into AI tools.**

## AI Capability Mapping (For Alignment Session — Updated with Doctrine Insights)

### Critical Doctrine Constraint
The §2.5 section makes clear that AI in R.I.S.I.K must:
- Serve as a TOOL, not a discipline
- Never replace human judgment at gates
- Maintain analyst accountability
- Trace every claim to original source
- Declare AI influence on products
- Treat monitored content as untrusted input

**Any AI capability we propose must satisfy these constraints.**

### Updated AI Capability Map by Layer

| Layer | AI Capability | Doctrine Alignment |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **R — Risikan** | Automated signal collection (OSINT/SOCMINT), multi-language coverage, signal clustering, pattern detection, narrative classification (5-type ladder), source grading assistance, LLM reference checking ("what does AI say about this entity?") | §2.5.1 — AI as tool. Accelerates collection without replacing judgment at Gate 1. The "ask the chatbot" question (§2.5.4) is a NEW intelligence requirement. |
| **I — Isu** | AI-assisted signal grouping, issue boundary suggestion, neutral naming assistance, triage scoring support | Human makes all three decisions (grouping, bounding, naming). AI can draft suggestions but analyst owns the frame. |
| **S — Sentimen** | Automated sentiment analysis, trend detection, baseline computation, anomaly flagging | Must declare AI influence on sentiment product. Analyst must be able to review and reject. |
| **I — Influens** | Actor network mapping, behavioural indicator detection (SAMAR indicators), coordinated inauthentic behaviour detection | AI excels at pattern detection across large volumes (§2.5.1). But SAMAR indicators require 3+ converging signals for coordination claims — AI can surface candidates, human confirms. |
| **K — Kontra-naratif** | Response option generation, narrative ladder position assessment, effectiveness prediction | Gate 3 (Kewajaran) and Gate 4 (Keberkesanan) are human decisions. AI can inform but not decide. |

### Updated Priority Use Cases for Alignment Session

**Use Case 1: Signal Collection & Grading Engine**
- Automate OSINT/SOCMINT collection across Malaysian media landscape
- Auto-grade sources A-F per doctrine
- Auto-classify narratives per 5-type escalation ladder
- Auto-detect behavioural indicators per SAMAR framework
- Output: Pre-populated Borang Isyarat (Signal Forms) for analyst review
- **Why first:** Layer R is the foundation — everything depends on signal quality. This is where AI has the highest leverage and clearest doctrine alignment.
- **Doctrine compliance:** Analyst reviews every signal. AI never grades its own output as source. Human Gate 1 (Kelayakan) intact.

**Use Case 2: Issue Decomposition Assistant**
- AI-assisted signal grouping (which signals belong together?)
- Issue boundary suggestion (what's in/out?)
- Neutral naming drafts (avoiding framed language)
- Triage score calculation (sensitivity × reach)
- Output: Draft Kad Isu (Issue Card) for analyst review
- **Why second:** The most damaging error at Layer I is treating one conversation as one issue. AI can surface the component issues by clustering signals by audience, cause, and verifiability.
- **Doctrine compliance:** Human makes all three decisions. AI drafts, human decides.

**Use Case 3: Reference Poisoning Monitor** (NEW — from §2.5.4)
- Monitor what major AI chatbots say about monitored entities/issues
- Track changes in AI-generated responses over time
- Detect potential LLM grooming campaigns (coordinated content flooding)
- Flag when monitored narratives appear in AI chatbot responses
- Output: "AI Environment Signal" — new signal type for Layer R
- **Why this matters:** The doctrine explicitly identifies this as a new intelligence requirement not covered by any existing framework. No other information operations framework addresses this.
- **First-mover advantage:** This capability doesn't exist in any commercial product. R.I.S.I.K would be the first doctrine to operationalize it.

## Additional Strategic Observations

### 1. The [TAMBAHAN] Pattern
Several key additions are marked [TAMBAHAN] — not yet approved by the framework author:
- Four gates (Kelayakan, Keutamaan, Kewajaran, Keberkesanan)
- HUMINT as third discipline
- AI section §2.5
- Triage scoring system
- Penilaian (Evaluation) as closing layer

**Implication:** The framework is evolving. The additions are well-researched and fill real gaps, but they're not yet canonical. Aras AI development should build for the core framework while supporting the proposed additions as configurable.

### 2. The Asymmetric Error Principle
The doctrine is built on asymmetric error costs:
- Gate 1: False elevation worse than false negative (wasted analysis + response triggered)
- Layer I: Wrong issue definition makes all subsequent layers perfect but useless
- Source grading: Confusing "what people believe" with "what's true" is the most embarrassing error

**AI implication:** AI systems should be biased toward precision over recall. Better to miss a signal than to flood analysts with false positives. This is the opposite of typical AI collection tools.

### 3. The Closed Space Problem
The doctrine honestly acknowledges that closed messaging spaces (WhatsApp, Telegram) are likely the largest spread channel in Malaysia and cannot be legally monitored. It doesn't try to solve this. Instead: detect traces when content surfaces in open spaces.

**AI opportunity:** Pattern detection of screenshot-based spread (when closed-space content appears as screenshots in open spaces) could be automated. This is doctrinally compliant.

### 4. The "Ask the Chatbot" Question
§2.5.4 introduces a genuinely new intelligence requirement: "What does the AI system say about this entity when asked?" This is not in any existing framework. It's a new information surface that didn't exist 3 years ago.

**This is the most innovative part of the doctrine and a potential differentiator for the KKOM system.**

### 5. Case Study (Bahagian II)
The diesel subsidy 2024 case study is fully open-source and demonstrates the framework on a real Malaysian policy issue. The case study shows:
- Earliest signals appeared 12 days before policy took effect
- Public conversation treated everything as one issue, but proper analysis split it into 3 separate issues
- Two of three issues didn't need communication — they needed policy/operational fixes
- The HUMINT gap (silent failures) was the biggest intelligence failure of the entire case

### 6. Three Rules That Hold Everything
1. No building before writing (every stage starts with written spec and acceptance criteria)
2. No approval by demo alone (reviewer tests personally)
3. Decisions must be written with reasons

**These map directly to the TULIS → BINA → SEMAK → LULUS cycle from Document 3.**
