# Cohort Programme — Strategic Objective (Canonical)

**Authority:** DAF | **Established:** 2026-08-18 | **Last refined:** 2026-08-18 | **TLP:** AMBER

---

## Canonical Statement

The primary objective of the Cohort Programme is to build and sustain a high-value Alumni Community of practitioners, technologists, institutional champions and strategic partners who remain actively connected beyond the formal cohort, creating a long-term ecosystem for collaboration, knowledge exchange and opportunity development.

Through this community, anchored on Aras's sovereign GPU compute platform as its infrastructure home, Aras will facilitate the co-development of applied AI and sovereign technologies, joint intellectual property creation, validation of real institutional use cases, and progression of viable capabilities toward pilot, commercialisation and sovereign technology deployment.

Joint IP ownership creates permanent entanglement — alumni cannot leave an ecosystem where they co-own intellectual property, because they need ongoing platform access to use, maintain, and extend what they have built. This lock-in mechanism is what converts a cohort from a temporary programme into an enduring relationship.

The community is self-reinforcing: each cohort produces new alumni who advocate, mentor, and bring new institutions into the ecosystem, compounding the network effect without requiring DAF to personally drive each connection.

All co-developed technology is Malaysian-built, Malaysian-owned, and deployable in government and national security contexts where foreign vendor solutions cannot operate — establishing a sovereign capability that is nationally distinctive, not merely another AI community.

This establishes a repeatable pathway:

**Cohort → Alumni Community → Co-Development → Joint IP → Validation → Pilot → Commercialisation → Sovereign Deployment → Enduring Strategic Ecosystem**

## Two Pillars

### Pillar 1: Alumni Community (Primary Objective)

The community IS the product. Not the workshop. Not the platform. The community.

Every cohort programme exists to feed graduates into a persistent alumni network. The network is the moat — competitors can replicate a workshop, replicate compute infrastructure, or undercut pricing. They cannot replicate an installed community of practitioners who co-own IP with Aras and depend on the platform for ongoing access to their work.

**Community composition:**
- Practitioners — government officers who built prototypes on the platform
- Technologists — architects and developers who co-developed sovereign technology
- Institutional champions — senior stakeholders who sponsored cohort participation
- Strategic partners — CSM, UiTM, PERJASA, and future partners who anchor the ecosystem

**Community functions:**
- Collaboration — ongoing co-development across institutions and cohorts
- Knowledge exchange — learning shared across domains, agencies, and academic boundaries
- Opportunity development — alumni create new programmes, partnerships, and deployments that didn't exist before

**What makes the community irreversible:**
- Alumni co-own IP built during cohorts → permanent platform dependency
- Alumni have institutional relationships forged through co-development → not replicable outside the cohort
- Alumni have evaluated, scored, and piloted on the platform → institutional commitment is documented
- Alumni reference each other → network compounds without central coordination

### Pillar 2: Co-Development & Joint IP Creation (Major Focus)

Cohort participants don't just learn AI or use compute. They co-build sovereign technology with Aras — and what they build becomes joint intellectual property.

**Why joint IP is the lock-in mechanism:**
- You can leave a training programme. You can leave a vendor relationship. You cannot leave an ecosystem where you co-own intellectual property.
- Joint IP requires ongoing platform access to use, maintain, extend, and deploy what was built.
- Each IP asset creates a permanent stake in the platform — the more IP, the deeper the entanglement.

**What co-development produces:**
- Applied AI solutions for real government use cases
- Sovereign technology deployable in national security contexts
- Validated capabilities (not just prototypes) — tested against real institutional requirements
- Commercial assets — IP that can progress to pilot, commercialisation, and deployment

**Sovereign framing:**
- Malaysian-built — developed on Aras infrastructure by Malaysian practitioners
- Malaysian-owned — joint IP shared between Aras and Malaysian institutions
- Government-deployable — operates in classified, sovereign, and national security contexts where foreign vendor solutions cannot
- Cyber Security Act 2024 aligned — national capability framing, not foreign dependency

## How Each Programme Serves the Objective

### PRG-001: PERJASA — First Cohort, First Alumni
- 24-32 participants co-build prototypes in 4 teams → prototypes become joint IP
- 100-point evaluation (70 threshold) → only viable prototypes progress to pilot
- 90-day continuation → pilots produce deployable sovereign technology
- Alumni network launches with first cohort graduates
- **KPI: alumni producing deployable joint IP, not workshop satisfaction scores**

### PRG-002: CSM-Aras — Anchor Partner Co-Development
- CSM/MyCERT on Co-Design Lab → GenAI research products co-developed on Aras compute
- 5-pillar JOM: P3 (AI Cyber R&D) and P5 (Capability Development) are IP-creation pillars
- 23 MyCERT personnel already onboarded — co-building on the platform now
- **KPI: joint IP assets in the JOM IP register, not compute hours consumed**

### PRG-003: PMO — Federal-Level Co-Development (Stalled)
- Same model as PERJASA, scaled to federal government
- Would produce federal-level joint IP → highest-value sovereign technology
- **Status: Zombie. Kill Aug 25.**

### PRG-004: R.I.S.I.K × UiTM — Academic-Validated Joint IP
- UiTM and Aras co-create the RISIK AI Platform as joint IP
- RM5M MCMC-funded → government pays for the co-development of sovereign IP
- Academic validation makes the IP credible for national security deployment
- 5 stage gates (G1-G5) → each gate produces validated IP deliverables
- **KPI: MCMC-funded joint IP assets delivered and accepted at each stage gate**

### PRG-005: VORON-C2 — Talent Feeding Co-Development
- Interns co-build tools that become Aras sovereign IP
- Red Team Division (5 FTE) operates and extends the co-developed stack
- Zero licensing cost stack → all IP is sovereign-built, no foreign vendor dependency
- **KPI: sovereign IP components built by interns that enter the production stack**

## Success Definition

The cohort programme succeeds when:

1. **A persistent alumni community exists** — graduates from multiple cohorts form an active network that collaborates, exchanges knowledge, and develops opportunities
2. **Joint IP portfolio is growing** — each cohort produces co-developed, co-owned IP assets that progress toward deployment
3. **Sovereign technology is deployed** — co-developed IP is operating in production government environments, not just prototypes
4. **The community self-reinforces** — alumni bring new agencies, mentor new cohorts, and expand the IP portfolio without DAF personally driving each connection
5. **Aras is the default platform** — when government institutions need AI compute or AI development capability, the alumni network routes them to Aras because that's where their IP lives
