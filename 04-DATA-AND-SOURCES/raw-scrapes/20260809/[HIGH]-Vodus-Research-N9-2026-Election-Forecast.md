## Core Election Forecast (Pre-Election)

The 2026 Negeri Sembilan state election was forecast as a tight race where popular vote leadership does not guarantee a governing majority.

| Metric | Pakatan Harapan (PH) | Barisan Nasional (BN) | Perikatan Nasional (PN) | Others/Undecided |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Popular Vote** | **42%** | 35% | 7% | 16% (Ind 2% + Undecided 14%) |
| **Projected Seats** | **17** | 15 | 2 | 2 (Too Close To Call) |
| **Net Trust Score** | 19% | **45%** | 5% | - |

> **Key Finding:** "PH leads the popular vote and current seat projection, but BN's trust advantage and two unresolved contests keep the outcome open."

*   **Majority Threshold:** **19 of 36 seats** required to form government.
*   **Current Status:** Neither PH nor BN had secured a majority at time of forecast. The **2 unresolved BN-PH contests** were decisive.
*   **Seat Dynamics:** PH leads in **8 of the 10** closely contested seats. BN held **15 clear leads** compared to PH's **9**.

## Key Voter Dynamics & Demographics [CRITICAL FOR PIR-PM-02]

### Ethnic Support Profiles
BN and PH were assembling different electoral coalitions.

| Ethnic Group | BN | PH | PN |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bumi Voters** | **41%** | 27% | 25% |
| **Chinese Voters** | 20% | **59%** | 9% |

*   **Implication:** Mixed-electorate areas are sensitive to turnout and whether Malay support remains consolidated or divides among BN/PN/Bersatu.

### Trust & Leadership
BN held a significant advantage in governing confidence despite trailing in vote share.

*   **Net Trust (Trust – Distrust):** BN (**45%**) vs PH (19%) vs PN (5%).
*   **Leadership Favorability:**
    *   MB Aminuddin Harun (PH): **31.7%**
    *   Johor MB Onn Hafiz Ghazi (BN): **58.1%** (Reference point from earlier Johor survey)
*   **Gap:** A **26.4-percentage-point** leadership favorability gap suggests PH lacks the same level of personal leadership appeal observed for BN in Johor.

### Top Campaign Issues
Voters were prioritizing economic livelihood concerns.

| Issue | % Ranking in Top 3 |
|---|---|
| **Cost of Living** | **55%** |
| Jobs and Wages | 34% |
| Housing Affordability | 26% |
| Corruption and Governance | 24% |

> **Insight:** "Livelihood concerns cannot be addressed through a general slogan alone. Voters are linking prices with wage growth, housing affordability, job opportunities, and the credibility of government delivery."

## Voter Mobilization & Certainty

*   **Vote Certainty:** **70%** completely/fairly certain; **30%** remain movable or unsure.
*   **Turnout Intention:** **71%** likely/very likely to vote; only **51%** in the strongest "very likely" category.
*   **Barriers to Voting:** Travel/being outside NS, inconvenience, work commitments, living outside voting area.
*   **Strategy:** Mobilization requires addressing practical barriers, not just persuasion.

---

## Deciding Factors

The final outcome depends on three interconnected forces:

1.  **BN:** Must retain Malay support against competition from Bersatu/PN.
2.  **PH:** Must preserve Chinese/other voter advantage while increasing turnout and converting undecideds.
3.  **Both:** Must manage the **close-contest layer**, where small changes alter the governing arithmetic.

---

## Methodology

*   **Sample:** **437 registered Negeri Sembilan voters**.
*   **Fieldwork:** July 9 to 21, 2026 (Nomination day: July 18).
*   **Method:** Vodus OMTOS online survey (short, one-question-at-a-time modules).
*   **Weighting:** Post-stratified to align with state-seat ethnic, age, and gender composition (DOSM).
*   **Model:** Popular vote uses effective likely-voter model; seat projections incorporate candidate availability and demographic interpolation.
