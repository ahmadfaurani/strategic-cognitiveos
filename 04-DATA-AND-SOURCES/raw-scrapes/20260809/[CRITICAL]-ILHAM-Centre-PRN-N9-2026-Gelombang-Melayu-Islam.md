## 1. Core Finding: Dominance of Identity Politics

The 2026 Negeri Sembilan State Election (PRN) was defined by **Malay-Islam identity politics**, overriding administration records and development achievements.

> "PRN Negeri Sembilan kali ini memperlihatkan politik identiti kembali menjadi faktor paling dominan dalam membentuk tingkah laku pengundi, khususnya dalam kalangan pengundi Melayu."

*   **Shift in Focus:** Campaigns moved from administration records to issues of religion, Malay status, and political power.
*   **Voter Decision:** Voters chose based on perceived ability to defend religion and race interests rather than economic or development performance.
*   **PH Failure:** Pakatan Harapan (PH) failed to offer a counter-narrative with comparable emotional strength.

---

## 2. BN-PN Cooperation & Vote Transfer Matrix [CRITICAL FOR PIR-PM-01]

The collaboration between Barisan Nasional (BN) and Perikatan Nasional (PN) was more structured and efficient than in previous elections (e.g., Johor PRN).

### Vote Transfer Efficiency
ILHAM Centre found asymmetric but effective vote transfer between coalition partners:

| Transfer Direction | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PN Supporters → BN Seats** | **~92%** | Highly efficient transfer |
| **BN Supporters → PN Seats** | **~75%** | Some traditional BN supporters took time to adjust |

### Impact
*   **Consolidation:** Reduced vote leakage when both parties previously contested against each other.
*   **Malay Support:** PH estimated to secure only **~18%** of Malay voter support.
*   **Result:** BN strengthened traditional strongholds; PN gained double benefits via vote spillover in contested areas.

---

## 3. PH Campaign Challenges

PH strategy focused on state administration success ("Tok Min" record) but failed to resonate emotionally.

*   **Strategy Shift:** After Johor defeat, PH morale was low. Campaign shifted to attacking opponents (e.g., Muhammad Sanusi Md Nor's controversial statements, RCI Tabung Haji) rather than promoting a positive narrative.
*   **Ineffectiveness:** Attacks on Sanusi impacted non-Malay sentiment but failed to neutralize the Malay-Islam wave. RCI Tabung Haji issues failed to break the deadlock due to mismatched communication strategy with Malay psychology.
*   **Narrative Control:** BN-PN consistently controlled the identity politics narrative throughout the campaign.

---

## 4. Demographic Shifts & Voter Sentiment [CRITICAL FOR PIR-PM-02]

### Chinese Voters: No Longer Automatic Support
*   **Trend:** Support is **pragmatic and defensive**, not enthusiastic like PRU15/PRN 2023.
*   **Driver:** Lack of viable alternatives rather than high confidence in PH performance.
*   **Grievances:** Disappointment in national policies (tax, subsidy adjustments, e-invoice, business enforcement) and slow institutional reforms.
*   **Impact:** PH failed to maximize mobilization among core supporters.

### Indian Voters: Moving Away from PH
*   **Trend:** Clear tendency to distance from PH and shift to opponents.
*   **Key Issues:** Overload vehicle enforcement, temple management issues, insensitive administration policies.
*   **Critical Constituencies (High Indian Voter %):**
    *   Jeram Padang (**33%**)
    *   Seremban Jaya (**28%**)
    *   Sri Tanjung (**27%**)
    *   Rantau (**27%**)
    *   Lukut (**26%**)
    *   Mambau (**25%**)
*   **Significance:** ~15% Indian voter composition statewide makes this shift impactful in competitive seats.

### Young Voters (18-20): Party Brand Over Candidate [CRITICAL FOR PIR-PM-02]
*   **Composition:** **~48%** of total electorate are young voters.
    *   **15 DUN** have >50% young voters.
    *   **Top 5 Areas:** Bagan Pinang (60%), Paroi (59%), Gemas (58%), Serting (58%), Labu (55%).
*   **Behavior:** Decisions made early; driven by **party brand and social media narratives** (Malay-Islam sentiment) rather than candidate personality or service record.
*   **Gap:** Parties failed to address youth-specific issues (jobs, housing, skills, future prospects), leaving space for identity politics to dominate.

---

## 5. Leadership Factor: "Tok Min" (Aminuddin Harun)

*   **Approval Rating:** Remained high at **~75%** (moderate image, stable admin record).
*   **Limitation:** Popularity did not translate to wider PH vote support.
*   **Campaign Constraint:** Shift from **Sikamat to Linggi** constituency forced him to focus resources on securing his own seat, limiting his ability to campaign statewide as a state leader.

---

## 6. Election Prediction & Seat Analysis

ILHAM Centre projected a significant restructuring of political support in Negeri Sembilan.

| Coalition | Projected Seats | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **BN + PN** | **22 Seats** | Stable Majority Block |
| **PH** | **9 Seats (Min)** | Reliant on Chinese-majority seats |

### Battleground Seats (Too Close to Call)
Five constituencies identified as highly dynamic:
*   Chennah, Linggi, Nilai, Repah, Temiang

---

## 7. Bersatu Solo Performance

*   **Vote Share:** 2.62% statewide (16,757 votes)
*   **Seats Won:** 0/24 contested
*   **Deposits Lost:** 23 out of 24 candidates
*   **Assessment:** Pre-election analyst fear of Bersatu as spoiler was **WRONG**. Bersatu's solo contest did not spoil any specific seats significantly; the BN-PN vote transfer was efficient enough to absorb any leakage.
