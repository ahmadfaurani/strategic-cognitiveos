## Election Projections

Ilham Centre projected a decisive victory for the **BN-PN alliance** in the Negeri Sembilan state election, driven by effective vote transfer and identity politics.

| Coalition | Projected Seats | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **BN-PN** | **22 Seats** | Stable Majority (Potential 2/3 Majority) |
| **PH** | **Min. 9 Seats** | Significant Deficit |

*   **Two-Thirds Potential:** If BN captured several of the 5 "too close to call" seats, the alliance could surpass the **two-thirds majority threshold** in the State Legislative Assembly.
*   **No Ethnic Full Support:** No major ethnic group was expected to throw full support behind Pakatan Harapan (PH).

---

## Driving Factors: Identity Politics [CRITICAL FOR PIR-PM-08]

Campaigning shifted away from governance and development toward **identity politics**, specifically regarding religion and Malay rights.

*   **Dominant Narrative:** Voters were choosing based on perceptions of who can better defend **religion and Malay community interests**, rather than administrative performance.
*   **Shared Narrative:** Unlike GE15 or 2023 state polls, identity politics was now a **shared narrative embraced by both BN and PN supporters**.
*   **PH Counter-Narrative:** PH's focus on governance and administrative achievements failed to match the emotional appeal of the BN-PN identity wave.
*   **Comparison:** The Malay-Muslim political wave in Negeri Sembilan was **more organised and effective** than the cooperation seen during the Johor state election.

---

## Coalition Dynamics & Vote Transfer [CRITICAL FOR PIR-PM-01]

Vote transfers between BN and PN supporters were smoother than expected, consolidating Malay support.

| Transfer Direction | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **PN to BN Transfer** | **92%** | Highly efficient |
| **BN to PN Transfer** | **75%** | Some traditional BN supporters still adapting |

*   **Malay Vote Share:** PH estimated to secure only **18% of the Malay vote**.

---

## PH Challenges & Voter Sentiment

PH faced a "double deficit": losing Malay support while failing to fully mobilise Chinese voters.

### Chinese Voter Sentiment
*   Support was **pragmatic, not automatic**, driven by the absence of a credible alternative.
*   **Dissatisfaction Drivers:** Federal policies influencing sentiment included:
    *   Taxation
    *   Subsidy rationalisation
    *   E-invoicing
    *   Business enforcement measures
    *   Perception of slow institutional reforms

### Key Candidate Risk
*   **Anthony Loke (PH/DAP):** Despite a perfect winning record (Lobak 2x, Chennah 3x since 2013), he was **not expected to secure an easy victory** in Chennah.
*   **Opponent:** Contesting against Negeri Sembilan MCA Youth Chief **Siow Kong Choon** (BN).
