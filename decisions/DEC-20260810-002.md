# Context

GovSec TIP has reached v3.0 (August 2026), delivering Threat Visualization, Executive Dashboard, AI Analyst (RAG), Threat Hunting Workbench, Malaysian Compliance features, and the Core Platform MVP (v2.4). The platform has evolved through four major releases from v1.0 (Q1 2026) to v3.0 (August 2026).

The CyberDSA October 2026 milestone (DEC-20260804-001) requires a stable, demonstrable platform for the joint product launch with CSM. The current development pace — adding major features each release — risks destabilising the platform before the launch window.

DAF has determined that the platform has reached sufficient feature maturity for the CyberDSA demonstration. The strategic priority now shifts from feature development to stabilisation, hardening, validation, and handover.

# Decision

**Development Freeze:** Effective immediately, new major feature development on GovSec TIP is frozen unless the feature is critical for the CyberDSA launch.

**Five priorities replace feature development:**

1. **Stabilise and harden the current build** — focus on stability, performance, and security of the v3.0 platform
2. **Close critical technical and security gaps** — triage and resolve known issues that pose launch risk
3. **Validate key end-to-end workflows and integrations** — ensure all demonstration flows work reliably
4. **Complete development handover to Hadri** — compile comprehensive technical/development documentation including full changelog, architecture, completed components, outstanding items, known issues, dependencies, deployment requirements, and immediate development priorities
5. **Prepare platform and demonstration flow for CyberDSA** — define and validate the demo experience

**Hadri tasked** with producing a CyberDSA Product Launch Checklist.

# Rationale

- **Feature maturity reached:** v3.0 delivers Threat Visualization, Executive Dashboard, AI Analyst, Threat Hunting, Malaysian Compliance — sufficient for a credible CyberDSA demonstration
- **Risk of continued development:** each new feature introduces regression risk; the October launch window allows no time for stabilisation if features continue to be added
- **Handover criticality:** Hadri (Lead Architect) receives handover documentation; Hadi (GovSec Product Manager) onboarding is the critical path dependency
- **CyberDSA as firm milestone:** DEC-20260804-001 established CyberDSA October 2026 as the delivery target; this decision operationalises the runway
- **CSM collaboration dependency:** the three collaboration areas identified on Aug 10 (Telemetry, Score Card, CBOM Agent) require a stable GovSec platform to integrate with — not a moving target

# Alternatives Considered

- **Continue feature development alongside hardening:** Rejected — splitting focus risks neither being complete. The development window between now and October is ~8 weeks, insufficient for both new features and full hardening.
- **Partial freeze (freeze only v3.0+ features):** Rejected — the directive explicitly states "minimise any new major feature development unless it is critical for launch." A partial freeze creates ambiguity about what's in scope.
- **Postpone CyberDSA to 2027:** Not discussed — CyberDSA October 2026 is a firm milestone (DEC-20260804-001) with CSM joint launch commitment (COM-20260810-001).

# Assumptions

- GovSec TIP v3.0 is feature-sufficient for a credible CyberDSA demonstration

- **Resource reallocation:** development effort redirects to QA, security testing, documentation, and demo preparation

- Hadi onboarding delay risk — if Hadi's start date slips, the product management dimension of CyberDSA launch is underresourced (RSK-20260810-001)
