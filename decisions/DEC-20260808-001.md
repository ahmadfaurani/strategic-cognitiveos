# Context

DAF requested a full Red Team Division build-out under the Cyber Security Practice at Aras Integrasi. The division needed: (1) a structured document with org chart, roles, capability stack, revenue model, and phased roadmap for management committee presentation, and (2) 5 detailed job descriptions suitable for HR firm submission. This elevates offensive security from a 2-role workstream in the June BU hiring plan to a standalone 5-FTE division.

# Decision

1. **5 FTE structure approved** — Head of Division (RM 12–16K/mo), Senior Red Team Operator (RM 8–12K/mo), Dark Web Intelligence Analyst (RM 7–10K/mo), Detection Engineer (RM 7–10K/mo), Offensive Security Researcher (RM 7–11K/mo)

2. **Division structure document approved** as management committee ready — 16 sections covering org chart, capability stack, revenue model, roadmap, engagement lifecycle, daily operations, OPSEC, governance, onboarding, and infrastructure budget

3. **5 job descriptions approved** as HR-firm ready — standalone documents with position summary, responsibilities (%-allocated), requirements, tools/stack, performance expectations, and compensation

4. **Dark Web Intelligence Analyst prioritised as second hire** (after Head of Division) because dark web monitoring becomes the first revenue stream (standalone subscription from Month 3, before red team matures)

5. **Zero licensing cost stack maintained** — VORON-C2 (open source), Wazuh/ELK (open source), Mr.Holmes (in-house), CyberStrike (open source)

6. **National capability framing adopted** — Malaysian-controlled, sovereign, aligned with Cyber Security Act 2024

# Rationale

- Red Team Division elevates offensive security from workstream to standalone division with clear P&L
- Dark web intelligence provides first revenue stream before red team capability matures
- Detection engineering creates a national detection rule library — a strategic asset that compounds with each engagement
- Offensive research with 20% protected time produces CVEs and thought leadership that positions Aras Integrasi in the market
- Zero licensing cost keeps margins high and eliminates foreign vendor dependency

# Alternatives Considered

- Keep offensive security as 2-role workstream in BU hiring plan (RM1.3M) — rejected: too small for national capability framing
- Use commercial C2 frameworks (Cobalt Strike, Brute Ratel) — rejected: foreign vendor dependency, licensing costs, not sovereign
- Outsource dark web intelligence to third-party provider — rejected: capability should be in-house, feeds red team operations

# Assumptions

- Management committee will approve 5 FTE headcount and RM 30–50K infrastructure budget
- Malaysian talent market can supply qualified candidates for all 5 roles (may require skunkworks pipeline for junior roles)
- Dark web intelligence subscription service can secure 3+ clients within 90 days of analyst hire
- Year 1 revenue target of RM 1.75M is achievable with 4–6 red team engagements + 3 dark web intelligence clients + 2–3 purple team exercises

# Expected Outcome

- Management committee approval within 30 days
- Head of Division hired within 90 days of approval
- First revenue (dark web intelligence subscription) within 90 days of analyst hire
- First red team engagement within 180 days of Head hire
- Year 1 revenue: RM 1.75M, Year 2 revenue: RM 3.8M

# Consequences

- 5 FTE hires required (RM 750K–800K Year 1 payroll)
- RM 30–50K Year 1 infrastructure budget
- VORON-C2 infrastructure must be operationalised
- Skunkworks programme must be maintained as talent pipeline
- Division governance, OPSEC, and quality standards must be enforced

# Risks Accepted

- Talent scarcity for offensive security roles in Malaysia
- Dark web intelligence service may take longer than 90 days to secure first client
- EDR lab licensing may exceed budget (mitigation: vendor partnerships, open-source alternatives)
- Management committee may require phased hiring instead of full team upfront
