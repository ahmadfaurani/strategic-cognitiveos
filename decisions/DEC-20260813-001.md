# Decision

DAF formally adopts the 5-pillar CSM–Aras Joint Operating Model structure as the operating framework for all CSM–Aras collaborative work.

# The 5 Pillars

| Pillar | Scope | Pillar Lead |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| P1 — Cyber Intelligence | GovSec, SiberSUITE, CMERP, LebahNet | Hadri (Technical Coordinator) |
| P2 — Sovereign AI Platform | GPU, models, tokens, APIs, AI agents | DAF (Strategic Owner + Programme Coordinator) |
| P3 — AI Cyber R&D | UPM, Purple Teaming, AI security research | Hadri (Technical Coordinator) |
| P4 — Product & GTM | VoronCitadel, GovSec, ChainSentry, 193-account campaign | DAF (Strategic Owner + Programme Coordinator) |
| P5 — Capability Development | Training, technical enablement, co-development | DAF (Programme Coordinator) |

# Governance Assignments

- **Programme Coordinator (Aras):** DAF — strategic ownership of the Joint Operating Model
- **Technical Coordinator P1 + P3:** Hadri
- **CSM Programme Coordinator:** Not yet requested — deferred by DAF at this time

# Rationale

The structure is already de facto in use (Athena's review mapped everything to it). Formal adoption means:
1. All future CSM–Aras work is categorised by pillar
2. Each pillar has a named lead
3. The cross-cutting governance layer gets built

The gap is governance, not architecture. The pillars have real content; the connective tissue that holds them together needs to be formalised.

# What This Enables

- Master Workstream Register as first Joint Operating Model deliverable (ACT-20260813-012)
- Clear escalation paths per pillar
- Single programme structure to present to CSM
- Basis for monthly executive steering once established
