Secure COO and CMO approval for the people, tooling, and budget required to sustain the VoronDRQ market outreach programme. This plan defines who is communicated to, in what sequence, with what message, and what decision is requested at each step.

## 3. Communications Sequence

The asks are sequenced so that each conversation builds on the previous one. Kenny never hears a proposal that hasn't been pre-validated by the people who'll execute it.

### Step 1: Fuad — Product Review (This Week)

**Channel:** Email + working session
**Message:** "Before I take the GTM plan to Kenny and Azzatullina, I need your validation that the product claims, diagnostic, and demo are ready. Here's the CMO Outreach Package and a set of specific questions."
**Decision requested:** Product sign-off on claims, diagnostic feasibility, demo readiness
**Why first:** Everything downstream depends on product truth. If Fuad says something isn't ready, the plan changes before it reaches the COO.

### Step 2: Shuhada — Sales Coordination (By Aug 15)

**Channel:** Direct conversation (in-person or call)
**Message:** "Kenny asked me to coordinate with you on the Sales engagement approach. I have a 15-account pilot with a defined cadence. Here's how I see it working — what do you need from your side?"
**Decision requested:** Alignment on account ownership model, input on CRM, confirmation she can work with 2 additional account owners
**Why second:** Kenny directed this. When DAF goes to Kenny, he needs to say "Shuhada and I are aligned" — not "I have a proposal for Shuhada."

### Step 3: Hadri — Technical Capacity Check (By Aug 15)

**Channel:** Direct conversation
**Message:** "The GTM plan requires demo delivery and POC engineering. I'm proposing a POC Engineer role to offload you and Fuad. Here's the draft JD — does this match what you need?"
**Decision requested:** Input on POC Engineer JD, assessment of his own demo training feasibility
**Why third:** Hadri's input shapes the JD before Kenny sees it. If Hadri identifies technical requirements DAF missed, the JD is stronger.

### Step 4: Azzatullina — Marketing Co-Design (By Aug 22)

**Channel:** Standalone email (not reply-to-thread) + 30-minute conversation
**Message:** "I have a GTM plan that's been validated by Product and aligned with Sales. The marketing shape is where I need your perspective — not your review of my plan, but your input on how to shape the campaign. Here are two specific questions about financial services CISOs and CROs."
**Decision requested:** Commitment to Marketing Operations resource, acceptance of content development ownership, guidance on campaign messaging and channels
**Why fourth:** She's not being asked to react to a finished plan — she's being asked to co-design the marketing track. By this point, Product and Sales are aligned, so she's not being asked to validate technical substance.

### Step 5: Kenny — COO Approval Package (By Aug 22)

**Channel:** Formal working session (Marketing + Sales + CyberSec Practice)
**Message:** "Here's the GTM programme: strategy validated by Product, Sales aligned, Marketing shaped with Azzatullina. Here's what I need to execute: 4 hires, CRM, budget, Legal reviewer. Here are the JDs, the resource requirements, and the financial estimate. Can you approve?"
**Decision requested:** Approve 4 hires, programme budget, CRM platform, Legal reviewer assignment
**Why last:** Kenny sees a complete package — not a proposal, but a plan that's been validated across the team. His decision is about resource allocation, not strategy. Every question he might ask has already been answered by someone on the team.

## 7. Internal Communications Principles

1. **No surprises.** Every person who will be affected by a decision is consulted before the decision is made — not after.
2. **Pre-alignment before approval.** Kenny never sees a proposal that hasn't been validated by the people who'll execute it.
3. **Co-design, not review.** Azzatullina is asked to shape the marketing track, not review DAF's version of it.
4. **Bounded asks.** Every conversation has a specific decision requested — not an open-ended "what do you think?"
5. **Evidence-backed.** Every ask comes with data, documents, and a clear business case.
6. **Sequenced, not simultaneous.** Each conversation builds on the previous one. The order matters.
