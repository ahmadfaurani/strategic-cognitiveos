# Summary

The **PERJASA × Aras Government Sector AI Systems Co-Design Lab** is a strategic collaboration between Persatuan Juruanalisa Sistem Sektor Awam (PERJASA) and Aras Integrasi, coordinated through Jabatan Digital Negara (JDN), to build AI capability within the public sector through a structured co-design and prototyping programme. Unlike conventional technical training, this is a build-oriented platform where real government-sector problems are translated into valuable, evaluable AI use cases with clear execution pathways.

The programme is a **5-stage progressive cohort model**: (1) AI Awareness, (2) Design Thinking, (3) AI Sandbox — the 2-day strategic workshop (proposed Sept 2–3, 2026), (4) Executive Demo Day, (5) Alumni Network. The workshop itself targets **24–32 participants** (per Workshop Agenda document; email stated 20–24 — agenda is authoritative) organised into four multidisciplinary teams (AI Infrastructure, AI Development, AI Cybersecurity, AI Productivity), each producing at least one prototype, workflow, or technical architecture. The programme uses a **50/30/10 illustrative cohort design** (50 enter Awareness, 30 advance to Design Thinking, 10 enter Sandbox — final numbers to be approved during pilot design).

Beyond the workshop, the initiative builds a **PERJASA Government AI Cohort Alumni Network** — a sustained practitioner network of subject matter experts supporting government AI planning and implementation.

# Strategic Significance

This initiative is a **flagship sovereign AI workstream** under Workstream A (Sovereign AI & Government AI Adoption):

1. **Government AI adoption engine** — Translates national AI adoption intent into practical, demonstrable outcomes through real use cases, prototypes, and pilot pathways
2. **Institutional capability building** — Creates a self-sustaining expert network within PERJASA that grows cohort-over-cohort, reducing dependency on external providers
3. **JDN strategic alignment** — Positions JDN as the coordinating catalyst for government-wide AI adoption, strengthening the Aras–JDN institutional relationship
4. **GPU compute enablement** — Provides governed access to GPU compute infrastructure, creating a recurring engagement/usage model
5. **Alumni network as institutional asset** — The cohort alumni model creates reusable technical assets (reference architectures, implementation playbooks, control frameworks, validation methodologies) and a mentor pipeline for future cohorts
6. **Distinct from CSM track** — While INIT-20260804-004 (CSM Co-Design Lab) targets cybersecurity-specific AI use cases with MyCERT, this PERJASA/JDN track targets broader government-sector AI adoption across multiple domains

# Workshop Structure

## 4-Stage Delivery Model

| Stage | Day | Purpose | Decision Point |
|-------|-----|---------|---------------|
| **Discover** | Day 1 | Define the operational problem, users, evidence and constraints | Is the problem suitable and important enough for AI intervention? |
| **Design** | Day 1 | Select the use case and design the workflow, architecture and controls | Is the solution feasible, governed and ready to build? |
| **Build** | Day 2 | Develop the minimum demonstrator, workflow or architecture prototype | Does the proposed solution work against the defined scenario? |
| **Validate** | Day 2 | Test value, usability, security, governance and pilot readiness | Proceed, refine, research or hold? |

## Day 1 — Strategic Alignment, Discovery and Design (08:30–17:30)
Theme: From government problems to validated AI solution designs

- 08:30–09:00: Registration and technical readiness (verify workspace, GPU-token, team access)
- 09:00–09:15: Opening and mandate (PERJASA establishes public-sector context, partnership model, expected decisions)
- 09:15–09:45: Strategic framing (government AI adoption across supply, platform, demand, practitioners, security, trust)
- 09:45–10:15: Workshop method and evaluation (Discover→Design→Build→Validate, deliverables, review gates, scoring)
- 10:15–10:30: Break
- 10:30–11:00: Agency and practitioner perspectives (surface agency priorities, practitioner capabilities, constraints)
- 11:00–11:30: Government AI opportunity landscape (review opportunities across 4 focus areas, identify shared patterns)
- 11:30–12:30: Problem discovery (define users, current process, pain points, evidence, root causes, constraints, outcomes)
- 12:30–13:00: **Gate 1: Problem validation** (facilitators challenge problem ownership, evidence, scope, urgency, AI suitability)
- 13:00–14:00: Lunch
- 14:00–14:30: Use-case prioritisation briefing (scoring against value, urgency, feasibility, data, security, scalability, time to impact)
- 14:30–15:15: Use-case assessment and selection (score candidates; select 1 primary + 1 reserve per team)
- 15:15–15:30: Break
- 15:30–16:15: Solution co-design (target users, AI-assisted workflow, human decisions, operating boundaries, success measures)
- 16:15–17:00: Architecture, data, security and governance clinics (target architecture, data flow, deployment, controls, risks, approvals)
- 17:00–17:25: **Gate 2: Design review** (each team presents problem, solution design, value, architecture, risks → build approval or refinement)
- 17:25–17:30: Day 1 close (confirm Day 2 build scope, owners, dependencies)

## Day 2 — Prototyping, Validation and Pilot Selection (08:30–18:00)
Theme: From validated design to a pilot-ready government AI initiative

- 08:30–09:00: Design confirmation and build briefing (prototype objective, user journey, success conditions, mentors, technical boundaries)
- 09:00–10:30: Prototype sprint — Build 1 (core application, workflow, agent, control demonstrator, architecture proof)
- 10:30–10:45: Break
- 10:45–11:30: Technical mentor clinics (model choice, integration, compute, performance, deployment, implementation assumptions)
- 11:30–12:45: Prototype sprint — Build 2 (incorporate mentor guidance, complete end-to-end demonstration scenario)
- 12:45–13:45: Lunch
- 13:45–14:30: Security and responsible-AI review (threat modelling, privacy, misuse review, access-control validation, human-oversight assessment)
- 14:30–15:15: Operational and user validation (test against original workflow, user expectations, success indicators)
- 15:15–15:30: Break
- 15:30–16:15: Pilot design and implementation planning (scope, owner, users, data, environment, dependencies, timeline, resources)
- 16:15–16:45: Final submission preparation (demonstration, documentation, assurance findings, 90-day action plan)
- 16:45–17:35: **Gate 3: Final demonstrations** (4 teams present: 12 min each — 7 min problem/prototype/implementation, 3 min security/governance/validation, 2 min panel questions)
- 17:35–17:50: Portfolio decision (evaluation panel classifies: Proceed, Refine, Research, Hold)
- 17:50–18:00: Commitments and closing (confirm owners, actions, reporting dates, practitioner-community continuation)

## Decision Gates (3)
1. **Gate 1 — Problem validation** (Day 1, 12:30): Is the problem suitable and important enough for AI intervention?
2. **Gate 2 — Design review** (Day 1, 17:00): Is the solution feasible, governed and ready to build?
3. **Gate 3 — Final demonstrations** (Day 2, 16:45): Does the proposed solution work? Proceed, refine, research or hold?

## Evaluation Framework

100-point scoring across 8 weighted dimensions. **Minimum 70 to proceed.** Must have identified agency owner and no unresolved critical security/governance issues.

| Dimension | Weight | Panel Test |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| Public-sector and operational value | 20 | Will the initiative materially improve an important government outcome? |
| Problem clarity and user relevance | 10 | Is the problem evidence-based, owned and meaningful to users? |
| Technical feasibility | 15 | Can the proposed architecture and solution be implemented reliably? |
| Data readiness | 10 | Are appropriate data sources, permissions and handling controls available? |
| Cybersecurity and privacy | 15 | Are threats, sensitive data and access risks understood and controlled? |
| Responsible AI and human oversight | 10 | Are accountability, review and operating boundaries explicit? |
| Scalability and government reuse | 10 | Can the approach be extended or reused across agencies? |
| Pilot readiness and ownership | 10 | Is there a committed owner, feasible scope and executable plan? |

## Decision Categories (4)

| Decision | Meaning | Next Action |
|----------|---------|------------|
| **Proceed to Pilot** | Strong value, feasibility and control maturity | Approve pilot charter and begin mobilisation |
| **Refine Before Pilot** | Promising but defined technical/data/governance gaps | Close remediation within 30 days, return for approval |
| **Continue as Research** | Strategic value exists, operational readiness insufficient | Assign research owner, question and review date |
| **Hold** | Insufficient value, feasibility, evidence or ownership | Record findings and discontinue current effort |

## Operating Controls (6)

1. **Data**: Public, synthetic or anonymised data is the default. Sensitive data requires explicit approval, purpose limitation and handling controls.
2. **Access**: GPU, model and workspace access is named, time-bound, monitored and limited to workshop purposes.
3. **Security**: Teams must not introduce unauthorised tools, credentials, datasets or external integrations into the workshop environment.
4. **Human authority**: AI outputs remain advisory unless an approved process explicitly authorises automation. Accountable human review must be defined.
5. **Documentation**: Architecture, data, decisions, risks and validation evidence must be recorded in the common workspace.
6. **Intellectual contribution**: Reusable patterns should be documented without disclosing protected agency information.

# 5-Stage Cohort Programme Model

The 2-day workshop is **Stage 3 (AI Sandbox)** within a broader progressive cohort programme:

| Stage | Purpose | Participant Output |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| **1. AI Awareness** | Create common understanding of AI opportunities, limitations, governance and public-interest considerations | Opportunity areas and readiness self-assessment |
| **2. Design Thinking** | Convert organisational challenges into clearly scoped, measurable, data-aware problem statements | Problem statement, baseline KPI, benefit owner, initial data map |
| **3. AI Sandbox** (the 2-day workshop) | Develop selected concepts into working evidence under technical and governance guidance | Prototype, validation results, production-readiness recommendation |
| **4. Executive Demo Day** | Place senior decision-makers in the room to assess evidence, risks, value and next steps | Decision record, implementation pathway, accountable follow-up |
| **5. Alumni Network** | Retain Stage 2 and Stage 3 participants as a professional community and future pipeline | Peer learning, referrals, mentoring, re-entry, continuing development |

**Illustrative cohort design (50/30/10 model):** ~50 participants enter AI Awareness, ~30 advance to Design Thinking, ~10 enter the AI Sandbox. Final numbers to be approved during pilot design. These are planning assumptions, not guaranteed targets.

## 6 Institutional Value Pillars

1. **National relevance** — Position PERJASA as practical contributor to responsible AI adoption, not only organiser of awareness events
2. **Member value** — Applied skills, executive exposure, peer learning, recognition, pathways into meaningful projects
3. **Community ownership** — Sustained practitioner and alumni network active between cohorts and events
4. **Evidence and influence** — Anonymised case evidence informing professional standards, policy dialogue, national capability
5. **Ecosystem convening** — Government, GLCs, academia, industry around validated problems under transparent governance
6. **Programme sustainability** — Sponsorship, training, partnership and advanced-cohort models without compromising neutrality

## 5 Differentiators

1. **Participant-owned problems** — Use cases originate inside participating organisations
2. **Progressive selection** — Each stage increases commitment and evidence; sandbox participation is earned
3. **Executive visibility** — Demo Day brings senior leaders in at the right moment — after evidence, before production commitments
4. **Bridge beyond the prototype** — Engineers, business analysts and change practitioners translate demos into architecture, governance, cost and adoption decisions
5. **Community after the cohort** — PERJASA-led alumni network protects value for those not selected for sandbox

## Operating Model (4 Parties)

| Party | Primary Role | Non-Negotiable Accountability |
|-------|-------------|------------------------------|
| **PERJASA** | Programme governance, brand stewardship, participant standards, stakeholder engagement, alumni ownership, outcome reporting | Protect member interests, neutrality, transparency, programme integrity |
| **ARAS** | Technical delivery, business analysis, production-readiness support, change enablement, implementation planning | Provide named capacity, reusable methods, clear handoffs, evidence-based recommendations |
| **Participating organisation** | Own the problem, data, executive sponsorship, benefit case, internal approvals | Nominate accountable business, data and technology owners |
| **Ambassadors and facilitators** | Inspire participation, facilitate engagement, strengthen senior-leader and alumni connections | Operate under role charter, continuity plan, PERJASA-owned relationship model |

**Operating principle:** No single person, vendor or partner should own the programme's relationships, knowledge or credibility. Account intelligence, decisions and commitments must be documented and institutionally held.

## 90-Day Pilot Mobilisation Path (Programme-Level)

| Window | Objective | Exit Condition |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Days 1–30 | Design and govern: appoint sponsor and Programme Director; confirm cohort size; define selection and stage gates; approve RACI; validate partner roles; prepare KPI/data/IP/security/production-readiness templates | Approved charter, delivery cell, methods, safeguards and participant proposition |
| Days 31–60 | Recruit and prepare: mobilise members and stakeholders; select organisations and use cases; secure executive sponsors and data owners; baseline KPIs; introduce engineers and analysts; activate alumni design | Every Sandbox candidate passes problem, sponsor, data, architecture and measurement gates |

## Pilot Success Threshold (7 Criteria)

1. Verified actuals for every programme stage and documented selection decisions
2. Majority of Sandbox projects passing agreed production-readiness review
3. Confirmed executive engagement for every Demo Day project
4. 2–3 credible reference candidates with measurable benefit potential
5. Recorded implementation decisions, including reasons for non-proceeding projects
6. Measured delivery capacity, partner performance, participant satisfaction and cohort economics
7. Functioning alumni model with consent, ownership and engagement measures

## Stakeholder Mobilisation (6 Asks)

1. **Nominate** — Put forward a high-potential member, practitioner, team or organisation-owned use case
2. **Sponsor** — Support participant places, technical resources, scholarships, Demo Day or reference-case development

5. **Partner** — Join the ecosystem under transparent roles, non-exclusivity and PERJASA governance
6. **Champion** — Serve as executive sponsor helping a viable use case move from evidence to organisational decision
