Programme overview
A Cohort Designed to Convert Learning into Action
The programme is designed as a progressive stage-gate. Participants begin with a broad understanding of AI, move into organisation-owned problem definition, and then compete for a place in an applied sandbox. The strongest projects are presented to senior leaders with a documented pathway toward implementation.

Why the model is different
Five Differentiators That Make the Cohort Pitchable
1. Participant-owned problems. Use cases originate inside participating organisations, creating stronger relevance and internal ownership.
2. Progressive selection. Each stage increases commitment and evidence. Sandbox participation is earned through problem quality, sponsorship, data readiness and expected value.
3. Executive visibility. Demo Day brings senior leaders into the decision at the right moment—after evidence exists but before production commitments are made.
4. A bridge beyond the prototype. Engineers, business analysts and change practitioners help translate demonstrations into architecture, governance, cost and adoption decisions.
5. Community after the cohort. Participants remain connected through a PERJASA-led alumni network, protecting the value of those not selected for the final sandbox.

Pilot Success Threshold
PERJASA should consider scaling only after the pilot produces a complete evidence pack:
Verified actuals for every programme stage and documented selection decisions.
A majority of Sandbox projects passing the agreed production-readiness review.
Confirmed executive engagement for every Demo Day project.
Two or three credible reference candidates with measurable benefit potential.
Recorded implementation decisions, including reasons for projects that do not proceed.
Measured delivery capacity, partner performance, participant satisfaction and cohort economics.
A functioning alumni model with consent, ownership and engagement measures.

PERJASA field guide
Likely Questions and Firm Responses
Why PERJASA?
PERJASA brings the professional legitimacy, member network, convening power and community stewardship needed to keep the programme focused on capability and public value.
Why ARAS?
ARAS is proposed as the delivery enabler for engineering, business analysis, production readiness and change support. Its role remains accountable to the programme charter and PERJASA governance.
Will every participant build an MVP?
No. The proposed model uses progressive selection. All participants receive learning value; selected use cases advance based on problem quality, sponsorship, data readiness and expected benefit.
What happens to participants who do not reach the Sandbox?
They should enter the PERJASA-led alumni network, access peer learning and mentoring, and remain eligible for future cohorts or stronger use-case submissions.
How do we protect organisational data and IP?
No sandbox work should begin until data access, confidentiality, intellectual property, publication rights and security responsibilities are documented.
How will success be measured?
Through stage progression, problem quality, executive engagement, production readiness, implementation decisions, measurable outcomes, alumni activity and cohort economics—not attendance alone.
Are the cohort numbers and targets guaranteed?
No. The 50/30/10 model and proposed KPI thresholds are planning assumptions for pilot design. Actual targets must be approved and then reported against verified results.
What is the immediate commitment?
One governed 90-day pilot: approve the charter, nominate participants and use cases, mobilise partners, run the cohort and decide on scale using evidence.
