## 1. Executive Assessment

Ir. Dr. Megat Zuhairy bin Megat Tajuddin is a Tier-1 national cybersecurity stakeholder whose relevance extends substantially beyond his title as Chief Executive of NACSA.

His position sits at the intersection of:
- National cybersecurity policy
- Statutory cyber regulation
- National Critical Information Infrastructure (NCII) protection
- Cyber incident and crisis preparedness
- Cybersecurity-service-provider regulation
- Artificial intelligence security
- Cryptography and post-quantum preparedness
- Public-private cybersecurity collaboration
- Malaysia's wider cyber-sovereignty agenda

NACSA is Malaysia's national lead agency for cybersecurity, operating under the National Security Council within the Prime Minister's Department. The Cyber Security Act 2024 gives the NACSA Chief Executive formal duties and powers covering national cybersecurity, NCII and regulated cybersecurity services.

**Key strategic finding:** Dr. Megat's public policy narrative increasingly centres on sovereign resilience, operational capability, risk-based security, local capacity, emerging technology security and government-industry collaboration. This creates substantial conceptual alignment with a Malaysian sovereign-cybersecurity capability proposition — provided it is framed as an operational national capability contribution, not a conventional vendor-product launch.

## 3. Institutional Authority

The Cyber Security Act 2024 established a strengthened statutory cybersecurity regime encompassing:
- National Cyber Security Committee
- Powers and responsibilities of the NACSA Chief Executive
- NCII sector leads and entities
- Cybersecurity incident and threat management
- Licensing of specified cybersecurity service providers

NACSA publishes Chief Executive Directives covering:
1. Cybersecurity incident notification
2. Cybersecurity-service-provider licensing
3. NCII entity designation
4. National cybersecurity baseline assessment
5. Cybersecurity risk assessment
6. Licensing transition arrangements
7. NCII codes of practice
8. NCII cybersecurity audits
9. Post-quantum cryptography migration information
10. Application of the National Cyber Crisis Management Plan

**Strategic interpretation:** The NACSA Chief Executive combines Policy Authority + Regulatory Authority + National-Security Coordination + Technology-Strategy Influence. For any cybersecurity proposition claiming national-grade, critical-infrastructure, sovereign, or government-scale relevance, NACSA is an essential institutional reference point.

## 4. Observable Strategic Doctrine

### Theme 1 — From Cyber Protection to Cyber Resilience
MCSS 2025–2030 represents a deliberate shift from reactive protection to proactive national resilience. Dr. Megat publicly characterises MCSS as a national strategy built around government, industry and society as partners. Credible solutions will be judged by contribution to operational resilience, not checklist compliance.

### Theme 2 — Cyber Sovereignty
His official CyberDSA 2026 statement describes MCSS 2025–2030 as setting Malaysia's direction towards "sovereign cyber security resilience." Sovereignty is already part of NACSA's national cybersecurity vocabulary. Sovereign cybersecurity should be presented as an implementation contribution to an existing national direction, not introduced as an unfamiliar commercial concept.

### Theme 3 — AI Security
NACSA introduced the Artificial Intelligence Systems Cyber Security Framework (AISCF). Dr. Megat has publicly discussed autonomous AI agent security risks with controls including: identifiable non-human identities, accountable human ownership, minimum privileges, time-limited access, continuous monitoring, kill-switch capability, explicit human approval for high-impact actions. His approach is risk-based + identity-centric + auditable + human-governed + operationally enforceable. Generic "AI-powered cybersecurity" claims are insufficient.

### Theme 4 — Cryptography and Digital Sovereignty
Updated MyKriptografi National Cryptography Policy (Cabinet-approved November 2025) explicitly supports Malaysia's digital sovereignty, self-reliant cryptographic ecosystem and quantum-era threat readiness. NACSA-Huawei cooperation agreement (July 2026) includes cybersecurity, cryptology, AI security, research, capacity development, governance, technology development and digital sovereignty.

### Theme 5 — Public-Private Collaboration
NACSA explicitly describes cybersecurity as shared responsibility. Willing to work with external technology organisations where there is identified national capability, research, training, policy or technology-development purpose. Engagement requirement is mission alignment, not conventional vendor selling.

## 7. Recommended Engagement Architecture

**Stage 1 — Establish Credibility:** Lead with national requirement → capability gap → Malaysian capability → operational evidence. Do NOT lead with company history or product features.

**Stage 2 — Demonstrate Alignment:** Map proposition directly against MCSS 2025–2030, Cyber Security Act 2024, NCII resilience, AISCF, national cybersecurity baseline, operational resilience, sovereign technology, cybersecurity talent/capability development.

**Stage 3 — Demonstrate the Capability:** Answer five questions:
1. What national cybersecurity problem does this solve?
2. What part is genuinely Malaysian-controlled or Malaysian-developed?
3. What does the technology do today — not eventually?
4. How does it integrate with existing institutions and infrastructure?
5. How can it progress from demonstration → validation → adoption?

**Stage 4 — Seek Strategic Alignment, Not Immediate Endorsement:** Prefer: Awareness → Strategic Alignment → Technical Familiarisation → Observation → Potential Validation. NOT: Introduction → Endorsement. This protects institutional credibility while allowing confidence to develop.

## 10. Key Risks

1. **Premature use of NACSA association** — must not present as endorsement/certification/approval/sponsorship/adoption unless formally authorised
2. **Excessive productisation** — feature-by-feature presentation diminishes strategic narrative; lead with national capability architecture
3. **Sovereignty without evidence** — "sovereign" is substantive policy concept; must demonstrate data residency, infrastructure control, IP ownership, operational control, supply-chain dependencies, local engineering, governance, security assurance, continuity
4. **AI claims without security governance** — given AISCF, uncontrolled AI autonomy undermines credibility; must demonstrate AI governance, human authority, auditability, identity management, least privilege, secure lifecycle controls
