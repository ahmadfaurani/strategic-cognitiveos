# R.I.S.I.K Framework

**Reality · Issue · Sentiment · Influence · Kontra Narrative**

An analytical and operational framework for modern psychological operations, strategic influence, and information warfare. Converts field intelligence, media data, public discourse, and audience behaviour into structured, measurable, and governed communication interventions.

---

## 1. Reality — Operational Reality

Establish the actual situation before any narrative, communication strategy, or intervention is developed.

### Key Questions
- What is actually happening?
- Who is involved and affected?
- What is the gap between fact, perception, and allegation?
- Which information has been verified?
- Which information remains uncertain, incomplete, or speculative?

### Outputs
- Baseline situational picture
- Verified fact set
- Actor and stakeholder map
- Information-gap register
- Source reliability assessment
- Confidence assessment

---

## 2. Issue — Core Issue

Identify the issues that carry political, social, emotional, operational, or reputational significance for the target audience.

### Issue Assessment Dimensions

| Dimension | Assessment Focus |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Relevance | The extent to which the issue affects the target audience |
| Urgency | The speed at which the issue is developing |
| Magnitude | The size of the affected population or stakeholder group |
| Credibility | The extent to which the claim or issue is believed |
| Exploitation Potential | The likelihood of the issue being manipulated by other actors |
| Escalation Risk | The potential to trigger conflict, instability, or crisis |
| Persistence | The expected duration of the issue |
| Strategic Impact | The possible effect on institutional objectives or public confidence |

### Outputs
- Issue register
- Priority issue ranking
- Issue lifecycle assessment
- Trigger indicators
- Escalation indicators
- Strategic impact assessment

---

## 3. Sentiment — Audience Sentiment

Assess not only whether sentiment is positive, negative, or neutral, but also the underlying emotions that influence perception, decision-making, and behaviour.

### Emotional Categories
- Anger
- Fear
- Frustration
- Distrust
- Hope
- Pride
- Apathy
- Anxiety
- Solidarity
- Resentment

### Sentiment Segmentation Dimensions
- Demographics
- Geography
- Community
- Political affiliation or orientation
- Media platform
- Level of engagement
- Stakeholder category
- Voter or audience segment
- Issue exposure
- Behavioural response

### Outputs
- Sentiment heatmap
- Emotional-driver analysis
- Audience segmentation
- Sentiment-shift detection
- Behavioural-risk indicators
- Escalation thresholds

---

## 4. Influence — Influence Mapping

Identify the individuals, organisations, platforms, networks, and channels that shape public understanding, perception, and behaviour.

### Influence Sources
- Community leaders
- Political actors
- Activists
- Journalists
- Media organisations
- WhatsApp or Telegram group administrators
- Micro-influencers
- Religious institutions
- Professional associations
- Civil-society groups
- Anonymous accounts
- Coordinated networks
- Automated or bot-driven amplification systems

### Influence Assessment Factors

| Factor | Assessment Focus |
|--------|-----------------|
| Reach | The number of people or communities that can be reached |
| Resonance | The extent to which the message is accepted or believed |
| Authority | Formal, professional, political, or social standing |
| Network Position | The actor's location within the communication network |
| Mobilisation Capacity | The ability to trigger action or coordinated behaviour |
| Narrative Alignment | Alignment with a specific issue, position, or narrative |
| Credibility | The level of trust placed in the actor |
| Amplification Capacity | The ability to increase message visibility and distribution |
| Credibility Risk | Exposure to reputational, ethical, or manipulation risks |

### Outputs
- Influence network map
- Key influencer registry
- Amplification pathway
- Narrative distribution map
- Vulnerable audience clusters
- Engagement priority list
- Trusted-messenger matrix

---

## 5. Kontra Narrative — Counter-Narrative

A counter-narrative should not merely deny or reject a claim. It should replace harmful, misleading, or destabilising interpretations with explanations that are more credible, relevant, evidence-based, and meaningful to the intended audience.

### Principles of Effective Counter-Narrative
- Address the audience's actual concerns
- Acknowledge realities that cannot reasonably be denied
- Use language that is understandable and culturally appropriate
- Be delivered through credible and trusted messengers
- Provide both explanation and practical action
- Avoid unnecessarily repeating or amplifying hostile narratives
- Be proportionate to the actual level of threat
- Be measured through changes in perception, confidence, and behaviour
- Remain grounded in verified facts
- Comply with legal, ethical, and institutional governance requirements

### Types of Intervention

| Intervention | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Pre-bunking | Building audience resilience before a harmful narrative spreads |
| Debunking | Correcting false or misleading information |
| Reframing | Changing the interpretive frame surrounding an issue |
| Positive replacement | Replacing a harmful narrative with a more credible alternative |
| Evidence amplification | Increasing the visibility of verified evidence |
| Trusted-messenger intervention | Using credible individuals or institutions to deliver the message |
| Contextual clarification | Providing missing facts or context |
| Strategic silence | Avoiding unnecessary amplification of low-impact narratives |
| Behavioural intervention | Directing audiences towards a constructive action or response |

### Outputs
- Narrative intervention plan
- Counter-narrative product (content, messaging, briefings)
- Messenger deployment schedule
- Channel activation plan
- Measurement framework with defined KPIs

## Three-Layer Operating Model

R.I.S.I.K operates as a structured management of perception, confidence, understanding, and behaviour through intelligence-led communication. It is not propaganda.

### Layer 1 — Intelligence

Establishes situational awareness and identifies emerging threats, issues, actors, and narratives.

- Media and digital-space monitoring
- Field intelligence
- Open-source intelligence
- Issue analysis
- Actor analysis
- Audience analysis
- Sentiment intelligence
- Narrative detection
- Influence-network analysis
- Disinformation and manipulation detection

### Layer 2 — Decision

Converts intelligence into governed strategic and operational decisions.

- Risk assessment
- Issue prioritisation
- Audience identification
- Response selection
- Channel selection
- Messenger selection
- Narrative approval
- Escalation-threshold setting
- Legal and ethical review
- Command-level authorisation

### Layer 3 — Engagement

Executes the approved intervention through appropriate channels, messengers, and engagement mechanisms.

- Strategic content
- Leadership briefings
- Community engagement
- Media response
- Stakeholder activation
- Public communication
- Counter-disinformation intervention
- Trusted-messenger mobilisation
- Performance measurement
- Continuous adaptation

## Operating Principles

- **Truth-grounded** — based on verified and defensible facts
- **Evidence-led** — supported by reliable sources and analytical confidence
- **Audience-specific** — tailored to the needs and characteristics of each audience segment
- **Proportionate** — calibrated to the actual severity of the issue or threat
- **Measurable** — supported by clear indicators and defined outcomes
- **Adaptive** — continuously updated as conditions and sentiment change
- **Legally governed** — compliant with applicable laws and institutional controls
- **Ethically controlled** — subject to clear ethical boundaries and human oversight
- **Non-deceptive** — excluding fabrication, covert impersonation, and unlawful manipulation
- **Human-authorised** — ensuring that significant interventions remain under accountable human authority
- **Secure by design** — protecting sensitive data, sources, methods, and operational decisions

## Strategic Positioning

R.I.S.I.K is a structured framework for establishing reality, identifying the central issue, understanding audience sentiment, mapping influence networks, and executing lawful, evidence-based, and measurable counter-narrative interventions.

### Application Domains

- Strategic communication operations
- Psychological operations
- Information warfare analysis
- Political intelligence operations
- Election operations centres
- Government crisis communication
- Counter-disinformation operations
- Stakeholder influence mapping
- Cognitive security operations
- National security communication
- Public sentiment and policy engagement
- Media and information warfare studies
- Strategic decision-support systems
